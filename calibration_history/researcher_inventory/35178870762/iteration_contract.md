# APA Researcher Inventory Agent Contract V63

Status: ACTIVE SUCCESSOR FOR CALIBRATION

Predecessor: `RI-CONTRACT-V62`

Authority: Leah's standing Researcher Inventory calibration instruction. This successor is justified by preserved paired-archetype V62 failure demonstrating a generalizable durable-instruction defect.

## Mission

Produce only a literal lightweight Researcher Inventory candidate from the supplied source. Reconstruct the source's represented research-coordinate structure at the approved lightweight grain, preserve source-native lexical and epistemic posture, and reconstruct the smallest materially complete compounds from the frozen primitive inventory.

Do not interpret psychological meaning, score APA, infer protected threads, draw conclusions, promote records, mint APA IDs, or admit anything to sovereign/production fabric.

The worker never receives approved archetypes, expected counts, gold rows, evaluator findings, prior scored outputs, sealed holdout source during calibration, or holdout outputs.

## 1. V63 controlling model: scene, role, binding, then class

Read the complete source before extracting primitives. First reconstruct a neutral source-order ledger of:

1. represented people/groups and coreference;
2. distinct physical scenes and participant/object positions;
3. distinct temporal/narrative episodes or periods;
4. stable concrete or abstract referents participating in those scenes/bindings;
5. source-present characterizations, states, classifications, corrections, comparisons, and materially qualifying manners;
6. material lexical relations, including embodied/performance actions when they are represented in the source;
7. material orientation relations;
8. materially distinct cross-coordinate bindings.

Only after this ledger is complete, project roles into PLACE, TIME, PERSON, OBJECT, LABEL, VERB, and LOCATOR.

**V63 correction:** primitive identity is determined by a source-supported role or binding slot at lightweight research grain. It is not determined by grammatical category, token census, nominalization, or the V62 requirement that a projection remain independently useful after an overlapping projection is removed. A coordinate may be implicit in wording yet required by a source-distinguished scene or binding. Conversely, explicit wording does not force a primitive when it is merely a wrapper, duplicate decomposition, or grammatical support for a role already represented.

## 2. Primitive admission test

Admit a primitive when all applicable conditions hold:

1. **SOURCE ANCHOR:** exact source wording/cue establishes the role, scene, episode, relation, quality, orientation, or the occurrence whose unnamed PLACE/TIME coordinate is being represented.
2. **RESEARCH SLOT:** the candidate fills a stable lightweight research slot: actor, thing/content, quality/state, lexical relation, occurrence position, episode/period, or orientation.
3. **DISTINCTION:** collapsing it with another same-class coordinate would erase a source-present distinction in scene, episode, participant/object identity, relation, quality/state, or orientation.
4. **BINDING VALUE:** it participates in, anchors, qualifies, or makes navigable at least one represented scene/binding, or is itself a stable source-distinguished coordinate worth revisiting.
5. **NO DUPLICATE WRAPPER:** it is not only a pronoun duplicate, discourse wrapper, clause/event nominalization, support predicate, alternate grain, or alternate decomposition whose research work is already carried by retained coordinates.
6. **LITERAL GRAIN:** use the shortest complete source-native wording that preserves identity, polarity, modality, uncertainty, question, correction, comparison, direction, manner, or fixed expression.

Do not add a primitive merely because a word or phrase parses into a class. Do not remove a primitive merely because it is low-salience, unnamed, implicit, performative, nested, reported, figurative, or overlaps another class.

## 3. Scene/frame completeness before excess pruning

V63 requires two passes in this order.

### 3.1 Completeness pass

For each source-distinguished scene or episode, account for materially represented participants, acted-on/referred content, lexical relations, qualities/states, orientation, physical occurrence position, and temporal frame. If the source clearly distinguishes a physical position or temporal frame but leaves it unnamed, a neutral unnamed PLACE/TIME coordinate is allowed and may be required.

Distinct interpersonal, remembered, reported, prospective, recurring, reflective, figurative, and present-telling scenes/episodes are not erased merely because their location/time is unnamed.

A present-telling PLACE or TIME is retained when the telling itself becomes a represented scene through current deictics, bodily/performance actions, explicit current reflection, or contrast with other narrated scenes. Do not infer it from narration alone.

### 3.2 Excess pass

After completeness, remove token-level or parse-level duplicates: repeated aliases, ordinary grammatical objects that lack stable referent identity, descriptive fragments with no quality/state role, auxiliary/support predicates with no material relation, temporal tokens that do not form a frame, prepositions with no orientation role, and alternate projections that merely restate an already retained research slot.

The excess pass may not delete a coordinate needed to preserve a distinct scene/binding slot.

## 4. Cross-class projection

Cross-class overlap is legal when one source span legitimately fills different research slots. Resolve overlap by asking **what job each projection performs inside the reconstructed scene/binding**, not whether one projection could survive in isolation.

Examples of permissible abstract distinctions (not expected answers):

- a physical scene coordinate and a locator that expresses orientation within it;
- a quality/state and a lexical relation when the source materially represents both characterization and relation;
- a stable referent and a quality applied to it;
- a temporal frame and a temporal/orienting locator.

Reject overlap that is only grammatical recasting or nominalization.

## 5. PLACE

PLACE records physical occurrence positions and settings at the source's scene grain.

Retain named settings and source-supported unnamed positions when a distinct interaction, participant position, object whereabouts, destination/offscreen position, or embodied scene is represented but the place name is omitted. A larger setting and a contained position may both survive when the source distinguishes both as research positions.

Do not turn every physical noun, surface, component, path, or object into PLACE. The test is whether the source distinguishes a physical occurrence position, not whether the wording names a physical thing.

For unnamed PLACE, `source_wording` may be null; `source_cue` must be exact source text that establishes the position.

## 6. TIME

TIME records source-distinguished episodes, periods, phases, and temporal frames that organize represented relations.

Retain named periods and source-supported unnamed frames for distinct attempts, interactions, waits, transitions, remembered/reported events, recurring periods, intended/prospective periods, reflective episodes, and other scene changes. Several relations may share one TIME; do not make one TIME per verb or clause.

Temporal words or durations are not automatically TIME. Conversely, an episode need not have an explicit temporal noun to qualify when source structure clearly distinguishes the frame.

## 7. PERSON

Retain the speaker (`B`) and every source-distinguished human/social actor or stable social group participating in represented material, including indirect, possessive, relational, offscreen, remembered, reported, quoted, or prospective actors.

Resolve aliases/coreference. Exclude generic/rhetorical addressees or classification words that do not denote a represented actor.

## 8. OBJECT

OBJECT records stable concrete things and stable abstract/content referents used as research coordinates in represented bindings.

Retain concrete referents when they materially participate, are located, acted on, described, compared, or otherwise given stable identity. Retain abstract choices, relations, decisions, internal referents, figurative referents, categories/sets, amounts, results, services, topics, or reported content when they function as stable arguments/content in a represented binding.

Do not require explicit nominal reification when the source clearly represents a stable content/choice/relation slot. But reject discourse wrappers, whole-clause/event wrappers, generic summary nouns, and nominalizations whose only job is to point back to a binding already represented by its constituents.

## 9. LABEL

LABEL records source-present qualities, states, evaluations, comparisons, classifications, corrections/rejections, polarity/status, and materially qualifying manners when the characterization itself matters to a represented coordinate or relation.

Use the shortest complete source-native phrase. Preserve question, negation, uncertainty, intensity, correction, comparison, and idiomatic wording.

Do not census every adjective/adverb. Retain a quality/manner when removing it would erase a source-present characterization of a participant, object, scene, relation, or state at lightweight research grain. Collapse redundant restatements of the same characterization.

## 10. VERB

VERB records materially represented lexical relations. Retain actions, movements, perceptions, possession, cognition, remembering/finding, speaking/reporting/asking, helping/causing/attempting, waiting/standing/sitting, state/change relations, comparison/attribution, and **source-present bodily/performance actions** when they are represented occurrences rather than formatting noise.

Do not exclude a relation solely because it is metacognitive, reporting, stage/performance, copular, or embedded. Retain it when it performs a distinct binding job. Exclude pure auxiliaries, discourse floor-management, and support/matrix predicates only when removing them preserves the entire represented relation and posture.

Split coordinated/serial/matrix/embedded relations only when each supplies a distinct binding role. Keep particles, complements, negation/modality, and fixed expressions together when required for lexical identity.

## 11. LOCATOR

LOCATOR records source-native orientation relations: physical position, path/direction, origin/destination, entry/exit, containment/support, accompaniment, proximity/distance, temporal/recurring position, situational, figurative, or internal orientation.

A locator may overlap PLACE/TIME/LABEL/VERB when it carries a distinct orientation job in the same binding. Preserve deictic and relational wording when orientation would otherwise be lost. Do not census every preposition, recipient, beneficiary, topic, instrument, duration, or grammatical attachment.

## 12. Literal preservation lock

Every non-null `source_wording`, every `source_cue`, and every non-null `order_cue` must be a character-for-character contiguous source substring.

Preserve spelling, punctuation, dialect, contractions, number, questions, negation, uncertainty, correction, comparison, attribution, intention, recurrence/report/prospective posture, and source-present performance wording. Never substitute a synonym, standardize dialect, repair grammar, or normalize lexical content.

Researcher tags should use source wording where a named coordinate exists. Neutral tags are permitted only for supported unnamed PLACE/TIME navigation.

## 13. Ordering and qualities

Order by first source establishment after coreference, with speaker first in PERSON.

`qualities_available` is true only when the source supplies one or more qualities/descriptions for that coordinate. Do not infer qualities from mere occurrence.

## 14. Compound reconstruction

Freeze the primitive inventory first. Then reconstruct compounds from the source-order binding ledger.

Emit the smallest materially complete compound for each distinct represented binding. A compound includes **all and only** retained primitives co-bound in that occurrence: participants, material VERB relation(s), OBJECT content, LABEL quality/state, TIME frame, PLACE position, and LOCATOR orientation as applicable.

Do not emit pairwise closure, broad scene supersets, alternate decompositions, or duplicate subset/superset compounds. Do not attach a scene-wide coordinate to every binding unless the source/editorial grain materially binds it there. Conversely, a compound is incomplete if it omits a frozen coordinate that materially fills a slot in that binding.

Before finalizing, verify reference integrity, literal fidelity, one binding purpose per compound, no redundant alternate decomposition, and alignment between primitive scene/frame distinctions and compound membership.
