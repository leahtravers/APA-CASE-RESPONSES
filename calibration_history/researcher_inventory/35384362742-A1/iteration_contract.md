## Mission

Produce only a literal lightweight Researcher Inventory candidate from the supplied source.

Recover the source's **bounded independently reconnectable research coordinates at canonical class-native grain**. The inventory is broader than a semantic summary and narrower than a token, phrase, grammar, predicate, preposition, or arbitrary-clause census.

Low salience does not disqualify a coordinate. Lexical or grammatical separability does not by itself qualify one. First decide whether the source actually stages an independently reconnectable class-native research handle or a materially necessary support coordinate; only then resolve that admitted coordinate at canonical grain.

## 1. Construction order

Read the complete source before extracting any class. Silently resolve source order, true coreference, materially distinct settings/episodes, represented actors/referents, lexical relations, values/states, and contextual orientations.

For each requested class:

1. **BROAD CANDIDATE CONSIDERATION** — consider low-salience, nested, one-use, reported, remembered, prospective, uncertain, negative, figurative, and background material.
2. **BOUNDED ADMISSION TEST** — retain only a source-staged class-native handle that is independently reconnectable or materially required to preserve a distinct represented scene/frame/participant/referent/relation/value/orientation.
3. **CANONICAL GRAIN AFTER ADMISSION** — once a coordinate qualifies, choose the smallest complete source-native unit that expresses that one coordinate. Do not use atomic splitting as a reason to admit otherwise non-qualifying grammar or lexical fragments.
4. **TYPE BY RESEARCH USE** — classify by the research question the coordinate supplies, not by part of speech or surface spatial/temporal wording alone.
5. **CROSS-CLASS ENTITLEMENT** — overlap is allowed only when the same wording independently supplies different class-native research access.
6. **SUPPRESS SHADOWS AND SUPPORT** — suppress aliases, pronoun/deictic duplicates, auxiliaries/function grammar, unreified proposition shells, arbitrary analyst subparts, exact duplicate tokenizations, metadiscourse, generic context, and class shadows.
7. **LITERAL LOCK** — preserve source language exactly wherever a source string is required.

Freeze primitives only after every requested class is complete. Build compounds afterward from frozen primitives.

## 2. Bounded admission test

A candidate qualifies through at least one route.

### Route A — independent class-native handle

The source gives the candidate enough identity that a later researcher could select and reconnect to it as a distinct item of that class without reconstructing it merely from grammar or from another retained coordinate.

Low salience, one-use occurrence, reported status, uncertainty, figurative form, or background placement do not defeat this route.

### Route B — material support coordinate

The candidate is needed to preserve a materially distinct represented scene/frame/participant/referent/relation/value/orientation that would otherwise be collapsed, merged, or mistyped.

This route is narrow. Lexical detail, grammatical support, modifier content, generic context, or a prepositional complement does not qualify merely because it adds information.

### Negative check

Reject a candidate when removing it leaves the same class-native research access because it is only:

- an alias or alternate tokenization;
- auxiliary/control/support grammar rather than a separately staged relation;
- a local descriptive or temporal/spatial fragment whose work is already carried by another coordinate;
- an unreified proposition/event shell;
- metadiscourse organizing the telling;
- an automatic cross-class shadow;
- a generic orientation or sequencing cue rather than a materially queryable orientation.

## 3. PLACE — represented setting positions, not spatial vocabulary

PLACE is a represented physical/institutional setting or occurrence position at scene/interaction scale.

Retain explicit broad and contained settings when each is independently represented. Reconstruct a supported unnamed PLACE when a distinct interaction, wait, reported/remembered interpersonal scene, comparison scene, or present-telling situation has a represented occurrence position that would otherwise disappear or merge with another setting.

Do not create PLACE merely because wording names space, distance, water, a path, an object position, a community descriptor, or a figurative spatial phrase. A spatial expression that functions as a referent, comparison vehicle, label, or locator should be typed by that research use instead.

Supported unnamed PLACE may use `source_wording: null` with exact source evidence in `source_cue`.

## 4. TIME — episode/period frames, not temporal vocabulary

TIME is a represented episode, period, transition, recurring span, remembered/reported frame, prospective frame, relationship period, comparison frame, or present-telling/reflection frame at researcher-useful scale.

Retain an intended, recurring, future, remembered, reported, or present frame when the source stages it as a distinct reconnectable period/episode. Reconstruct supported unnamed TIME where required to keep such a frame distinct.

Do not create TIME merely from a frequency word, duration phrase, tense marker, sequencing adverb, predicate, subordinate clause, or local action that remains inside an already represented episode. Temporal wording may instead function as a locator or only as context.

## 5. PERSON

Retain `B` plus every distinct represented human/social actor or stable group after true coreference when the source gives that actor a distinguishable identity or participant role.

Minor, possessive/relational, offscreen, remembered, reported, prospective, and institutional actors may qualify. Exclude nonreferential grammatical/rhetorical addressees and true aliases already merged.

A PERSON span is also LABEL only when the source separately stages it as characterization/classification.

## 6. OBJECT — represented referents and reified contents

OBJECT is a source-established concrete or abstract referent/content coordinate with independent identity.

Retain distinct physical things, products/documents/parts, scene objects, amounts/values, choices, decisions, plans, relationships, recurring situations, reified mental contents, and comparison vehicles when the source treats them as things a researcher could reconnect to. Low salience does not exclude a real referent.

A spatially worded or event-related phrase may be OBJECT when its source job is referential rather than setting/orientation. Conversely, reject pronoun/deictic duplicates, arbitrary noun fragments, telling-management language, and proposition/event shells created only by nominalizing a clause. Do not duplicate a setting as OBJECT when its only job is PLACE.

## 7. LABEL — separately staged values/states/classifications

LABEL is a separately source-staged value, state, quality, classification, evaluation, comparison, correction, rejection, identity, manner, or status that itself provides a research handle.

Prefer the shortest complete source-native value/state span. Preserve question, negation, uncertainty, contrast, intensity, or deictic wording only when it belongs to the staged value/classification itself.

Do not turn every adjective, number, degree phrase, temporal phrase, proposition, cognition, intention, or predicate into LABEL. Ordinary description may instead be represented by `qualities_available` on its coordinate.

## 8. VERB — material lexical relations, with atomic grain after admission

VERB represents a separately staged content-bearing relation or happening that provides independent research access or materially preserves a represented relation edge.

First decide that the relation qualifies. Then split it to the smallest complete lexical relation grain.

For matrix/control/complement, perception/help, and serial/coordinated constructions, split separately predicated lexical relations when each relation is independently staged. Do not keep an admitted chain over-merged merely because the relations share a clause or participant.

But atomicity is **not** a predicate census. Suppress pure auxiliaries, do-support, empty copulas, aspect/support verbs, generic reporting scaffolding, infinitival support, metadiscourse, and lexical fragments whose only role is to support another admitted relation. A contemplated course may remain one relation when the source presents it as one selectable course rather than several independent happenings.

Preserve particles, reflexives, required complements, and negation when removing them changes relation identity.

## 9. LOCATOR — materially queryable orientation

LOCATOR is a source-native orientation handle that materially tells where, from where, toward where, through what, inside/outside what, with/along what, relative to what time/context, or in what represented comparative/figurative orientation an admitted coordinate/relation is staged.

Retain material position, containment, direction, origin/destination, path, entry/exit, proximity/distance, accompaniment/carrying, recurring-context orientation, and materially staged figurative/comparison orientation.

Do not inventory every deictic, sequencing adverb, temporal word, discourse relation, recipient phrase, ownership/provenance phrase, or prepositional complement. A locator should orient an admitted research coordinate/relation in a way that remains independently queryable after broad scene/frame coordinates are known.

Movement wording may overlap VERB and LOCATOR only when it separately supplies both a material happening and a material orientation handle.

## 10. Cross-class entitlement

The same wording may appear in multiple classes only when each projection answers a genuinely different class-native research question.

A valid coordinate in one class does not automatically create a shadow in another. Surface spatial wording does not automatically mean PLACE; temporal wording does not automatically mean TIME; a predication does not automatically mean LABEL; a noun phrase does not automatically mean OBJECT; a preposition does not automatically mean LOCATOR.

## 11. Literal preservation lock

Every non-null `source_wording`, every `source_cue`, every non-null `order_cue`, and every source-derived short tag must preserve source language character-for-character where the schema requires source text.

Never substitute synonyms, repair grammar, normalize dialect, change numbers, expand contractions, clean punctuation, or invent semantically convenient wording.

Only genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME frames may use `source_wording: null`, with exact source evidence in `source_cue`.

## 12. Qualities and order

`qualities_available` is boolean only. Set it true when source-present qualities/descriptions are available for that coordinate or compound. It does not require a separate LABEL.

Order by first source establishment after true coreference, with speaker-first PERSON and broad-before-contained spatial ordering only where consistent with source navigation.

## 13. Primitive freeze

Complete whole-source candidate consideration, bounded admission, canonical grain/type resolution, cross-class entitlement, coreference, order, qualities, suppression, and literal lock before compounds.

Compounds may never create, add, delete, merge, retype, or repair primitives.

## 14. Compound reconstruction — local material bindings

After primitives freeze, build compounds from distinct local material source propositions/events/states/orientations.

An admitted material VERB normally anchors one smallest complete local compound when another frozen coordinate participates. A separately staged LABEL or LOCATOR may anchor a compound when it binds multiple frozen coordinates into a material state/orientation without a better VERB anchor.

Include only coordinates that materially participate in that one local binding: relevant PERSON/OBJECT roles, admitted relation/value/orientation units, and useful PLACE/TIME anchors. Split compounds when separately admitted relations form separately selectable local propositions; combine relations only when the source presents one inseparable event/course.

Do not emit pairwise closure, nested subset variants, alternate tokenizations, one-compound-per-primitive, grammatical-support bundles, whole-scene mega-bundles, or cross-proposition bundles.
