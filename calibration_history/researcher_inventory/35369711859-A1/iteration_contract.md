## Mission

Produce only a literal lightweight Researcher Inventory candidate from the supplied source.

Recover the source's **source-staged research coordinates at canonical class-native grain**. The inventory is broader than a semantic summary and narrower than a token, phrase, grammar, or arbitrary-clause census. Low salience does not make a source-staged coordinate disposable. At the same time, a phrase does not become a coordinate merely because it can be named or grammatically isolated.

Do not interpret psychological meaning, score APA, infer protected threads, draw conclusions, promote records, mint APA IDs, or admit anything to sovereign/production fabric. The worker never receives approved archetypes, expected counts, evaluator findings, prior scored outputs, sealed holdout source during calibration, or holdout outputs.

## 1. Construction order

Read the complete source before extracting any class. Silently resolve source order, true coreference, materially distinct settings/episodes, represented actors/referents, lexical relations, values/states, and contextual orientations.

For each requested class:

1. **BROAD CANDIDATE COVERAGE** — consider every plausible source-staged coordinate, including low-salience, nested, one-use, reported, remembered, prospective, uncertain, negative, figurative, and background material.
2. **SOURCE-STAGED COORDINATE TEST** — retain a candidate when the source distinctly establishes that class-native research handle; do not require global salience, compound membership, repetition, or topology necessity.
3. **CANONICAL GRAIN** — choose the smallest complete source-native unit that expresses that one coordinate. Split neighboring lexical relations/values/orientations instead of bundling them simply because they share a clause.
4. **TYPE BY RESEARCH USE** — classify by the question the coordinate lets a researcher ask, not by part of speech alone.
5. **CROSS-CLASS ENTITLEMENT** — overlap is allowed only when the same wording independently supplies different class-native research access.
6. **HARD-EXCLUSION SUPPRESSION** — suppress only true aliases, pronoun/deictic duplicates, pure auxiliaries/function grammar, unsupported proposition shells, arbitrary analyst subparts, exact duplicate/alternate tokenizations, nonreferential rhetoric, and true class shadows.
7. **LITERAL LOCK** — preserve source language exactly wherever a source string is required.

Freeze primitives only after every requested class is complete. Build compounds afterward from frozen primitives.

## 2. Source-staged coordinate presumption

When the source distinctly stages a class-native actor, referent, setting/frame, value/state, lexical relation, or contextual orientation, presume it belongs unless a hard exclusion applies.

Do **not** reject a coordinate merely because:

- another retained coordinate already captures the broad scene or event;
- it is background detail, one-use, nested, reported, remembered, hypothetical, prospective, uncertain, negative, or figurative;
- omitting it would leave the overall narrative understandable;
- it is not central to the speaker's main point;
- it never participates in a compound.

The inventory preserves research reconnectability, not only minimum narrative topology.

## 3. Hard exclusions

Reject a candidate when it is only:

- a true coreferential alias or pronoun/deictic duplicate of an already retained coordinate;
- pure auxiliary, complementizer, determiner, conjunction, or other function grammar without independent class-native research use;
- an arbitrary subspan cut from a larger source-native unit;
- an unsupported nominalization of a whole clause/proposition that the source does not establish as a stable referent;
- meta-discourse whose only job is to organize the telling rather than establish a research coordinate;
- an exact duplicate or alternate tokenization of the same class-native coordinate;
- a mechanical shadow created solely because another class already retained the wording;
- a nonreferential rhetorical addressee or discourse filler.

Suppression is evidence-based. Do not use vague notions such as importance, centrality, elegance, redundancy, or narrative compression to thin valid source-staged coordinates.

## 4. PLACE — represented setting positions, including supported unnamed positions

PLACE is a represented physical/institutional setting or occurrence position.

Retain explicit broad and contained settings when each is separately represented. Also reconstruct a supported unnamed PLACE when the source distinguishes a setting/position through a participant configuration, interaction, wait, arrival/departure, reported/remembered scene, comparison scene, or present-telling context even if no proper place phrase names it.

A new PLACE does not arise from every noun, surface, path phrase, or object position. The test is whether the source stages a distinguishable setting/position frame, not whether a preposition exists.

Supported unnamed PLACE may use `source_wording: null` with exact source evidence in `source_cue`.

## 5. TIME — represented episode/period frames, including supported unnamed frames

TIME is a represented episode, period, transition, recurring span, remembered/reported frame, prospective frame, comparison frame, stable relationship period, material date/time-of-day frame, or present-reflection/telling frame.

Retain a supported unnamed TIME whenever the source distinguishes an episode/frame that a later researcher could reconnect to even if no explicit temporal phrase names it. Distinct interactions, waits, departures, intended periods, recurring practice, later explanation/reflection, and future recurrence may form separate frames when the source stages them separately.

Do not create TIME for every predicate, tense marker, frequency word, duration token, or subordinate clause. The unit is a represented episode/period frame, not grammar.

## 6. PERSON

Retain `B` plus every distinct represented human/social actor or stable group after true coreference when the source gives that actor a distinguishable identity or participant role.

Minor, possessive/relational, offscreen, remembered, reported, prospective, and institutional actors may qualify. Exclude nonreferential grammatical/rhetorical addressees and true aliases already merged.

A PERSON span is also LABEL only when the source separately stages it as characterization/classification.

## 7. OBJECT — source-established referents, including low-salience scene objects

OBJECT is a source-established concrete or abstract referent/content coordinate.

Retain distinct physical things, products/documents/parts, scene objects, amounts/values, choices, decisions, plans, relationships, recurring situations, mental contents, comparison vehicles, and other referents that the source actually establishes as things a researcher could reconnect to. Low-salience physical objects are not excluded merely because they are descriptive background.

Distinguish materially different referents even when ordinary discourse connects them closely. Merge only true coreference.

Reject pronoun/deictic duplicates, arbitrary noun fragments, pure telling-management/meta-discourse, and event/proposition shells created by nominalizing a clause when the source does not establish a stable referent beyond that event. Do not duplicate a setting as OBJECT when its only job is PLACE.

## 8. LABEL — atomic source-staged value/state/classification handles

LABEL is a source-staged value, state, quality, classification, evaluation, comparison, correction, rejection, identity, manner, deictic characterization, or status that is itself selectable as a research handle.

Prefer the **atomic lexical value/state span** over a whole proposition. Preserve question, negation, uncertainty, contrast, intensity, and attribution only when they belong to that value/classification itself.

A source can stage a short state or characterization even when it occurs inside a larger relation. Conversely, do not convert a whole cognition, intention, action, question, or proposition into LABEL when the class-native handle is actually VERB/OBJECT or only ordinary predication.

`qualities_available` may remain true for descriptive material that does not deserve its own LABEL row.

## 9. VERB — separately lexicalized source relations at atomic complete grain

VERB represents each distinguishable **content-bearing lexical relation or happening** asserted/staged by the source.

Retain lexical actions, changes, transfers, choices, reports, perceptions, speech acts, cognition/intention, possession, location relations, and other source-staged predicates when they express a distinct relation a researcher could reconnect to. Low-salience, nested, subordinate, reported, serial, or perception/help/control relations may qualify.

Use the smallest complete source-native relation span. Preserve particles, reflexives, required complements, and negation when removing them changes relation identity.

**Do not over-merge.** When a matrix/control/complement or serial/coordinated construction contains separately lexicalized relations that the source actually predicates, split them into separate VERB coordinates even when they share a subject, object, clause, or episode. Keep them together only when they form one genuinely lexicalized relation or one explicitly single contemplated course whose internal words do not establish separately selectable happenings.

Suppress pure auxiliaries, copular/support grammar with no independent content relation, and alternate tokenizations of the same relation.

## 10. LOCATOR — source-native contextual orientation, not only physical prepositions

LOCATOR is a source-native orientation/context handle that tells where, when-relative-to-what, from where, toward where, through what, with/along what, inside/outside what, across what recurring context, or in what represented comparative/figurative orientation a coordinate/relation is staged.

Retain direct position, containment/surface, direction, origin/destination, entry/exit, path, proximity/distance, accompaniment/carrying, recurrence/context anchoring, embodied/internal orientation, and materially represented figurative/comparison orientation. A simple deictic, temporal-context, or prepositional span may qualify when it supplies a distinct orientation handle.

Do not retain generic syntax, pure ownership/provenance/attribution, or every prepositional complement merely because it connects words. Movement wording may overlap VERB and LOCATOR when it separately supplies both a happening and an orientation/context handle.

## 11. Cross-class entitlement

The same wording may appear in multiple classes only when each projection answers a genuinely different class-native research question.

A valid coordinate in one class does not automatically create a shadow in another. Do not create PLACE/TIME for every relation, LABEL for every predicate/modifier, OBJECT for every proposition, or LOCATOR for every prepositional complement.

Cross-class overlap is permitted when the source independently supports both jobs; grammatical category alone neither grants nor forbids overlap.

## 12. Literal preservation lock

Every non-null `source_wording`, every `source_cue`, every non-null `order_cue`, and every source-derived short tag must preserve source language character-for-character where the schema requires source text.

Never substitute synonyms, repair grammar, normalize dialect, change numbers, expand contractions, clean punctuation, or invent semantically convenient wording.

Only genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME frames may use `source_wording: null`, with exact source evidence in `source_cue`.

## 13. Qualities and order

`qualities_available` is boolean only. Set it true when source-present qualities/descriptions are available for that coordinate or compound. It does not require a separate LABEL.

Order by first source establishment after true coreference, with speaker-first PERSON and broad-before-contained spatial ordering only where consistent with source navigation.

## 14. Primitive freeze

Complete whole-source candidate coverage, source-staged coordinate decisions, canonical grain/type resolution, cross-class entitlement, coreference, order, qualities, hard-exclusion suppression, and literal lock before compounds.

Compounds may never create, add, delete, merge, retype, or repair primitives.

## 15. Compound reconstruction — anchor-driven local proposition/event bindings

After primitives freeze, build compounds from **distinct local source propositions/events/states/orientations**, not from arbitrary pairwise closure and not only from a few globally focal moments.

A retained content VERB normally anchors one smallest materially complete local compound when at least one other frozen coordinate participates in that source relation. A separately staged LABEL or LOCATOR may anchor a compound when it binds multiple frozen coordinates into a source proposition/state/orientation without a better VERB anchor.

Include only coordinates locally participating in that one source-staged binding: relevant PERSON/OBJECT roles, relation/value/orientation units, and useful applicable PLACE/TIME anchors. Split compounds when the source asserts separately selectable relations even inside the same sentence. Combine multiple relations only when they form one inseparable source event/course.

Do not emit pairwise closure, nested subset variants, alternate tokenizations, one-compound-per-primitive, whole-scene mega-bundles, or bundles that reach across unrelated propositions merely because coordinates share a scene.

5. **Verbs:** separately lexicalized relations are split at atomic complete grain; auxiliaries/support grammar and over-merged chains are excluded.
6. **Locators:** contextual orientations are covered beyond only globally salient spatial paths, without a preposition census.
7. **Cross-class:** every overlap has independent class-native research value.
8. **Coreference and literal lock:** aliases merge correctly and source language remains exact.
9. **Compounds:** local anchor-driven proposition/event bindings only from frozen primitives.
