Only afterward build a selective set of multi-coordinate compounds from those frozen coordinates. A clause or proposition is not itself a primitive merely because it contains one.**

## Mission

Read the complete source and recover source-native research coordinates in exactly seven classes:

1. `PLACE`
2. `TIME`
3. `PERSON`
4. `OBJECT`
5. `LABEL`
6. `VERB`
7. `LOCATOR`

Preserve literal source language and posture. Recover a comprehensive but non-fragmented primitive inventory at class-native grain, freeze that inventory, and then construct only materially useful lightweight compounds.

Do not perform APA scoring, protected-thread analysis, psychological interpretation, promotion, APA-ID creation, executive analysis, sovereign/admitted writing, or APA Data Fabric mutation.

## 1. Two-layer method: coordinates first, compounds second

Primitive inventory and compound construction are different tasks.

### Layer A — primitive coordinates

For each requested class, inventory independently selectable source coordinates in that class. A primitive can be valid even when it never appears in a compound. Do not use likely compound membership, narrative salience, thematic centrality, or later usefulness as the admission test.

### Layer B — selective compounds

After all seven primitive classes are frozen, construct only the smallest useful multi-coordinate bindings. Compounds may use primitives, but they do not define which primitives exist.

Never add a primitive merely to make a compound easier. Never delete a valid primitive merely because it is unbundled.

## 2. Primitive admission: source-reified coordinate test

A primitive is admitted when the source makes it independently selectable in the requested class at a stable semantic grain.

A coordinate may qualify through one of these routes:

### 2.1 `EXPLICIT_COORDINATE`

The source directly presents a distinct actor, referent, characterization, predicate/relation, orientation, place, or time frame that can be revisited without restating an entire proposition.

This includes ordinary concrete scene entities and low-salience source objects. A coordinate does not need to be thematically important or used in a compound.

### 2.2 `REIFIED_ABSTRACT_COORDINATE`

The source treats an abstract choice, decision, relation, condition, internal object, amount, set, category, or other nonphysical content as a distinct handle that can be referred to or related to independently.

Do not manufacture analyst abstractions. The source must supply the relation or reification.

### 2.3 `IMPLICIT_SUPPORT`

Available only to `PLACE` and `TIME`. Admit a neutral support coordinate when a distinctly represented relation/episode/span needs a place or time frame for researcher reconnection but the exact setting/time is not named.

For implicit support:
- `source_wording` may be null;
- `source_cue` must be exact source text anchoring the supported relation/episode/span;
- the short tag must be neutral and mechanical;
- do not invent geography, chronology, actors, causes, or substantive facts.

## 3. Proposition-versus-coordinate boundary

A sentence or clause is a container for coordinates, not automatically a coordinate.

Do not return a whole clause as:
- a `VERB` merely because the clause contains a predicate;
- a `LABEL` merely because the clause conveys an evaluation;
- an `OBJECT` merely because the clause is content of thought/speech;
- a `TIME` merely because the clause occurs in sequence;
- a `LOCATOR` merely because the clause contains a preposition, adverb, recipient, topic, or temporal wording.

Instead identify the smallest complete class-native kernel or frame inside the clause.

A whole phrase may remain intact only when the entire phrase is itself the source-reified coordinate, such as an idiomatic relation, an inseparable characterization, or a source-treated abstract handle.

## 4. Referential-handle rule

For `PERSON`, `OBJECT`, and source-reified `PLACE`, ask whether the wording denotes a distinct source entity/handle that a researcher could reconnect later.

Concrete scene objects qualify when the source actually presents them as entities, even if they are mentioned once and never enter a compound.

Generic anaphors or metadiscourse terms do not qualify merely because they are nouns or pronouns. Forms such as `it`, `this`, `that`, `something`, `point`, `part`, `thing`, or similar wording require a distinct source-reified identity in that occurrence. If they merely stand in for already represented content or organize discourse, do not create a new primitive.

## 5. Class-native rules

### PLACE

Inventory settings and location-support coordinates in which represented actors, objects, relations, conversations, waits, reports, reflections, or other distinct material are situated.

Keep a broad setting and a materially distinct contained/local setting separate when both function as researcher locations. Use `IMPLICIT_SUPPORT` for a distinct relationship/episode/conversation/present-telling setting whose exact place is not supplied.

Do not type a phrase as PLACE merely because its wording is spatial. A source-treated space, surface, distance, container, or figurative region that functions as a referent rather than the setting of represented material belongs in its referential class, usually OBJECT or LOCATOR.

Do not create a new PLACE for every clause or movement.

### TIME

Inventory semantic episode/span frames, not temporal tokens.

A TIME may represent an attempt/response episode, transition, wait, later conversation/report, recurring span, relationship period, present reflection/telling, intention, or prospective frame when that frame is distinct and useful for reconnecting represented material.

Merge multiple temporal expressions that support the same semantic frame. Do not create a TIME merely because the source says now, then, always, every time, for a duration, or uses tense. Use source cues to build one neutral frame at the represented episode/span grain.

Use `IMPLICIT_SUPPORT` when a distinct episode/span exists but its exact time is not named.

### PERSON

Retain the speaker and every distinct represented human/social actor or stable group after true coreference.

Minor, one-use, offscreen, remembered, reported, relational, possessive, beneficiary, institutional, or prospective actors may qualify when the source represents them as distinct people/groups.

Suppress rhetorical/nonreferential addressees and true aliases/coreferent repeats.

### OBJECT

Inventory independently selectable concrete or abstract referential handles.

Concrete scene entities, physical items, documents, amounts, parts, and source-presented environmental objects qualify when they are represented as things, even when low-salience or unbundled.

Abstract choices, decisions, relations, conditions, internal objects, sets/categories, and other nonphysical content qualify only when the source reifies them as distinct handles.

Do not turn every noun phrase, clause complement, nominalized action, generic anaphor, explanatory phrase, or proposition into an OBJECT. Do not type an entity as PLACE merely because the entity's wording sounds spatial.

### LABEL

Inventory source-presented characterizations, qualities, states, identities, comparisons, candidate labels, corrections, acceptances/rejections, and polarity units at the smallest complete characterization grain.

When the source presents a sequence of candidate characterization units, denials, revisions, or alternatives, preserve each independently selectable characterization/polarity unit rather than merging the whole sequence into one label.

Do not return an entire proposition just because it communicates attitude. Do not split intensifiers or grammatical fragments away from the characterization they qualify. Do not duplicate wording whose only role is already integral to an OBJECT identity.

### VERB

Inventory semantic relation kernels, not propositions and not raw verb tokens.

Use the shortest complete source-native predicate construction that preserves the relation's identity and posture.

Normally:
- omit the subject from the VERB;
- externalize independently selectable actor, object/content, label, place, time, and locator arguments into their own classes;
- keep particles, reflexives, required prepositions, negation, modality, light-verb support, or complements only when they are necessary to preserve the predicate's lexical/semantic identity;
- keep generic pronominal material only when it is inseparable from the source relation construction;
- split matrix, embedded, or coordinated relations when each is independently selectable.

A clause-sized restatement of subject + predicate + content is over-broad when a smaller predicate kernel preserves the relation.

Do not inventory copular/auxiliary/support wording by itself when it carries no distinct semantic relation.

### LOCATOR

Inventory source-native orientation/frame relations that position a represented coordinate relative to another coordinate or frame.

These may include:
- spatial position or containment;
- origin/destination/path;
- movement direction or approach/exit;
- accompaniment when it functions as orientation;
- proximity or relative position;
- figurative/comparative orientation;
- a recurrence/context frame when it genuinely situates represented material.

Use the smallest complete orienting construction. A LOCATOR may overlap wording with a VERB, PLACE, TIME, or LABEL when the orientation facet is independently selectable.

Do not create a LOCATOR merely from:
- every preposition/particle/adverb;
- a recipient or addressee relation;
- a topic introduced by wording such as `about` when it only supplies content;
- a duration or temporal token already serving a TIME frame;
- discourse deixis with no independent orientation;
- an entire proposition that happens to contain orienting words.

## 6. Cross-class overlap without substitution

Different classes preserve different research functions.

The same source trace may support more than one class when each row preserves a genuinely different class-native coordinate. A movement phrase can carry both a relation kernel and an orientation; a characterization can coexist with an object it characterizes; a location can coexist with a locator relation.

Do not suppress one valid class because another class overlaps its wording. Do not duplicate identical semantic work across classes when there is no different class-native function.

## 7. Semantic identity and deduplication

Primitive identity follows the represented coordinate, not mention count.

Merge true aliases, coreference, and restatements of one stable actor/referent/characterization/support/orientation.

For relations, create separate VERB rows when the source presents distinct relation occurrences even if the lexical kernel repeats and each occurrence is independently reconnectable.

Do not collapse distinct source coordinates merely because their words are similar. Do not multiply one stable coordinate merely because it is mentioned repeatedly.

## 8. Source-native grain and literal lock

Select the smallest complete semantic unit that performs the class job.

For source-derived wording:
- preserve exact source language, dialect, contractions, numbers, uncertainty, negation, modality, comparison, attribution, punctuation, and figurative posture;
- do not normalize, improve, clean, translate, diagnose, euphemize, summarize, or replace source wording with a synonym;
- do not introduce analyst-preferred labels;
- use neutral mechanical tags only for legitimate implicit PLACE/TIME support.

A split is valid only when each resulting row has its own independently selectable class-native job.

## 9. Primitive freeze audits

Before compounds, complete these audits for every class.

### 9.1 Referent sweep
For PERSON and OBJECT, scan the entire source again for represented entities/handles, including ordinary concrete scene entities and low-salience one-use referents. Restore any independently selectable handle omitted merely because it seemed narratively incidental.

### 9.2 Proposition de-bundling
For VERB, LABEL, OBJECT, and LOCATOR, inspect every clause-sized candidate. If a smaller kernel/handle/frame performs the class job while the remaining words belong to other coordinates or discourse scaffolding, shrink or remove the broad candidate.

### 9.3 Characterization atomization
For LABEL, preserve independently selectable candidate/denial/correction/comparison units separately. Remove sentence-level bundles that merely collect several such units.

### 9.4 Support-frame audit
For PLACE/TIME, distinguish actual researcher support frames from surface spatial/temporal words. Restore needed implicit frames; collapse redundant subframes belonging to the same episode/span.

### 9.5 Locator boundary audit
Restore orientation frames omitted because another class overlaps their wording. Remove recipient/topic/duration/discourse phrases that do not independently orient represented material.

### 9.6 Dedup and fidelity audit
Collapse true semantic duplicates and confirm exact source language/posture. Remove any synonym, analyst-created abstraction, or unsupported inference.

Repeat until all audits pass.

## 10. `qualities_available`

`qualities_available` is a mechanical boolean only.

It is true when the source supplies qualitative/descriptive material associated with that coordinate or compound under the schema. It is not confidence, importance, salience, admission authority, or a demand to create a LABEL.

A quality may make `qualities_available` true without becoming its own primitive. A LABEL can exist independently without forcing `qualities_available` true on every neighboring row.

## 11. Selective compound construction

After primitive freeze, build only materially useful multi-coordinate research bindings.

A primitive may remain unbundled. Do not manufacture a compound merely because a VERB, LABEL, TIME, PLACE, PERSON, OBJECT, or LOCATOR exists.

Create a compound when two or more frozen coordinates jointly express one source-local relation/event/frame that is materially useful to reconnect as a bundle. Use the smallest set of refs needed to identify that binding.

Prefer one canonical compound for one useful source-local binding. Do not create:
- graph closure;
- pairwise closure;
- one compound per primitive;
- a compound for every proposition or sentence;
- singleton-equivalent bundles;
- alternate subset/superset decompositions;
- redundant restatements;
- broad scene mega-bundles.

Compounds recompose relation kernels with their independently frozen participants/content/support/characterization/orientation. They do not repair missing primitives and they do not justify extra primitives.

Preserve source-local semantic order and source posture. Use `qualities_available` only as the mechanical Q boolean.
