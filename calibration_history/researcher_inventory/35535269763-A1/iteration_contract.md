Across the repeated V122 failures, the stable defect is not lack of literal coverage alone. > **BUILD THE MATERIAL EPISODE LEDGER FIRST. A PRIMITIVE IS ADMITTED ONLY AS A SOURCE-NATIVE VALUE FILLING A DISTINCT ROLE IN THAT LEDGER. TYPE THE VALUE BY THE ROLE THE SOURCE USES IT TO PERFORM, NOT BY THE WORDS' SURFACE SHAPE.**

This is a prospective, generalizable worker correction only. No archetype rows, expected counts, evaluator findings, prior scored outputs, calibration answers, canonical gold extracts, or sealed holdout material are supplied to the worker.

## Mission

Produce a literal, lightweight Researcher Inventory of the complete supplied source in exactly seven primitive classes:

1. `PLACE`
2. `TIME`
3. `PERSON`
4. `OBJECT`
5. `LABEL`
6. `VERB`
7. `LOCATOR`

Then build lightweight compounds only from frozen registered primitives.

The inventory is neither a sparse summary nor a surface-span census. It preserves the source-native researcher coordinates that occupy distinct represented roles at the intended editorial resolution.

## 1. Read the whole source before any class answer

Read the complete source first. Do not extract one class by scanning isolated sentences for words that look like that class.

Before answering any requested class, privately build the material episode ledger described below. The ledger is internal reasoning only and must never be emitted.

## 2. Private material episode ledger — mandatory first pass

Segment the source into materially differentiated represented episodes, not sentences or clauses.

Open a new episode/support row only when the source itself differentiates at least one of these:

- spatial support;
- temporal phase or episode;
- materially different participant configuration;
- materially different action/relation cluster;
- memory/report versus directly narrated occurrence;
- intention/choice/prospect versus occurrence;
- question/uncertainty/correction/rejection posture;
- reflection or present-telling frame;
- another source-established shift that changes what is being represented.

Several sentences may belong to one episode. One sentence may contain more than one materially distinct episode. Do not equate punctuation with episode boundaries.

For each episode, privately fill only the roles actually established by the source:

- spatial support;
- temporal support;
- participants/endpoints;
- thing/content handles;
- applied characterizations or states;
- operative relation kernels;
- orientation/context relations;
- source posture where needed to preserve whether content is remembered, reported, questioned, negated, uncertain, intended, hypothetical, prospective, corrected, or rejected.

The episode ledger is the admission surface. If a candidate cannot be placed as a distinct source-established value in one of these roles, do not emit it merely because its wording is literal, vivid, grammatical, concrete, descriptive, or semantically plausible.

## 3. Primitive admission — ledger-constrained source-use role

Admit a primitive only when all applicable conditions hold.

### A. Source grounding

The coordinate is established by the source. `PLACE` and `TIME` may be unnamed only when the source clearly establishes a distinct support frame. Never invent content.

### B. Ledger role

The candidate fills one distinct role in the private material episode ledger.

A valid role may be local, one-use, mundane, colloquial, uncertain, negated, remembered, reported, hypothetical, prospective, figurative, idiomatic, or peripheral. It does not need recurrence, persistence, global salience, or compound indispensability.

But a literal span with no distinct ledger role is not a primitive.

### C. Material binding participation

The role must participate in, support, characterize, orient, or provide an endpoint/content handle for something materially represented in the episode ledger.

Reject lexical furniture that only elaborates an already represented scene or proposition without occupying a separate researcher-selectable role. Reject narration/discourse management, rhetorical color, isolated intensification, generic scene detail, and grammar/support scaffolding unless the source separately construes them as a ledger value.

### D. Natural editorial grain

Use the smallest complete source-native unit that performs the ledger role without losing represented meaning.

Keep idioms, phrasal relations, negation, modality, reflexives, bound complements, comparisons, orientation constructions, and other natural multiword units whole when splitting would destroy the role. Do not inflate a coordinate into an entire clause, proposition, question shell, or rhetorical bundle when a smaller source-native nucleus performs the role.

### E. Nonduplication

Merge true aliases/coreference and same-class restatements of one coordinate. Do not multiply rows from repeated wording, syntactic recasts, nested subspans, or alternate phrasings that do not establish a new ledger role.

## 4. Source-use role controls type — mandatory arbitration

Do not classify a span from what its words seem to denote in isolation. First ask what job the source is making that exact span perform in the represented episode.

Use this precedence by represented job:

- support in space -> `PLACE`;
- support in time/episode -> `TIME`;
- human/social participant or stable group -> `PERSON`;
- thing/content handle being referred to, related, possessed, considered, remembered, reported, compared, or otherwise treated as an endpoint/node -> `OBJECT`;
- characterization, status, evaluation, identity, comparison, state, rejection, correction, or other predication applied to a target -> `LABEL`;
- operative action/state/relation kernel connecting or positioning represented values -> `VERB`;
- orientation/path/direction/containment/proximity/context relation -> `LOCATOR`.

A phrase with spatial-sounding words is not `PLACE` when the source is using the phrase as a thing/content handle. A noun-like phrase is not automatically `OBJECT` when it is being applied as a characterization. An adjective-like phrase is not automatically `LABEL` when the source treats it as quoted or represented content. A verbal surface form is not automatically `VERB` when it is only a report/question shell or a thing/content handle. A temporal expression is not automatically `TIME` when it merely orients an episode.

Cross-class duplication is allowed only when the source genuinely establishes two independently selectable represented jobs, not because one phrase admits several grammatical descriptions.

## 5. PLACE — spatial support ledger

Retain every materially differentiated spatial support established by the private episode ledger.

A spatial support may be explicit or unnamed, broad or contained, directly narrated or remembered/reported, destination/later, interaction-specific, waiting-specific, or present-telling when the source establishes it.

Do not require a place noun. Do not infer an invented location. Several bindings may share one PLACE when the source does not differentiate support. Conversely, distinct unnamed PLACE rows are required when materially different episodes have spatially distinct support even if neither place is named.

For unnamed PLACE use null `source_wording`, an exact source cue, and a neutral mechanical tag.

Do not turn every physical noun, spatial phrase, destination phrase, or scenic reference into PLACE. PLACE is support, not surface geography.

## 6. TIME — temporal support ledger

Retain every materially differentiated temporal episode/support established by the private episode ledger.

A temporal support may be explicit or unnamed and may include an attempt/condition episode, response phase, transition, wait, intended period, remembered/reported period, later conversation/report episode, recurring span, present-reflection frame, or prospective frame.

Do not require clock/date wording. Several relations may share one TIME where they belong to the same episode. Conversely, open distinct unnamed TIME rows when the source materially differentiates episode support.

For unnamed TIME use null `source_wording`, an exact source cue, and a neutral mechanical tag.

Do not inventory temporal expressions merely because they mention duration, recurrence, sequence, or time. Type them by source-use role.

## 7. PERSON — participant ledger

Retain the speaker plus every distinct source-established human/social actor or stable group after true coreference when that actor fills a participant, anchor, possessor/beneficiary, relation-endpoint, remembered/reported, institutional, prospective, peripheral, or one-use role in the episode ledger.

Reject generic/rhetorical/nonreferential person wording that never becomes a represented actor coordinate.

## 8. OBJECT — thing/content-handle ledger

Retain source-established concrete or abstract thing/content handles that fill an independently usable endpoint/node role in the episode ledger.

OBJECT may include concrete things or distinguished parts, amounts/values, services/results, decisions or next steps treated as things, choices treated as choices, named sets/categories, remembered/reported content, quoted or figurative content when treated as content, internal represented handles, and other source-construed nodes.

The controlling question is not whether the words are nouns. Ask whether the source is treating this exact unit as something that can stand as the content/endpoint of a represented relation.

Reject generic noun phrases, pronouns/deictics, clause/proposition shells, explanations/discourse points, scenic props with no separate ledger role, descriptive fragments, and nominalized actions without an independent thing/content job.

## 9. LABEL — target-bearing characterization ledger

Retain distinct source-applied characterization events that fill a target-bearing predication role in the episode ledger.

A LABEL may express state, status, identity, evaluation, comparison, self/candidate label, correction, rejection, contrast, or materially represented posture. It may be one-use, colloquial, idiomatic, uncertain, questioned, negated, corrected, rejected, or figurative.

Require a target or represented state being characterized. Do not admit a phrase as LABEL merely because it sounds descriptive or adjectival.

Use the smallest complete exact source-native form carrying the characterization. Reject decorative/rhetorical color, incidental description, generic manner wording, detached modifiers/intensifiers, and scene-setting language that never fills an independent target-bearing role.

## 10. VERB — operative relation ledger

Retain each distinct source-native operative relation kernel filling one material relation role in the episode ledger.

A VERB may express action, state, cognition, perception, communication, intention, decision, possession, comparison, evaluation, movement, transition, gesture, existence/location, obligation, recurrence, or another represented relation. One-use relations qualify.

Do not create one VERB per lexical verb or clause. Reject auxiliaries/support verbs, filler/discourse predicates, reporting or question shells when the represented content is carried elsewhere, empty copular scaffolding, duplicate restatements, whole-clause wrappers, rhetorical bundles, and fragments nested inside a stronger natural relation kernel.

Preserve particles, reflexives, negation, modality, and bound complements when required for relation identity. Preserve nonasserted posture.

## 11. LOCATOR — orientation/context ledger

Retain each distinct source-native orientation/context relation filling one material orientation role in the episode ledger.

A LOCATOR may express position, path, direction, containment, proximity, movement orientation, accompaniment, origin/destination, entry/exit, recurrence/context, internal/relational orientation, or materially spatialized figurative orientation. One-use orientations qualify.

Do not create LOCATOR from every prepositional/adverbial phrase. Reject recipient/topic/purpose/possession/degree complements, generic adverbials, isolated deictics with no independent orientation role, and rhetorical location wording.

A spatial or temporal phrase may be LOCATOR when it orients a larger support frame rather than constituting the PLACE/TIME support itself.

## 12. Literal lock and source posture

Every non-null `source_wording`, `source_cue`, and `order_cue` must be character-for-character source text.

Preserve colloquial language, dialect, spelling/grammar, idiom, figurative wording, negation, modality, uncertainty, question form, attribution, comparison, remembered/reported posture, hypothetical/prospective posture, correction, and rejection.

Never normalize, polish, translate, diagnose, euphemize, substitute synonyms, or lemmatize into a different surface form.

Researcher short tags are navigational only and must remain source-near.

## 13. Qualities flag

`qualities_available` is only a yes/no availability flag indicating that source qualities/descriptions exist for a coordinate. Do not replace LABEL rows with Q and do not expand qualities into APA analysis.

## 14. Primitive freeze audit — ledger completeness and compression

Before compounds:

1. replay the source by material episodes rather than sentences;
2. confirm every materially differentiated episode has the spatial/temporal support the source establishes;
3. confirm each participant, thing/content handle, characterization, operative relation, and orientation fills a distinct ledger role;
4. restore legitimate local/one-use/colloquial/uncertain/figurative values omitted only because they looked small or nonpersistent;
5. remove candidates admitted only because they are literal spans, nouns, verbs, adjectives, prepositional phrases, scenic details, rhetorical material, or grammatical constituents;
6. re-run source-use type arbitration without relying on lexical shape;
7. reduce relation-like units to the smallest complete natural source-native grain;
8. verify exact wording, coreference, class-local distinctness, and first-establishment order; and
9. freeze primitives.

Coverage and compression are equally mandatory. Do not solve over-admission by sparse summarization. Do not solve omission by lexical census.

## 15. Compounds — selective material episode bindings

Build compounds only after primitive freeze.

Return to the same private material episode ledger. A valid primitive may remain unbundled. Emit a compound only when two or more frozen coordinates jointly form one source-local materially complete researcher binding.

For each compound:

- choose one materially distinct represented relation/characterization/question/choice/report/reflection binding;
- include only the actual participating primitive refs;
- include support/orientation refs only when they distinguish or materially situate that binding;
- split when actor set, target/content, source posture, support frame, or independently represented relation changes materially;
- keep jointly functioning predicates together only when the source makes them one binding.

Do not create one compound per sentence, clause, lexical verb, primitive, phrase, or episode row. Do not create a compound merely to justify a primitive. Do not create arbitrary co-occurrence, all-pairs links, support chains, scene mega-bundles, singleton equivalents, duplicate restatements, alternate subset/superset decompositions, or graph closure.

## 16. Identity and ordering

Primitive identity is class-local source identity after true coreference, not mention count.

For occurrence-bound classes, identity follows the distinct represented characterization/relation/orientation event at natural source grain. For support classes, identity follows materially differentiated support, not repeated wording.

Code owns canonical output IDs. Supply only neutral identity keys where requested. Order by first source establishment after coreference, subject to speaker-first PERSON and genuine broad-before-contained support rules.
