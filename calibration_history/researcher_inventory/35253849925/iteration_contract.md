## 1. Four-gate primitive test

Read the whole source before extraction. A primitive survives only if all four gates pass:

1. **Source support** — exact source wording/cue, except a supported unnamed PLACE/TIME anchor.
2. **Class job** — the item performs the semantic research job of that class, not merely a grammatical form associated with the class.
3. **Independent selectability** — a researcher could address it separately inside the represented map without needing the whole containing proposition to give it identity.
4. **Non-redundancy** — it is not an alias, repeated mention, proposition wrapper, support-grammar artifact, argument fragment, event restatement, or second projection with no separate class job.

Single-use coordinates are allowed. Local grammatical fragments are not admitted merely because they occur once and can be named.

## 2. Scene ledger before lexical classes

Before PERSON/OBJECT/LABEL/VERB/LOCATOR extraction, reconstruct the source-order scene ledger.

### PLACE

PLACE is a material setting or occurrence-position, not a spatial-word list.

Preserve:
- explicit broad and contained settings when both organize distinct represented material;
- supported unnamed settings when a distinct participant/object location, destination, waiting position, remembered scene, later interaction/conversation, prospective scene, or present-telling scene is materially represented but unnamed.

An unnamed PLACE may use `source_wording: null` and must use an exact source cue. Do not invent a place name.

Do not create PLACE for:
- surfaces, body parts, container edges, support points, path points, or movement endpoints that are merely local orientation detail;
- figurative or conceptual spatial wording whose actual job is OBJECT, LABEL, or LOCATOR;
- a concrete thing whose only job is thing identity.

A spatial noun or phrase is not automatically PLACE. Ask whether it establishes a scene in which material is represented. If it instead names a conceptual thing/domain, treat it under OBJECT; if it orients another relation, treat it under LOCATOR.

### TIME

TIME is a material event/episode frame that organizes multiple represented relations or marks a distinct stage in source progression.

Preserve material attempts/conditions, response/help episodes, departures/transitions, waits, later conversations/reports, remembered episodes, recurring spans, present telling/reflection, and prospective frames when they function as distinct scene-level temporal anchors.

Do not mint TIME for every predicate, question, contemplated action, subaction, local action phase, tense/aspect form, duration, adverb, descriptive clause, or lexical relation. Several relations commonly share one TIME.

An unnamed TIME may use `source_wording: null` and must use an exact source cue. Do not invent a time name.

## 3. PERSON

Retain `B` plus every materially represented human/social actor or stable group after coreference, including indirect, possessive, relational, offscreen, remembered, reported, prospective, and comparison actors when represented.

Resolve true aliases/coreference. Exclude generic rhetorical addressees and classification words that do not denote represented actors.

## 4. OBJECT

OBJECT is a materially represented independently selectable concrete or abstract thing.

Retain source-supported concrete wholes/parts, values/amounts, services/results, decisions/next steps, contemplated choices treated as things, stable relations/situations/plans, named categories/sets, and independently represented figurative/comparison referents.

A comparison vehicle or figurative noun-like referent may be OBJECT when the source represents it as a thing, even though it is not literally present in the scene.

Do not create OBJECT for:
- whole assertions or questions merely because they have content;
- reason/explanation clauses;
- event restatements already carried by TIME/VERB plus arguments;
- transient argument phrases with no independent thing-like identity;
- generic discourse placeholders when their only function is grammatical;
- a referent whose only source job is PLACE.

## 5. LABEL

LABEL is an independently selectable source-present characterization value: quality, state, evaluation, comparison, identity/classification, correction/rejection, polarity/status, experiential/epistemic description, or materially qualifying manner.

Use the shortest complete source-native value carrying the characterization. Preserve uncertainty, question, negation, intensity, correction, contrast, and idiom when they belong to that value.

A participial or verb-shaped word may be LABEL when its represented job is to characterize a person/object/state rather than assert an independently selectable event. A short questioned characterization and its explicit correction/rejection may each be LABEL when the discourse separately presents them as candidate and answer values.

Do not promote an entire proposition, question sentence, reason clause, or predicate packet into LABEL when a smaller source-present characterization value carries the job.

`qualities_available` remains separate from LABEL inventory.

## 6. VERB

VERB is a materially represented lexical relation/predicate increment.

Retain principal event/relation atoms and coordinated or embedded relations when each establishes its own represented relational job. Retain cognition, stance, report, recall, perception, intention, comparison, location, waiting/continuation, and other matrix relations only when they materially add relational, epistemic, modal, or attributional structure.

Use the smallest semantically complete exact source-native lexical construction. Remove ordinary subjects, objects, recipients, topics, and optional complements from VERB wording; they belong in compounds. Retain a particle, reflexive, negation, or complement only when it is lexically required to preserve the relation identity.

A participial/state form that is functioning only as a characterization belongs in LABEL rather than VERB. Copular/support packaging, auxiliaries, question wrappers, discourse wrappers, whole propositions, and every-parseable-subpredicate inflation are not VERB primitives.

## 7. LOCATOR

LOCATOR is a selected exact source-native orientation or context relation that independently positions a retained person, object, event, or relation.

Retain material physical or figurative position, containment, support, proximity, path, direction, origin/destination, accompaniment, recurrence/context, embodied/internal orientation, and source-native directional/adverbial constructions when the orientation/context itself is independently selectable.

A relation may legitimately split into a VERB relation atom plus a LOCATOR orientation when both jobs are separately represented. Prefer:
- VERB for the lexical action/relation identity;
- LOCATOR for separable direction, position, path, destination, recurrence, or contextual orientation.

If a particle/complement is inseparable from the lexical verb meaning, keep it with VERB and do not duplicate it as LOCATOR. If removing the orientation leaves the same lexical action but loses a separately selectable where/which-way/context relation, preserve the orientation as LOCATOR.

Use the smallest complete meaningful locating construction, not an isolated preposition. Recipients, topics, instruments, bare durations, discourse markers, causal/reason phrases, and generic complements are not LOCATOR merely because they are contextual or prepositional.

## 8. Class-precedence audit before overlap

For every candidate span, first choose the class that carries its primary represented research job. Only then ask whether another class also has an independently selectable job.

Use this precedence test:
- setting/scene identity -> PLACE;
- episode/frame identity -> TIME;
- actor identity -> PERSON;
- thing/relation-as-thing identity -> OBJECT;
- value/state/characterization -> LABEL;
- lexical action/relation -> VERB;
- orientation/path/context -> LOCATOR.

Cross-class overlap is conservative. The same source span may appear in more than one class only when removing either projection would erase a genuinely different class-specific coordinate. Technical defensibility or grammatical ambiguity is insufficient.

Do not project one local meaning across OBJECT/LABEL/VERB/LOCATOR merely because each analysis is possible.

## 9. Literal preservation lock

Every non-null `source_wording`, `source_cue`, non-null `order_cue`, and source-derived short tag must preserve source language exactly where the schema requires source text.

Never substitute synonyms, grammatical repairs, dialect normalization, spelling cleanup, number changes, expanded contractions, inferred terminology, or semantically convenient replacements.

Preserve questions, negation, uncertainty, correction, comparison, attribution, intention, recurrence, reported posture, prospective posture, figurative wording, punctuation, and dialect.

Only genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME coordinates may use `source_wording: null`, with exact source evidence in `source_cue`.

## 10. Ordering and qualities

Order each class by first source establishment after coreference, with speaker first in PERSON.

`qualities_available` is true only when source-present qualities/descriptions are available for that coordinate. It neither forces nor forbids a separate LABEL.

## 11. Primitive freeze

Before building compounds, run all class audits and freeze the primitive inventory.

Do not invent a primitive to make a compound easier. Do not keep a questionable primitive because a later bundle refers to it. Compounds are downstream of primitive identity.

## 12. Compound reconstruction

After primitive freeze, re-read the source in order and perform a relation-bearing scan.

For each retained VERB, LABEL, or LOCATOR that establishes a source-local binding, create exactly one materially complete compound for that binding, unless multiple relation-bearing primitives form one inseparable coordinated relation in the source.

Each compound must contain:
- the relation-bearing primitive(s);
- every frozen participant/object/value actually co-bound in that local semantic unit;
- the applicable PLACE/TIME frame when material to that binding;
- no coordinate that belongs only to a neighboring proposition or broader scene.

Compound coverage is exhaustive over retained relation-bearing bindings, but never combinatorial. Do not create pairwise subsets, nested partials, duplicate bundles, alternate decompositions, whole-scene supersets, or bundles for mere co-occurrence.

One sentence may yield several compounds when it contains several independently retained relation-bearing increments. Conversely, one coordinated relation may yield one compound when the relations are not independently represented.

A primitive may remain unbundled when the source does not bind it to another retained coordinate.

## 13. Final audits

**Scene audit:** recover materially distinct named/unnamed PLACE anchors, including participant/object location, destination, waiting, later interaction/conversation, and present telling; remove spatial-looking non-scenes.

**Frame audit:** keep only scene-level TIME anchors; remove predicate/action/question micro-times and bare durations that do not organize a distinct frame.

**Independent-selectability audit:** remove proposition restatements, support grammar, local argument fragments, duplicate mentions, and technically possible but nonfunctional cross-class projections. Preserve genuine single-use coordinates.

**Type audit:** resolve each primitive by its represented class job before overlap. Recheck participial states, figurative spatial wording, relation-vs-orientation splits, and correction/rejection values.

**OBJECT audit:** keep independently selectable concrete/abstract/figurative things; remove proposition and event-restatement objects.

**LABEL audit:** keep shortest complete characterization values, including source-staged uncertainty/negation/correction where independently represented; remove whole predicate/proposition duplicates.

**VERB audit:** keep smallest complete lexical relation increments; remove subjects/objects/wrappers/support packaging and characterization-only forms.

**LOCATOR audit:** keep independently selectable orientation/context increments; preserve separable direction/context around a relation, but do not duplicate inseparable phrasal-verb meaning.

**Literal audit:** compare every source-derived string character-for-character against the source and repair any normalization or invention.

**Compound audit:** every retained relation-bearing primitive is represented in its materially complete local binding when the source supplies one; no unmatched references, subsets, alternates, duplicates, or scene-wide inflation.

Then verify source order, qualities flags, candidate-only status, and holdout isolation.
