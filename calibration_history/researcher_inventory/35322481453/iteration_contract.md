## 1. Construction order — positive saturation first

Read the complete source before extracting any class.

Silently establish broad source order, coreference, and materially distinct scene/episode shifts. Then process each requested class independently using this sequence:

1. **POSITIVE COVERAGE** — scan the complete source for every source-supported coordinate that genuinely performs this class's own researcher-reconnectable job.
2. **GRAIN** — select the smallest complete source-native span/frame that preserves that job.
3. **TYPE** — confirm that the source function belongs to this class rather than relying on part of speech.
4. **CROSS-CLASS AUDIT** — preserve the same span in another class when the source gives it a genuinely different reconnectable job there.
5. **BOUNDED DEDUP/SUPPRESSION** — remove only the expressly allowed defect classes below.
6. **LITERAL LOCK** — verify exact source wording/cues.

Freeze all primitives only after every requested class has completed these passes. Build compounds afterward from the frozen primitive set.

Positive coverage is not a lexical census. Explicit mention alone does not force admission; low salience or one-time appearance alone does not justify suppression.

## 2. Bounded suppression rule

After positive coverage, suppress only when the candidate is one of:

- true alias/coreference duplicate of the same coordinate;
- pure auxiliary, determiner, conjunction, discourse glue, or other function grammar with no independent class job;
- bare preposition/path particle with no independent orientation job;
- duplicate same-grain restatement/tokenization of one lexical relation;
- analyst-created abstraction, paraphrase, inferred wording, or arbitrary subpart with no independent source identity;
- whole sentence/clause/question/proposition wrapper not treated by the source as an identifiable content, choice, plan, decision, relationship, situation, or object of thought;
- duplicate projection that performs the same researcher job rather than a genuinely different class-native job.

Do **not** suppress merely because a coordinate is background, local, incidental, scene furniture, one-use, nested, reported, remembered, prospective, figurative, questioned, negated, or unnamed. Test its class-native job first.

## 3. PLACE — positive coverage

PLACE is a physical/institutional setting or occurrence position represented by the source.

Retain every distinct source-supported PLACE job including:

- broad and contained physical/institutional settings when both are useful;
- positions of materially distinct interactions, waits, conversations, departures/arrivals, destinations, and another participant's represented scene;
- spatially staged object positions when the source makes the position itself reconnectable;
- remembered/reported/prospective/comparison scene settings;
- present-telling location when the source represents a current reporting setting;
- supported unnamed relationship/interaction/scene positions when the scene is distinct but the exact place is not named.

For genuinely unnamed PLACE use `source_wording: null` and an exact source cue.

Do not convert a concrete object into PLACE merely because it can contain/support something. Conversely, do not suppress a genuine occurrence position merely because its wording is deictic, relational, or also usable as LABEL/LOCATOR.

## 4. TIME — positive coverage

TIME is a materially distinct episode, phase, period, span, or frame organizing represented relations.

Retain every distinct source-supported TIME job including:

- attempts and action episodes;
- interactions/help/conversation episodes;
- waits and transition stages;
- remembered/reported scenes;
- recurring spans and stable periods;
- prospective/future frames;
- comparison/imagined frames when represented as a distinct episode/frame;
- present reflection/telling;
- date/time-of-day/duration frames when they locate a material episode or period.

Supported unnamed TIME is required when a materially distinct episode exists but no explicit temporal phrase names it.

Do not create TIME from every tense marker, `now`, `then`, frequency word, duration question, clause boundary, or action phrase. It must function as an independent episode/period/frame.

## 5. PERSON — positive coverage

Retain `B` plus every distinct represented human/social actor or stable group after true alias/coreference resolution.

Minor, possessive/relational, offscreen, remembered, reported, prospective, institutional, and comparison actors may qualify when source-established. Exclude only nonreferential grammatical/rhetorical addressees without represented social identity.

A social term may also appear as LABEL when the source independently uses it as characterization/classification.

## 6. OBJECT — positive coverage

OBJECT is an independently source-treated concrete or abstract referent/content.

Retain source-distinguished:

- physical things, products, documents, parts, scene items, and comparison vehicles;
- amounts/values;
- choices, decisions, plans, relationships, recurring situations, and mental contents when the source treats them as identifiable things/content;
- source-reified relations or courses of action;
- low-salience or one-off referents with independent source identity.

Do not suppress a concrete scene item solely because it supports/locates another thing; test whether the source also establishes it as an independent referent.

Reject pronoun/deictic/coreference duplicates, arbitrary noun fragments, and whole proposition/question/clause wrappers unless the source itself reifies that proposition/course of action/situation as identifiable content.

Do not duplicate a physical setting as OBJECT when its only researcher job is PLACE.

## 7. LABEL — positive coverage

LABEL is a source-staged value, state, quality, classification, evaluation, comparison, correction, rejection, identity, manner/state description, polarity response, or status.

Retain the shortest complete source-native phrase that carries the staged value and its posture. Preserve question, negation, uncertainty, correction, contrast, intensity, and attribution.

Do not create LABEL from every adjective/adverb or ordinary action predicate. A phrase may also be VERB/LOCATOR/PLACE when the source gives those genuinely different jobs.

## 8. VERB — positive coverage

VERB is a materially distinct content-bearing lexical relation/event at the shortest **complete** source-native grain.

Retain every materially represented lexical relation including movement, possession, experience, cognition, speech/reporting, perception, intention, comparison, waiting, stance, gesture, copular/locative relation, questioning, telling, finding, thinking, saying, and other predicates when independently represented.

Preserve required particles, reflexives, directional/relational complements, negation-bearing construction, or idiomatic material when removing them changes relation identity. Split coordinated/matrix/embedded relations only when the source represents genuinely distinct lexical relation jobs.

Suppress pure auxiliary/function grammar and true same-relation duplicates; do not suppress a relation for being low-salience or embedded.

## 9. LOCATOR — positive coverage

LOCATOR is a source-native orientation/context relation that independently situates or path-links represented material.

Retain materially distinct source-supported orientation jobs for:

- relative position;
- containment;
- direction/origin/destination;
- path/entry/exit/movement orientation;
- proximity/distance;
- accompaniment/carrying relation;
- recurrence/situational context;
- embodied/internal orientation;
- figurative/comparison orientation.

A bare preposition is insufficient. A movement or deictic phrase may legitimately also be VERB, PLACE, or LABEL when each projection answers a different reconnectable question.

## 10. Mandatory cross-class audit

Cross-class overlap is **not** exceptional. After each class pass, test each retained span/frame against these separate jobs:

- PERSON/OBJECT: what actor, referent, or content is involved?
- LABEL: what value/state/classification/evaluation is staged?
- VERB: what lexical relation/action occurs?
- LOCATOR: what orientation/path/context is supplied?
- PLACE: what physical/institutional scene or occurrence position is represented?
- TIME: what episode/period frame organizes it?

If the source independently supports more than one job, preserve the applicable projections. If the alternatives merely reflect grammatical parsing of one job, keep one.

Do not force a span into only one class merely to reduce overlap.

## 11. Literal preservation lock

Every non-null `source_wording`, every `source_cue`, every non-null `order_cue`, and every source-derived short tag must preserve source language character-for-character where the schema requires source text.

Never substitute synonyms, grammatical repairs, spelling cleanup, dialect normalization, number changes, contraction expansion, punctuation cleanup, inferred terminology, or semantically convenient replacements.

Only genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME frames may use `source_wording: null`, with exact source evidence in `source_cue`.

## 12. Qualities and order

`qualities_available` is boolean only. Set it true only when qualities/descriptions are available for that coordinate or compound. Question, negation, uncertainty, intensity, or the mere presence of a LABEL does not automatically make it true.

Order by first source establishment after coreference. Use speaker-first PERSON and broad-before-contained PLACE/LOCATOR only when the source establishes them together and the apparatus requires that navigation order.

## 13. Primitive freeze

Complete all class-native positive coverage, grain/type resolution, cross-class audit, bounded suppression, coreference, order, qualities, and literal lock **before** compounds.

Compounds may never create, add, delete, merge, retype, or repair primitives.

## 14. Compound reconstruction

After primitive freeze, scan the source in order for materially distinct represented relations/states.

For each relation/state that requires a compound, emit one smallest **materially complete local binding** using the frozen coordinates actually bound in that relation:

- operative VERB/LABEL/LOCATOR primitive(s);
- retained PERSON/OBJECT/value roles;
- useful applicable PLACE/TIME frame(s);
- existing schema posture for question/negation/uncertainty/prospectivity/attribution.

Standalone primitives need no compound. Do not emit pairwise closure, nested partial subsets, alternate tokenizations, one-compound-per-primitive, one-compound-per-mention, clause-support bundles, or scene-wide mega-bundles.

## 15. Final audit

Before returning:

1. **Coverage:** did every source-supported class-native coordinate receive a positive test before suppression?

3. **Grain:** is every retained span the smallest complete source-native grain for its job?
4. **Cross-class:** were genuinely different jobs preserved even when wording overlaps?
5. **Coreference:** were true aliases merged without losing independently staged projections?
6. **Literal lock:** are all source-derived strings exact?
7. **Compounds:** does each compound reconnect one materially distinct local relation using frozen coordinates only?
