## Mission

Produce only a literal lightweight Researcher Inventory candidate from the supplied source. Recover the source-present research-coordinate map at the source's natural represented grain, preserving lexical form, source order, local binding, and epistemic posture.

`Lightweight` is neither narrative summarization nor lexical census. Preserve represented people, things, values, relations, orientations, scenes, and episode anchors even when minor or unnamed; do not mint a coordinate from every phrase, clause, predicate token, modifier, preposition, or discourse shell.

Do not interpret psychological meaning, score APA, infer protected threads, draw conclusions, promote records, mint APA IDs, or admit anything to sovereign/production fabric. The worker never receives approved archetypes, expected counts, evaluator findings, prior scored outputs, sealed holdout source during calibration, or holdout outputs.

## 1. Reconstruct the represented map before extracting classes

Read the complete source first. Silently reconstruct in source order:

1. **Episode / scene ledger** — distinct represented situations, waits, transitions, remembered/reported episodes, recurring periods, prospective episodes, and the present-telling frame.
2. **Participant / referent ledger** — people, groups, concrete things, abstract/reified things, scene objects, values, choices, and other source-present referents.
3. **Binding ledger** — actions, relations, characterizations, corrections/rejections, reports, comparisons, intentions, orientations, and local co-bindings.
4. **Location / time anchors** — named or source-supported unnamed occurrence positions and temporal frames that locate those episodes or bindings.

The ledgers are internal planning devices. Do not output a lexical parse.

### 1.1 Episode-anchor completeness

Do not require an explicit location noun or time phrase before admitting PLACE/TIME. A supported unnamed PLACE or TIME is required when the source establishes a materially distinct interaction scene or action stage whose local bindings need their own occurrence position or temporal frame.

Use episode granularity, not clause granularity. Evidence for a distinct stage can include a meaningful change in participant configuration, setting, action regime, wait/transition state, remembered/reported scene, prospective scene, or present-telling frame. Do not create a new anchor for every sentence, clause, verb, or temporal adverb. Do not collapse all scenes into one global unnamed anchor when the source represents distinct stages.

## 2. Primitive admission test

A primitive survives only when all are true:

1. **Source grounding** — exact source wording/cue, except genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME with an exact source cue.
2. **Class job** — it performs the semantic research job of one of the seven classes.
3. **Represented-coordinate identity** — it is a distinct node, frame, value, relation, or orientation, not merely grammar or an arbitrary parse fragment.
4. **Natural grain** — it is the smallest semantically complete source-native expression of that coordinate: not an under-specified token, and not an unnecessarily broad proposition wrapper.
5. **Non-duplication** — after true coreference and role reconciliation, it is not merely an alias, support shell, or alternate decomposition of the same coordinate.

Do not add a narrative-importance or explicitness-entitlement gate. Minor/background coordinates can qualify; explicitly worded fragments do not qualify merely because they are explicit.

## 3. PLACE

PLACE is a represented physical setting or occurrence-position that locates an episode, participant/object configuration, destination, wait, remembered/reported interaction, prospective episode, or present telling.

Preserve broad and local/nested places when each supplies a distinct useful occurrence-position. Preserve supported unnamed places when the source clearly establishes a local scene or participant/object position without naming the place; use `source_wording: null` plus an exact source cue.

Do not create PLACE merely from a deictic word, preposition, surface, body part, direction token, or movement particle. A concrete thing whose primary job is thing identity is OBJECT, not automatically PLACE. Cross-class projection requires a genuinely separate place job.

## 4. TIME

TIME is a represented episode, period, transition-stage, wait, recurrence, remembered/reported frame, intended/prospective frame, or present-telling frame that organizes distinct bindings.

Preserve multiple local/nested TIME coordinates when the source distinguishes genuinely different interaction/action stages. Supported unnamed TIME is valid and sometimes necessary even without explicit temporal wording; use `source_wording: null` with exact source evidence.

A duration, sequence word, tense/aspect form, adverb, question about duration, or local predicate is evidence, not automatically a TIME primitive. Admit it only when it names or anchors a represented frame.

## 5. PERSON

Retain `B` plus every represented human/social actor or stable group after true coreference, including minor, indirect, possessive, relational, offscreen, remembered, reported, prospective, and comparison actors when the source represents that actor.

Exclude only rhetorical/generic addressees that do not denote a represented actor. For true aliases, preserve one coordinate and use the most stable source-native identity wording while ordering by first establishment.

## 6. OBJECT and discourse-shell dereferencing

OBJECT is a represented concrete or abstract thing/referent at natural grain. Preserve source-present scene objects and props, products, documents, surfaces/things, parts, values/amounts, internal/mental referents, plans, choices-as-things, stable relations/situations, categories/sets, reified concepts, and comparison referents when the source itself represents them as things.

Do not atomize every noun phrase into heads/modifiers/subparts. Do not turn a whole event/assertion into OBJECT merely because it can be nominalized.

### 6.1 Discourse shells are not automatic objects

Words such as `point`, `part`, `thing`, `fact`, `idea`, `issue`, or similar shells are not OBJECT merely because they grammatically refer to nearby content. If the shell only points to proposition/relation/choice content already represented elsewhere and has no stable independent referential identity downstream, dereference it: inventory the represented content in its proper classes and omit the shell object.

Retain a discourse-shell expression as OBJECT only when the source actually treats that shell/reified content as a stable thing with its own identity, recurrence, comparison, possession, or downstream binding.

## 7. LABEL

LABEL is a represented characterization, state, evaluation, identity/classification, comparison value, correction/rejection, status, experiential/epistemic description, or other source-staged value.

Preserve separately staged candidate/rejection/correction values and their question/negation/uncertainty posture. Use the shortest complete source-native value. Do not inventory every adjective, adverb, or noun modifier.

## 8. VERB — relation grain and complement separation

VERB is a represented lexical action/relation/predicate at its natural source-native grain.

Preserve distinct movement, possession, experience, cognition, speech/reporting, perception, intention, comparison, waiting, gesture, meta-telling, and other predicates when they establish their own represented relation.

Do not split one relation into every parseable predicate token. Include a particle, reflexive, directional element, or lexical complement when removing it would change or under-specify the relation itself.

**Do not absorb independently represented complement content merely to make the verb span longer.** When a predicate establishes a relation and its complement expresses a separate state, object, label, proposition-content, or subordinate event that qualifies independently, keep the relation at its natural lexical grain and represent the complement through its own qualifying coordinate(s) and binding. A broad predicate-plus-complement span is valid only when the source treats the whole expression as one lexically inseparable relation.

Coordinated actions may share a single VERB only when the source presents one inseparable action/result relation; otherwise preserve distinct relation coordinates.

## 9. LOCATOR — orientation, not prepositional grammar

LOCATOR is a complete source-native orientation/context relation that actually positions, contains, directs, originates, terminates, accompanies, situates, or otherwise locates a retained coordinate or binding.

Preserve physical and figurative position, containment, path, proximity, direction, origin/destination, accompaniment, embodied/internal orientation, and other genuine locating constructions.

A phrase is **not** LOCATOR merely because it begins with or contains `to`, `for`, `about`, `with`, `of`, `at`, or another preposition. Semantic complements such as beneficiaries, topics, recipients, purpose complements, self-directed arguments, possessive/association phrases, or ordinary verb arguments belong in the appropriate PERSON/OBJECT/LABEL/VERB binding unless they independently express where/which-way/from/to/relative-position orientation.

Movement particles or directional phrases that materially change action geometry may be LOCATOR when they independently encode orientation, and may coexist with VERB if the action and orientation are genuinely separate research jobs.

## 10. Class assignment and legitimate overlap

Choose class by represented semantic job:

- occurrence-setting / physical position -> PLACE;
- temporal episode / period / frame -> TIME;
- actor identity -> PERSON;
- thing / referent identity -> OBJECT;
- characterization / value / state -> LABEL;
- lexical action / relation -> VERB;
- orientation / path / relational position -> LOCATOR.

Cross-class overlap is conservative but **must not be suppressed when the same source material performs two independent represented jobs**. Ask whether each projection would remain a useful distinct research coordinate if the other projection disappeared. If yes, overlap can be correct; if one projection is merely grammatical reinterpretation or duplicate wording, keep only the real job.

## 11. Literal preservation lock

Every non-null `source_wording`, `source_cue`, non-null `order_cue`, and source-derived short tag must preserve source language character-for-character where the schema requires source text.

Never substitute synonyms, grammatical repairs, spelling cleanup, dialect normalization, number changes, expanded contractions, punctuation normalization, inferred terminology, or semantically convenient replacements. Preserve question, negation, uncertainty, correction, comparison, attribution, intention, recurrence, reported/prospective posture, and figurative wording.

Only genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME coordinates may use `source_wording: null`, with exact source evidence in `source_cue`.

## 12. Ordering, coreference, and qualities

Order each class by first source establishment after true coreference, with speaker first in PERSON.

Coreference collapses aliases into one coordinate; it must not discard a minor actor/object because the first mention is possessive, figurative, descriptive, or indirect. A later clearer source-native identity may name the coordinate while order remains tied to first establishment.

`qualities_available` is true only when source-present qualities/descriptions are available for that coordinate. It neither forces nor forbids a separate LABEL.

## 13. Primitive freeze: coverage pass, then anti-census pass

Before compounds, perform two separate passes.

### Coverage pass
Check for omitted minor actors, scene objects/props, broad/local places, supported unnamed occurrence positions, distinct episode frames, source-staged values, minor/reporting/gesture actions, and meaningful orientations. Explicitly ask whether each distinct interaction/action stage has adequate PLACE/TIME anchoring even when no location/time noun is named.

### Anti-census pass
Remove temporal/deictic cue tokens that do not define a frame; arbitrary noun subparts; modifier inflation; support/auxiliary/aspect fragments; discourse-shell objects without independent identity; proposition wrappers; prepositional complements without orientation job; alias duplicates; and cross-class duplicates without separate semantic jobs.

Freeze the resulting primitive inventory. Compounds may not repair or expand it.

## 14. Canonical local compound reconstruction

After primitive freeze, re-read the source in order and rebuild the binding ledger. Compounds represent source bindings, not primitive co-occurrence and not every syntactic clause.

For each distinct represented binding, create one materially complete local compound containing:

- the relation-bearing VERB/LABEL/LOCATOR primitive(s) that define the binding;
- every frozen PERSON/OBJECT/value coordinate actually co-bound in it;
- the applicable PLACE and TIME episode anchors when they locate that binding;
- preserved question/negation/uncertainty/attribution posture through the schema.

Several relation-bearing primitives may share one compound when the source presents one inseparable binding. Split only independently selectable bindings. Do not emit pairwise subsets, nested partial duplicates, alternate decompositions, or giant scene-wide bundles. Do not omit applicable unnamed episode anchors simply because they are implicit once the episode ledger is built.

## 15. Final audits

**Coverage audit:** no narrative-importance pruning; check minor/background/one-off coordinates.

**Episode audit:** every materially distinct interaction/action stage has source-supported PLACE/TIME anchoring where the represented map requires it; no one-anchor-per-clause inflation and no global-anchor collapse.

**Discourse-shell audit:** no generic shell OBJECT unless the shell has independent stable referential identity.

**Type/overlap audit:** classify by semantic job; do not suppress a valid second independent job and do not duplicate for grammatical ambiguity.

**VERB audit:** relation span is semantically complete but does not swallow independently represented complement content.

**LOCATOR audit:** every locator expresses genuine orientation/location, not merely prepositional grammar.

**Literal audit:** every source-derived string matches source text character-for-character.

**Compound audit:** one canonical source binding -> one complete compound with all and only co-bound frozen coordinates plus applicable episode anchors; no unmatched refs, alternate subsets, or scene-wide inflation.

Then verify source order, coreference, qualities flags, candidate-only status, and holdout isolation.
