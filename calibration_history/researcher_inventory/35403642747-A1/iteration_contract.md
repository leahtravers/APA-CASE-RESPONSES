## Mission

Produce only the literal lightweight Researcher Inventory candidate requested by the schema.

Recover the source's **bounded reconnectable research coordinates at class-native grain**, then bind those coordinates into the smallest useful local compounds. The inventory is broader than a summary and narrower than a token, grammar, predicate, preposition, clause, or modifier census.

Do not interpret psychological meaning, score APA, infer protected threads, draw conclusions, promote records, mint APA IDs, or write sovereign/admitted APA data.

## 1. Construction order

Read the whole source before extracting any class.

### Pass A — whole-source semantic ledger

Silently map the source in order. For every materially represented scene, episode, actor, referent, relation, value/state, choice/decision, recurring relation, comparison, and orientation, decide how its source material will be accounted for.

The ledger is a coverage device, not output. A detail may be accounted for without becoming a primitive.

### Pass B — primitive entitlement

For the requested class, admit only coordinates that satisfy the bounded class-native test below. Consider low-salience, nested, one-use, remembered, reported, prospective, negative, uncertain, figurative, and background material; none is excluded merely for being subtle.

### Pass C — dominant projection and shadow audit

For each admitted span, ask what research job it primarily performs. Do not create TIME merely because wording is temporal, LOCATOR merely because a preposition exists, VERB merely because a lexical predicate exists, LABEL merely because a modifier/evaluation exists, or OBJECT merely because a noun phrase can be named.

Cross-class overlap is exceptional. Allow it only when the same source wording independently supplies two genuinely different class-native research handles. If one projection already preserves the lightweight research access, suppress the shadow projection.

### Pass D — canonical grain and primitive freeze

Resolve true coreference, source order, exact literal wording, type, qualities, support frames, duplicates, and grain. Freeze primitives.

### Pass E — selective compounds

Build only materially useful multi-coordinate bindings from frozen primitives. A primitive may remain unbundled. Do not create a compound merely because a proposition or predicate can be named.

## 2. Bounded admission test

A standalone primitive qualifies only when at least one route applies.

### Route A — reconnectable source-native handle

The source gives the candidate enough identity that a later researcher can select and reconnect to it as a distinct item of that class without reconstructing it merely from grammar, a neighboring retained primitive, or a broader canonical binding.

### Route B — necessary support frame

An unnamed PLACE or TIME is required to keep a materially distinct represented interaction, wait, report, remembered scene, comparison scene, present-telling/reflection scene, recurring period, or prospective frame from collapsing into another occurrence context.

### Route C — distinct relation/value/orientation edge

For VERB, LABEL, or LOCATOR only: the source stages a relation, value/state, or orientation that remains a distinct lightweight research edge after its participants and broader scene/frame are known. It must do more than provide grammatical scaffolding or phrase-internal detail.

### Negative check — accounted for but not primitive

Reject standalone admission when the candidate is adequately preserved and adds no distinct class-native research access because it is only:

- an alias, pronoun/deictic repeat, or alternate tokenization;
- auxiliary, control, aspect, infinitival, copular, reporting, or other support grammar whose research work is carried by a larger admitted relation/state;
- a phrase-internal preposition, recipient/beneficiary phrase, ownership phrase, or contextual complement that does not remain a distinct orientation handle;
- a temporal/frequency/sequencing fragment that qualifies another episode rather than defining a distinct episode/period;
- an adjective, degree phrase, comparison fragment, or manner phrase already carried by a retained source-native value/state or quality;
- an unreified event/proposition/clause shell;
- a local lexical fragment whose complete relation is better represented as one source-native relation handle;
- a cross-class shadow of an already admitted coordinate;
- metadiscourse that only organizes the telling;
- duplicate information fully carried in a canonical compound or an admitted primitive's exact source phrase/cue.

Low salience alone is never a reason to reject a real research handle.

## 3. PLACE — scene and occurrence position

PLACE represents physical/institutional settings and occurrence positions at scene/interaction scale.

Retain explicit broad and contained settings when each supplies distinct navigation. Reconstruct a supported unnamed PLACE (`source_wording: null`, exact `source_cue`) when a materially distinct interaction, wait, report, remembered scene, comparison scene, object-location scene, or present telling/reflection has an occurrence position that would otherwise be lost or collapsed.

Do not turn every spatial noun, body part, path, distance, water mention, object position, community descriptor, deictic, or figurative spatial phrase into PLACE. Type those by their actual research job.

## 4. TIME — episode and period frame

TIME represents reconnectable episodes, periods, transitions, recurring spans, remembered/reported frames, prospective frames, relationship periods, comparison frames, and present-telling/reflection frames.

A local action is not automatically a TIME. Frequency words, durations, tense, sequencing phrases, temporal locators, and subordinate clauses stay inside a broader frame unless the source stages them as a distinct episode/period that a later researcher could independently revisit.

Reconstruct supported unnamed TIME (`source_wording: null`, exact `source_cue`) when a distinct represented frame exists without a separately named temporal phrase.

## 5. PERSON

Retain `B` plus every distinct represented human/social actor or stable group after true coreference when the source gives that actor a distinguishable identity or participant/relational role.

Minor, possessive/relational, offscreen, remembered, reported, prospective, and institutional actors may qualify. Suppress only true aliases and nonreferential/rhetorical addressees.

## 6. OBJECT — source-reified referents

OBJECT is a source-established concrete or abstract referent/content coordinate with independent identity.

Retain physical things, products, documents, parts, amounts/values, choices, decisions, plans, recurring relations/practices, relationships, represented sets/categories, comparison vehicles, and explicitly reified mental/content objects when the source treats them as things a researcher can reconnect to.

A recurring practice or relation can be OBJECT when the source treats that stable relation as the content/object of another concern, rather than as a mere episode frame.

Do not manufacture OBJECTs by nominalizing events, clauses, intentions, questions, or relations that the source does not itself reify. Do not admit every noun phrase or grammatical complement. Do not duplicate a setting as OBJECT when its only job is PLACE.

## 7. LABEL — staged value/state/posture

LABEL is a separately staged source value, state, classification, evaluation, comparison, correction/rejection, identity, manner, status, or explicitly questioned candidate value.

Use the shortest complete source-native span that preserves the actual posture. Preserve negation, uncertainty, correction, contrast, and question form when they belong to the value itself.

Do not merge successive source-staged postures merely because they concern one topic. A questioned value and its explicit rejection/affirmation may remain distinct value handles when the source stages them separately.

Do not turn every adjective, degree phrase, temporal phrase, relation, cognition, or proposition into LABEL. Ordinary description may remain `qualities_available` without a LABEL primitive.

## 8. VERB — admitted relation before lexical splitting

VERB represents a separately staged content-bearing relation/happening that remains useful as a research edge after participants and broader context are known.

**Admission precedes splitting.** Do not inventory every lexical predicate, control verb, embedded verb, reporting verb, copula, or motion fragment merely because it appears in the sentence.

First identify the complete source-native relation handle. Preserve particles, reflexives, negation, modal posture, and required complements when removing them changes relation identity. Then split only when neighboring predicates create genuinely different independently reconnectable relations with different research work, participants, targets, or states.

Suppress pure auxiliaries, do-support, empty copulas, aspect/control scaffolding, generic reporting/telling scaffolding, redundant identity copulas already carried by a LABEL, and fragments whose only role is to support another admitted relation.

Ordinary or one-use relations may still qualify; the test is distinct research-edge identity, not dramatic importance.

## 9. LOCATOR — independent orientation handle

LOCATOR is a source-native orientation handle that materially tells where, from where, toward where, through what, inside/outside what, with/along what, relative to what context, or in what represented comparative/figurative orientation an admitted coordinate/relation is staged.

Retain position, containment, origin/destination, direction/path, proximity/distance, movement-course, accompaniment/carrying, recurring-context, and figurative/comparative orientation when that orientation remains independently queryable after broad PLACE/TIME and participant/referent coordinates are known.

A locator may be a larger source-native orientation phrase rather than a bare preposition. Prefer the smallest complete phrase that performs the orientation job.

Do not inventory every preposition, recipient phrase, temporal adverb, ownership/provenance phrase, discourse connective, or phrase-internal complement. If the orientation is fully carried by the admitted relation/compound and adds no distinct handle, keep it accounted for there rather than creating a LOCATOR primitive.

## 10. Literal preservation and semantic accounting

Every source-derived non-null `source_wording`, `source_cue`, `order_cue`, and source-near tag must preserve the source's actual language where the schema requires source text. Do not repair grammar, normalize dialect, substitute synonyms, change numbers, expand contractions, or clean punctuation.

Literal preservation does not require atomizing every literal token into its own primitive. Source material that is not independently admitted remains preserved in the exact source phrase/cue or canonical bundle that carries it.

Only genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME support frames may use `source_wording: null`, anchored by exact source text.

## 11. Coreference, order, qualities

Merge true coreferents even when wording changes. Do not split aliases to inflate inventory.

Order by first source establishment after true coreference, with speaker-first PERSON and broad-before-contained setting navigation where source structure supports it.

`qualities_available` is boolean only. It signals source-present descriptive material; it does not itself force a LABEL.

## 12. Primitive freeze ledger

Before compounds, silently audit each material source item against two different questions:

1. **ACCOUNTED FOR?** Is its literal/semantic contribution preserved somewhere in the candidate representation?
2. **PRIMITIVE ENTITLED?** Does it independently satisfy the class-native admission test?

Every material item should be accounted for. Only entitled items become primitives. Never use the first question as proof of the second.

## 13. Compound reconstruction — selective canonical bindings

After primitive freeze, build only the smallest materially useful local multi-coordinate bindings.

A compound qualifies when at least two frozen primitives participate in one reconnectable source-local relation/state/orientation and their binding adds research value beyond the primitives viewed separately.

Use the admitted relation/value/orientation anchor and participating PERSON/OBJECT coordinates plus only the PLACE/TIME/LOCATOR coordinates materially needed to situate that binding.

Multiple lexical relations may share one compound only when the source presents one inseparable course. Otherwise use the single admitted relation handle that best represents the binding; do not create alternate subset/superset versions.

Do not emit:

- one compound for every proposition or sentence;
- one compound for every primitive;
- pairwise closure;
- nested subset variants;
- alternate tokenizations;
- grammatical-support bundles;
- broad scene mega-bundles;
- duplicate compounds;
- compounds containing primitives that do not belong to the same local binding.

A compound cannot repair a missing primitive and cannot justify admitting an otherwise non-entitled primitive.
