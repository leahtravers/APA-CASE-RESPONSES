## 1. Source-coordinate admission rule

Read the whole source before extraction. A primitive survives when all of the following hold:

1. it is source-supported;
2. it expresses a source-distinguished semantic coordinate with a real research lookup or binding job in its class;
3. retaining it preserves a distinction that would otherwise disappear from the inventory; and
4. it is not merely an alias, duplicate surface mention, auxiliary-only fragment, punctuation/discourse filler, or mechanically generated token.

A coordinate does **not** need to recur, be useful outside its original proposition, or remain independent of every local binding. Single-use but source-distinguished event, relation, orientation, characterization, option, content, or frame coordinates may be archetypally necessary.

Do not use grammatical category alone to admit or reject a coordinate. Type by semantic research job.

## 2. Coverage before compression

Use a two-pass discipline:

### Pass A — source-distinction coverage

Walk the source in order and identify each source-distinguished scene/frame, represented actor, stable concrete or abstract target, characterization, relation/predicate, and orientation/context relation that carries separate semantic information.

### Pass B — bounded compression

Remove only true aliases/coreference duplicates, purely grammatical support, duplicate projections that have no separate class job, and mechanical micro-fragments. Do not compress two source-distinguished coordinates merely because they occur in the same scene, clause, or event.

When unsure between omitting a source-distinguished coordinate and retaining it, retain it if omission would erase a distinct semantic lookup/binding axis. Lightweight is achieved by rejecting mechanical census items, not by deleting represented distinctions.

## 3. PLACE

PLACE records source-distinguished physical or represented settings/scene anchors.

Retain named settings and supported unnamed scene anchors when they organize a distinct interaction, remembered/reported scene, participant/object whereabouts, comparison scene, prospective setting, or represented current setting.

Co-located or nested settings may both survive when the source distinguishes their scene jobs. Do not create PLACE for every surface, body part, container edge, path point, or movement endpoint unless the source treats it as a distinct represented setting rather than mere orientation detail.

A concrete referent may also be OBJECT when thing identity and setting identity perform separate research jobs.

## 4. TIME

TIME records source-distinguished temporal/event frames, including materially distinct subepisodes within one broader scene.

Retain, when source-supported and semantically distinct:

- event attempts and action episodes;
- help/intervention episodes;
- departures, waits, returns, or other event-state changes;
- intended or counterfactual periods;
- remembered/reported episodes;
- recurring relationship or activity frames;
- prospective/future similar situations;
- present telling/reflection frames.

Do not collapse distinct source-staged subepisodes merely because they share a location or happen consecutively. Conversely, do not mint a TIME for every verb, tense marker, adverb, or duration when no separate event/frame distinction exists.

## 5. PERSON

Retain `B` and every source-distinguished represented human/social actor or stable group after coreference, including indirect, possessive, relational, offscreen, remembered, reported, prospective, and comparison actors.

Resolve true aliases/coreference. Exclude generic rhetorical addressees and classification words that do not denote represented actors.

## 6. OBJECT

OBJECT records source-distinguished concrete things and stable abstract/content targets that can be referred to, chosen, related to, evaluated, remembered, decided, or acted upon.

Retain, where source-supported, concrete objects plus stable abstract referents such as choices/options, decisions, relations, situations, plans, represented content, internal/figurative targets, and other thing-like semantic objects. A coordinate may be single-use; recurrence is not required.

Reject only true pronoun/coreference duplicates, generic class nouns with no represented referent, purely grammatical complements, and nominalizations that add no distinct target beyond an already retained relation and its arguments.

## 7. LABEL

LABEL records exact source-present characterizations: qualities, states, evaluations, comparisons, identity/classifications, corrections/rejections, polarity/status, experiential or epistemic descriptions, and materially qualifying manners.

Retain the smallest complete source-native span that preserves the characterization's full posture. LABEL may be verbal, clausal, idiomatic, locative-looking, or evaluative in surface grammar when its semantic job is characterization.

Preserve uncertainty, question form, negation, intensity, correction, contrast, attribution, and idiom when they belong to the value. Do not reduce a source characterization to an adjective head if the larger phrase is what carries the represented value.

Reject only incidental decorative modifiers, discourse filler, and duplicate labels with no distinct characterization job.

## 8. VERB

VERB records exact source-native relation/predicate coordinates.

Retain source-distinguished relations that establish action, perception, cognition, stance, speech/report, possession, location, intention, evaluation, change, causation, comparison, waiting/continuation, or other represented relational structure.

A relation is not excluded merely because it is a cognition/reporting/stance verb, a copular/location relation, or part of an embedded construction. If it contributes a distinct represented relation, retain it.

Choose the smallest **semantically complete** source-native relation span. Keep particles, infinitival material, complements, negation, or other wording inside the VERB when removing them would change the relation identity expressed by the source. Do not atomize a complete relation into a bare verb head if the archetypal relation is the larger source-present packet.

Exclude only auxiliary-only morphology, duplicate relation mentions/coreference, and support wording that contributes no separate semantic relation.

## 9. LOCATOR

LOCATOR records exact source-native orientation/context relations that position a person, object, event, or relation.

Retain material physical and figurative orientation, including position, containment, proximity, path, direction, origin, destination, approach/departure, arrival/exit, accompaniment, recurrence/context, embodied/internal position, and other source-distinguished locating relations.

LOCATOR may overlap VERB or LABEL wording when the same source span genuinely performs a separate orientation job. Movement expressions and state expressions may therefore project into both classes when each class job is real.

Use the smallest semantically complete source-native locating span. Reject only incidental prepositional fragments, duplicate aliases, and context wording that adds no locating/orienting distinction.

## 10. Cross-class projection

Cross-class overlap is permitted whenever one exact source span performs two different semantic jobs. Do not prefer deduplication across classes over preservation of distinct type assignments.

Examples of legitimate general patterns include a movement phrase functioning both as a relation and as direction/orientation, or a state phrase functioning both as a characterization and a located/embodied condition. The test is separate class job, not surface-form uniqueness.

Within a class, however, resolve aliases and duplicate mentions of the same coordinate.

## 11. Literal preservation lock

Every non-null `source_wording`, every `source_cue`, and every non-null `order_cue` must be a character-for-character contiguous source substring. Never substitute researcher tags, synonyms, grammatical repairs, normalized dialect, number changes, expanded contractions, or inferred wording.

Only genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME coordinates may use `source_wording: null`, with an exact source cue. Preserve spelling, punctuation, dialect, contractions, number, questions, negation, uncertainty, correction, comparison, attribution, intention, recurrence/report/prospective posture, and performance wording.

## 12. Ordering and qualities

Order each class by first source establishment after coreference, with speaker first in PERSON.

`qualities_available` is true only when source-present qualities/descriptions are available for that coordinate. Quality availability does not force or forbid a separate LABEL.

## 13. Compound reconstruction — one complete local proposition, no subset inflation

Freeze primitives only after the coverage and compression passes. Then re-read the source in order.

For each source-distinguished proposition, attribution, characterization, question, position, comparison, decision, intention, or event relation that connects retained coordinates, create one **maximally complete local compound** containing all retained coordinates materially co-bound in that semantic unit, including its relevant scene/frame coordinate(s).

Do not omit a retained coordinate merely because it shares wording with another class. If both class projections are materially active in the local semantic unit, both may participate.

Do not create nested partials, pairwise subsets, alternate decompositions, duplicate bundles, or scene-wide supersets merely because the same proposition can be represented several ways. One source semantic unit gets one complete compound unless the source itself establishes separately useful bindings through changed actor, target, relation, attribution, question, orientation, or frame.

A primitive may remain unbundled when the source does not bind it to another retained coordinate.

## 14. Final audits

**Coverage audit:** reread every clause/source segment and ask whether a source-distinguished semantic coordinate has disappeared solely because it was single-use or local. Restore it if omission erased a real class-specific lookup/binding axis.

**TIME audit:** preserve distinct event/subepisode frames while rejecting mere tense/adverb/action-token census.

**OBJECT audit:** preserve stable concrete and abstract targets/options/relations/situations/content; resolve only true duplicates and nonreferential grammar.

**LABEL audit:** preserve complete characterization posture, including verbal/idiomatic/clausal values when that is their semantic job.

**VERB audit:** preserve complete source-native relation identities, including cognition/stance/report/location/intention where semantically distinct; reject only auxiliary-only/support-only fragments.

**LOCATOR audit:** preserve complete physical/figurative orientation relations, including movement/position expressions even when they also project as VERB/LABEL.

**Cross-class audit:** do not erase a valid projection merely to avoid overlap.

**Compound audit:** one maximally complete local source semantic unit, no subset/alternate decomposition inflation.

**Literal audit:** compare every source-derived emitted string character-for-character against the source; repair any normalization before return.

Then verify source order, type assignment, qualities flags, compound references, and candidate-only boundaries.
