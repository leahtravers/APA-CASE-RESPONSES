## Mission

Produce only a literal lightweight Researcher Inventory candidate from the supplied source.

Recover the source's **represented research graph by binding-first entitlement**. First identify materially distinct represented propositions/relations and episode frames. Then emit only the primitives that those bindings and frames actually require, plus rare standalone coordinates whose independent identity is source-established.

Do not start from every noun, adjective, predicate, preposition, scene detail, or temporal cue and prune later. **Mention is not entitlement.**

Do not interpret psychological meaning, score APA, infer protected threads, draw conclusions, promote records, mint APA IDs, or admit anything to sovereign/production fabric. The worker never receives approved archetypes, expected counts, evaluator findings, prior scored outputs, sealed holdout source during calibration, or holdout outputs.

## 1. Binding-first construction

Read the complete source before extraction.

Silently create, in source order:

1. **Episode ledger** — materially distinct interaction/action scenes, waits, transitions, remembered/reported scenes, recurring frames, prospective frames, and present-telling frame only when the source represents them distinctly.
2. **Proposition ledger** — materially distinct source-represented relations or states that a researcher could later reconnect: who/what does, is, has, moves, says, perceives, thinks, compares, chooses, rejects, waits, relates, or is oriented to what.
3. **Role ledger** — the independent semantic participants/contents/values/orientations required by each selected proposition.
4. **Anchor ledger** — the PLACE/TIME positions needed to locate selected propositions or preserve materially distinct episode boundaries.

Do not create primitive candidates until the proposition ledger exists.

### 1.1 Proposition selection test

Select a proposition only when the source represents a distinct relation/state whose loss would erase a recoverable part of the represented situation.

Do not select a proposition merely because a clause or predicate token exists.

Suppress:

- auxiliary/aspect/support grammar;
- generic narration or discourse-management shells whose act is not itself represented as a material interaction;
- modifier-only relations already contained in a retained referent/value;
- duplicate paraphrases of the same local relation;
- grammatical support that introduces no independent represented edge.

Minor or one-off relations may still qualify. Narrative importance is not the test.

## 2. Primitive entitlement rule

A primitive may be emitted only if at least one entitlement is true:

### A. Binding-role entitlement

The primitive fills an independent semantic role in a selected proposition: actor, affected/related referent, represented content, relation edge, staged value/state, or genuine orientation.

### B. Episode-anchor entitlement

The primitive is a PLACE or TIME needed to locate one or more selected propositions or to preserve a materially distinct episode/frame.

### C. Standalone-coordinate entitlement

The source establishes independent identity outside a single grammatical phrase through recurrence/coreference, later reference, explicit reification, possession/evaluation, comparison, choice/decision treatment, stable relational content, or another represented relation. Such a coordinate may be valid even when no dedicated compound is required.

If none applies, suppress the candidate even if it is explicit, concrete, vivid, grammatically complete, or easy to name.

### 2.1 Removal test

Before freezing a primitive, mentally remove it.

If every selected proposition, episode distinction, independently represented referent, staged value, and genuine orientation remains reconstructable without loss, the primitive is not entitled and must be suppressed.

This test is about represented structure, not narrative importance.

## 3. One-job arbitration and duplicate control

A source span/referent normally receives the single class demanded by its role in the represented graph.

A second projection is allowed only when the source gives it a genuinely independent second research job in a different relation or coordinate structure.

Do not duplicate merely because grammar supports more than one label.

Tie-breaking rules:

- a physical setting or occurrence position used to locate a binding is PLACE, not duplicate OBJECT;
- a temporal frame that scopes bindings is TIME, not duplicate OBJECT/LABEL;
- a nominalized/reified course of action, situation, relationship, comparison vehicle, or mental content may be OBJECT when the source treats it as content with independent identity;
- a value directly asserted/questioned/rejected/compared of a retained coordinate is LABEL;
- a lexical action/relation that carries a selected proposition is VERB;
- an orientation relation that genuinely positions/path-links retained material is LOCATOR;
- a noun/complement inside a lexical relation does not become OBJECT unless it has independent role entitlement outside merely completing that relation.

## 4. PLACE

PLACE is a physical setting or occurrence-position that locates selected propositions or materially distinct episode frames.

After selecting propositions, backchain each to its applicable physical position.

Retain a separate PLACE only when replacing it with the broader available setting would lose a distinct represented position of an episode, wait, interaction, destination, object configuration, remembered/reported scene, prospective scene, or present telling.

Supported unnamed PLACE is allowed when the source establishes a distinct position but does not name it. Use `source_wording: null` and an exact source cue.

Do not create PLACE from every surface noun, deictic, body part, movement particle, container word, prepositional phrase, or imagined destination.

## 5. TIME

TIME is a represented frame that scopes selected propositions: episode, period, wait, transition stage, recurrence, remembered/reported frame, prospective frame, or present-telling frame.

Retain a separate TIME only when it groups or distinguishes one or more selected propositions from another materially distinct frame.

Supported unnamed TIME is allowed when a distinct episode exists without explicit temporal wording.

Do not create TIME from physical location phrases, every clause, every transition word, adverb, tense/aspect marker, duration question, intended action, or predicate merely because it occurs in sequence.

## 6. PERSON

Retain `B` plus every source-represented human/social actor or stable group after true coreference when that actor participates in a selected proposition or has source-established independent identity.

This may include minor, possessive, offscreen, remembered, reported, prospective, relational, or comparison actors.

Exclude rhetorical/generic addressees without represented actor identity. Merge true aliases/coreference into one coordinate.

## 7. OBJECT

OBJECT is a concrete or abstract referent with independent source-established identity.

Strong entitlement includes being an argument/content of a selected proposition, being reidentified/coreferred later, being explicitly possessed/evaluated/compared/selected, or being treated as a stable choice, decision, relationship, recurring situation, mental content, plan, amount, set, document/product/part, or comparison referent.

A one-off scene noun is not automatically an OBJECT merely because it is visible or concrete. A scene prop survives only when the source gives it a represented role or independent coordinate identity under Section 2.

Suppress:

- grammatical noun phrases with no independent role;
- surfaces/containers used only as PLACE support;
- relation-internal complements with no independent identity;
- discourse shells such as generic `thing`, `part`, `point`, `fact`, or similar wrappers when only nearby content matters;
- aliases and alternate decompositions;
- arbitrary subparts not independently represented.

When a verbal/relational phrase is explicitly treated as content — weighed, chosen, lacked control over, intended, avoided, remembered, compared, or recurrently invoked — it may be OBJECT in addition to a distinct VERB relation if both jobs independently survive the removal test.

## 8. LABEL

LABEL is a source-staged value/state/classification/evaluation/comparison/correction/rejection/status.

It must be meaningfully asserted, questioned, rejected, compared, or foregrounded as a value of a retained coordinate or proposition.

A descriptive adjective/adverb or colorful phrase embedded in ordinary narration is not automatically LABEL. Noun-modifying texture survives only when the source separately stages that characterization as a research value.

Preserve exact source posture: question, negation, uncertainty, correction, comparison, attribution, recurrence.

Use the shortest complete source-native value that preserves that posture.

## 9. VERB

VERB is the lexical relation edge that carries a selected proposition.

Choose one source-native relation at the smallest complete lexical grain. Include required particles/reflexives/directional or idiomatic material only when removing it changes relation identity. Do not absorb entire clausal complements merely because they follow the verb.

Retain movement, possession, experience, cognition, staged speech/reporting, perception, intention, comparison, waiting, gesture, and other relations when they are selected propositions.

Suppress auxiliaries, support/aspect fragments, duplicate local restatements, generic narration/report shells whose reporting act is not itself a selected interaction, and predicate tokens that contribute no independent proposition.

## 10. LOCATOR

LOCATOR is a source-native orientation relation that independently positions or path-links retained material: relative position, containment, direction, origin/destination, path, proximity, accompaniment, embodied/internal orientation, or comparable locating relation.

A LOCATOR normally connects a retained coordinate/binding to a distinguishable orientation. Mere prepositional grammar is not enough.

Beneficiary, topic, recipient, purpose, possession/association, evidentiary source, ordinary idiom argument, or lexical complement is not LOCATOR unless it independently performs an orientation job that survives the removal test.

A movement relation may have both VERB and LOCATOR only when action and orientation are independently represented jobs.

## 11. Literal preservation lock

Every non-null `source_wording`, `source_cue`, non-null `order_cue`, and source-derived short tag must preserve source language character-for-character where the schema requires source text.

Never substitute synonyms, grammatical repairs, spelling cleanup, dialect normalization, number changes, expanded contractions, punctuation normalization, inferred terminology, or semantically convenient replacements.

Only genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME coordinates may use `source_wording: null`, with exact source evidence in `source_cue`.

## 12. Primitive freeze sequence

Perform these passes in this order:

1. **Binding-role coverage** — each selected proposition has every independently represented actor/referent/value/relation/orientation required to reconstruct it.
2. **Anchor backchain** — each selected proposition is assigned the most specific applicable PLACE/TIME frame, adding supported unnamed anchors only where a distinct episode/position requires them.
3. **Standalone-coordinate check** — admit only independently source-established coordinates satisfying Section 2C.
4. **One-job arbitration** — resolve duplicate class projections by represented role.
5. **Removal test** — suppress every primitive whose deletion loses no represented proposition, episode distinction, independent referent, staged value, or genuine orientation.
6. **Literal lock** — verify every source-derived string.

Freeze primitives before compounds. Compounds may never repair or add primitives.

## 13. Canonical compound reconstruction

Generate compounds directly from the selected proposition ledger, not from the primitive list.

For each selected proposition:

1. include its defining VERB/LABEL/LOCATOR relation primitive(s);
2. include every frozen PERSON/OBJECT/value coordinate that fills an independent role in that proposition;
3. include the most specific applicable PLACE/TIME anchor(s) needed to preserve its episode/position;
4. preserve question/negation/uncertainty/attribution posture through the existing schema;
5. emit one materially complete local compound.

Do not create a compound merely because a primitive exists. Standalone scene/environment/reified coordinates may remain primitives without dedicated compounds.

Do not emit pairwise subsets, nested partial duplicates, alternate decompositions, one-compound-per-token, clause-wide support bundles, or scene-wide mega-bundles.

Every compound must map to exactly one selected proposition; every proposition gets at most one canonical compound unless the schema requires a separate posture representation that cannot coexist.

## 14. Final entitlement audit

For every emitted primitive, answer internally:

`Which selected proposition, necessary episode anchor, or standalone-identity rule entitles this coordinate?`

If there is no precise answer, delete the primitive.

For every compound, answer internally:

`Which one selected proposition does this compound serialize?`

If there is no precise answer, delete the compound.

Then verify source order, coreference, type arbitration, qualities flags, literal strings, candidate-only status, and holdout isolation.
