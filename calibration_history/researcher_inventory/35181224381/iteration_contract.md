## 1. V64 controlling model: complete scenes, compressed primitives

Read the whole source before extraction. Build a neutral source-order ledger of represented actors, stable referents/content, source-present characterizations, lexical relations, physical scenes/positions, temporal episodes/periods, material orientation relations, and materially distinct bindings.

Then apply two separate operations that must not be collapsed:

1. **SCENE/FRAME COMPLETION** — recover physical PLACE and temporal TIME anchors needed to represent source-distinguished scenes and episodes, including supported unnamed anchors.
2. **PRIMITIVE COMPRESSION** — for PERSON, OBJECT, LABEL, VERB and LOCATOR, and for named PLACE/TIME wording, retain only the smallest source-native coordinate that performs a distinct lightweight research job.

Completeness of the scene ledger does not authorize lexical census. A phrase being visible, parseable, descriptive, temporal, spatial, or predicative does not by itself make it a primitive.

## 2. Primitive admission test

Admit a candidate primitive only when all applicable conditions hold:

1. **SOURCE ANCHOR** — exact source wording/cue establishes the coordinate, or establishes the occurrence whose unnamed PLACE/TIME anchor is represented.
2. **RESEARCH IDENTITY** — the candidate has one stable lightweight job: actor/group; stable thing/content; characterization/value/state; lexical relation; physical occurrence position; temporal episode/period; or orientation edge.
3. **SAME-CLASS DISTINCTION** — merging it with another same-class coordinate would erase a source-present identity, scene, episode, characterization, relation or orientation distinction.
4. **MINIMAL GRAIN** — use the shortest complete source-native form that preserves identity, polarity, modality, uncertainty, question, correction, comparison, direction, manner, or fixed-expression identity.
5. **NO WRAPPER/echo** — reject pronoun duplicates, clause/event wrappers, support predicates, nominalizations, adverb/preposition fragments, alternate decompositions, and grammatical restatements whose research job is already carried by retained coordinates.

Low salience, background status, implicit scene position, reported status, figurative wording or overlap with another class does not disqualify an otherwise valid coordinate.

## 3. Scene/frame completion

Segment the source into materially distinct represented scenes/episodes before projecting primitives.

### PLACE completion

A PLACE is a physical occurrence setting/position at scene grain. Retain named settings and source-supported unnamed positions when a distinct interaction, relationship episode, participant/object position, offscreen destination, remembered/reported scene, or embodied present scene requires a physical anchor and the source leaves it unnamed.

Do not create PLACE from every physical noun, component, surface, support, path, destination word, or object. A floor, counter, wall, side, line, container, body part, vehicle or other physical thing stays OBJECT/LOCATOR unless the source treats its position as a distinct occurrence setting worth revisiting.

A represented present-telling PLACE is legal only when current deixis, bodily/performance action, explicit current reflection, or contrast with narrated scenes makes the telling itself a represented physical scene. Narration alone is insufficient.

### TIME completion

A TIME is an episode, period, phase, or frame that organizes one or more represented bindings. Retain source-supported unnamed frames for distinct interactions, waits, remembered/reported episodes, recurring periods, relationship spans, intended/prospective periods, reflective episodes, and represented present telling.

Do not create TIME from every temporal/deictic token, aspect word, discourse transition, duration, frequency expression, adverb, or predicate-local moment. Such wording may locate a retained frame without becoming another TIME.

Several relations may share one PLACE and/or TIME frame.

## 4. PERSON

Retain `B` and every source-distinguished represented human/social actor or stable group participating in represented material, including indirect, possessive, relational, offscreen, remembered, reported, quoted, or prospective actors.

Resolve aliases/coreference. Exclude generic/rhetorical addressees and role/classification words that do not denote a represented actor.

## 5. OBJECT

OBJECT records stable concrete things and stable abstract/content referents that function as arguments or revisitable content in represented bindings.

Retain concrete objects when they are acted on, located, described, compared, possessed, exchanged, or otherwise given stable referential identity. Retain abstract choices, decisions, relations, internal referents, figurative referents, topics, amounts, results or services only when the source treats them as a stable referent rather than merely the meaning of a whole clause.

Use one object for one referential identity. Reject nested proposition wrappers, generic `what happened`/`what was wanted` style wrappers, duplicate event nouns, pronoun restatements, and nominalizations whose content is already represented by a relation plus its arguments. If both a concrete referent and an abstract decision/content slot are independently represented, both may survive.

## 6. LABEL

LABEL records a source-present characterization whose value itself is useful to revisit: quality, state, evaluation, comparison, classification, self/other label, correction/rejection, polarity/status, or materially qualifying manner.

Prefer the characterization value, not a duplicate predicate. If the source expresses a property through a support/copular construction, normally retain the source-native value as LABEL and do not additionally create a VERB solely for `be/seem/become + value` unless the relation itself contributes a distinct research job beyond carrying the characterization.

Preserve question, negation, uncertainty, intensity, correction, comparison and idiom. Do not census every adjective/adverb, ordinary modifier, discourse reaction, or entire question merely because it contains evaluative language. A materially distinct source question-label or explicit classification can survive as LABEL when the classification itself is the research coordinate.

## 7. VERB

VERB records the smallest complete lexical relation for a materially represented binding.

Retain actions, movements, perception, possession, cognition, remembering/finding, speaking/reporting/asking, helping/causing/attempting, waiting/standing/sitting, change relations, and represented bodily/performance actions when they connect retained coordinates or establish a distinct occurrence.

Compress syntactic support. A matrix/embedded, serial, coordinated, phrasal or support construction is **one VERB** when the source presents one research relation and splitting it would only reproduce grammar. Split only when each relation independently changes the represented binding or can participate in a distinct binding with retained coordinates.

Do not create VERB merely to restate a LABEL, LOCATOR, or grammatical support relation. Exclude pure auxiliaries, copular/property carriers without independent relational work, discourse-floor mechanics, and duplicate support predicates. Keep negation, modality, particles and required complements together when they define lexical identity/posture.

## 8. LOCATOR

LOCATOR records a source-native orientation edge that materially positions a retained coordinate or binding: position, path/direction, origin/destination, entry/exit, containment/support, accompaniment, proximity/distance, recurrence/temporal context, figurative displacement, or internal orientation.

Keep the smallest complete orientation phrase. Do not census every preposition, recipient, beneficiary, topic, instrument, duration, frequency, deictic, temporal word, or discourse transition. A bare temporal expression normally belongs to a TIME frame or its source cue rather than becoming LOCATOR too.

PLACE/TIME and LOCATOR may overlap only when the same source wording does two genuinely different jobs: one identifies a scene/frame, the other encodes a material orientation relation inside or across scenes/frames.

## 9. Cross-class overlap: different job, not different parse

Cross-class overlap remains legal, but only when each projection records a different schema job in the represented structure. Do not create overlap merely because the same words can be grammatically parsed in two ways.

Use this compression question: **If both projections are retained, can you name two different research jobs without describing one as a restatement of the other?** If not, keep the single best-fitting native class. This is a duplicate-control test, not an exclusive-primary-home rule; genuine multi-job spans remain legal.

Typical class-home preferences when there is no second job:

- stable entity/content identity → OBJECT;
- characterization/value/state → LABEL;
- lexical relation connecting coordinates → VERB;
- physical occurrence setting/position → PLACE;
- episode/period/frame → TIME;
- orientation edge → LOCATOR.

## 10. Literal preservation lock

Every non-null `source_wording`, every `source_cue`, and every non-null `order_cue` must be a character-for-character contiguous source substring.

Preserve spelling, punctuation, dialect, contractions, number, questions, negation, uncertainty, correction, comparison, attribution, intention, recurrence/report/prospective posture, and source-present performance wording. Never substitute synonyms, standardize dialect, repair grammar, or normalize lexical content.

Researcher tags should use source wording where a named coordinate exists. Neutral descriptive tags are permitted only for genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME navigation and may not introduce a substantive interpretation.

## 11. Ordering and qualities

Order each class by first source establishment after coreference, with speaker first in PERSON.

`qualities_available` is true only when source-present qualities/descriptions are available for that coordinate. A quality need not itself become a LABEL to make Q true. Q is a pointer to available descriptive material, not an instruction to emit every description as another primitive.

## 12. Compound reconstruction

Freeze primitives before compounds. Re-read the source binding by binding.

Emit one smallest materially complete compound for each distinct source-present research binding that connects, attributes, classifies, questions, positions, or otherwise materially relates retained coordinates. A compound need not contain a VERB when the source establishes a distinct classification/quality binding without one.

For each compound include all and only frozen primitives materially co-bound in that one binding, plus the PLACE/TIME frame and LOCATOR edge when those anchors materially belong to it. Do not attach every scene-wide primitive to every local binding merely because it is available in the same paragraph.

Do not emit pairwise closure, every primitive as a standalone compound, duplicate alternate decompositions, broad scene supersets, or redundant subset/superset variants. Do not invent compounds solely to provide a home for an admitted primitive.

Before finalizing compounds, verify:

1. one binding purpose per compound;
2. no unmatched or invented primitive refs;
3. no duplicate semantic binding under a different decomposition;
4. all source-material frozen coordinates belonging to that binding are present;
5. neighboring but non-bound scene material is absent;
6. literal posture is preserved in `researcher_bundle`.

## 13. Final excess/gap audit

Run both directions before returning:

**Gap audit:** for every represented actor, stable referent, research-significant characterization, lexical relation, physical scene, temporal frame and orientation edge, confirm the appropriate coordinate exists.

**Excess audit:** for every retained primitive ask whether it represents a distinct lightweight research job or merely a token, grammatical support, property-predicate duplicate, clause wrapper, surface/component, temporal/deictic fragment, or alternate decomposition. Remove the latter.

Then verify source-order, Q flags, compound reference integrity, and exact lexical preservation.
