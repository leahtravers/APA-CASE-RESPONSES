V21 supersedes V20 for forward calibration behavior because the V20 run preserved at `calibration_history/researcher_inventory/34791080786/` demonstrated a generalizable under-resolution failure: a global omit-when-marginal / material-loss pressure caused the worker to discard materially represented class-local coordinates simply because related content was already carried by another class or broader frame.

V21 replaces that global deletion pressure with **class-local coordinate closure**:

> **Discover the complete set of materially represented coordinates for the requested class first. Then prune only same-class aliases, true duplicates, and grammar debris. Cross-class overlap is allowed whenever the same source material performs a real job in more than one class.**

Retained unchanged:

- exactly seven inventory classes;
- literal/source-near lexical preservation and no synonym substitution;
- full-source reading before any class return;
- approved archetype, evaluator, scored-output, and sealed-holdout isolation;
- candidate-only status;

## Isolation

The worker never receives approved archetypes, gold outputs, expected answers, expected counts, evaluator findings, prior scored outputs, sealed holdout source, or holdout outputs.

The worker receives only this durable contract, one bounded source story, neutral task rules/schema, and, when explicitly supplied by the apparatus, an unscored provisional output from another stateless pass under this same contract.

Never infer or reconstruct a hidden expected answer.

## Job

Read the whole source. Build a lightweight Researcher Inventory in exactly seven classes:

1. `PLACE`
2. `TIME`
3. `PERSON`
4. `OBJECT`
5. `LABEL`
6. `VERB`
7. `LOCATOR`

Then build lightweight compounds from the retained units.

This is an index of researcher-selectable coordinates, not a token inventory, grammar parse, summary, psychological analysis, or exhaustive ontology.

## Class-local coordinate closure

For the requested class, perform two silent passes in this order.

### Pass A — coverage

Read the complete source and identify every materially represented coordinate that performs this class's positive job.

A coordinate can be local to one episode, proposition, relation, destination, reflection, recurrence, or future frame. It does not need to be globally reusable across the whole story.

The same source span or represented situation may legitimately support multiple classes. Do not delete a coordinate merely because another class carries related meaning.

### Pass B — pruning

After coverage is complete, remove only:

- same-class aliases or coreferential duplicates;
- a second row doing the identical class job at the identical semantic coordinate;
- bare grammatical/support fragments with no independent class function;
- invented coordinates not materially represented by the source.

Do not prune merely for row-count minimization. Do not ask whether another class already covers the idea. Ask whether this class has a real coordinate here.

## Source language

Never substitute a synonym for source language.

For an explicit coordinate:

- `source_wording` is a character-for-character contiguous substring of the source;
- `source_cue` is a character-for-character contiguous substring of the source;
- preserve punctuation, apostrophes, hyphens, capitalization, spelling, and dialect;
- copy from the source rather than reconstructing from memory;
- keep `researcher_short_tag` compact and source-near.

For a materially required unnamed PLACE or TIME only, `source_wording = null` is permitted. Its `source_cue` must still be an exact source substring and the short tag must neutrally describe the class job without inventing a named location or date.

`researcher_note` is normally null. Use it only for neutral coreference, an unnamed PLACE/TIME explanation, or required mechanical bookkeeping.

Preserve negation, questions, uncertainty, correction, comparison, recurrence, intention, hypothetical posture, reported speech, and futurity. Do not convert represented possibility into asserted occurrence.

## PLACE — where-coordinate job

PLACE indexes **where materially represented situations, people, objects, movements, relationships, conversations, destinations, or telling/reporting occurrences are situated**.

Keep, when materially represented:

- explicit broad and contained settings;
- explicit object/person positions;
- origins and destinations, including an asserted movement's represented goal/destination even when arrival is not established;
- unnamed positions of materially distinct occurrences, waits, encounters, relationships, conversations, reports, or later retellings when the source does not name the place;
- the materially distinct present telling/reporting occurrence.

Physical overlap does not force two represented situation-positions to merge. Two episodes can share a physical area and still be separately selectable where-coordinates when the source distinguishes the situations.

Do not create PLACE solely from:

- a hypothetical action that is only contemplated and has no represented place of its own;
- generic recurrence without a distinct situation-position;
- a mental/state metaphor whose primary job is not spatial situation;
- a direction/path particle whose job is only LOCATOR;
- every predicate within one undifferentiated situation.

## TIME — episode/frame job

TIME indexes **materially distinct temporal episodes, subepisodes, spans, transitions, recurrences, reflections, intended periods, reported periods, and prospective/future frames**.

No clock or date is required.

Keep a distinct TIME when the source materially establishes a frame or subframe in its own right, including when an event boundary, actor/task shift, changed state, later report, standing relation, intention, recurrence, reflection, or prospective frame makes that temporal coordinate separately selectable.

A subepisode can deserve its own TIME even inside one broader visit or scene.

Do not create TIME merely from:

- verb count or tense/aspect;
- every clause inside one unchanged frame;
- a duration question without a separate represented frame;
- a merely hypothetical action unless the source separately establishes an intended/prospective temporal frame for it.

## PERSON — human actor job

PERSON is a materially represented human or stable social group.

Keep the speaker and people/groups who act, speak, perceive, are acted upon, or are material human endpoints, owners, sources, beneficiaries, or participants of retained relations or objects.

Resolve pronouns, aliases, kinship expressions, and stable groups before counting. Do not create PERSON from a generic audience, grammatical person, or hypothetical role with no distinct represented human actor.

## OBJECT — independently selectable referent job

OBJECT is an independently selectable represented referent.

It may be concrete, abstract, relational, internal, or figurative. Keep, when materially represented as selectable content:

- things and source-distinguished wholes/parts;
- values and sets/categories;
- choices, decisions, or next-step alternatives;
- standing or recurring relations treated as something the source discusses or depends on;
- internal or figurative objects;
- states/situations treated by the source as an object of thought, explanation, decision, maintenance, or action.

An OBJECT may coexist with a VERB or LABEL that expresses related content. Cross-class overlap is not duplication.

Do not create OBJECT for every noun phrase, complement clause, proposition, pronoun, metadiscourse wrapper, or arbitrary nominalization. Prune abstract wrappers that add no independently selectable referent beyond a more specific retained object.

## LABEL — characterization job

LABEL is the **source-applied characterization**, including the amount of source wording needed to preserve its actual polarity, comparison, qualification, or state.

Keep materially represented:

- qualities and states;
- identity terms and evaluations;
- comparisons;
- corrections, rejections, or negated characterizations;
- locative-looking wording when it is functioning as a characterization;
- characterizations embedded in questions, reports, or reflections.

Use the shortest **complete** source phrase that carries the characterization. Preserve negation or other wording when removing it would change the characterization. Strip only scaffolding that does not belong to the characterization itself.

A LABEL may overlap a PLACE, LOCATOR, VERB, or OBJECT when the source phrase independently performs a characterization job too.

Do not create LABEL from a pure action failure, relation, or reporting scaffold unless the wording itself characterizes something.

## VERB — material relation-edge job

VERB indexes materially distinct **action or relation edges** needed to reconnect represented coordinates.

Use the shortest complete source-near predicate expression for each material relation edge. Distinct nested, controlled, embedded, modal, locative, reporting, perception, thought, state, or purpose relations may each receive a VERB when each one connects materially represented participants/referents or establishes a separately useful relation.

Do not merge two material relation edges merely because one syntactically contains the other.

Do not split into rows for bare auxiliaries, tense support, negation particles, infinitive markers, or coordination syntax that have no relation job of their own.

A VERB may overlap a LABEL or LOCATOR when the same wording independently performs a relation job.

## LOCATOR — locating/context relation job

LOCATOR is a source phrase that independently locates, positions, routes, contains, contextualizes, or relates a retained coordinate or situation.

Keep materially represented:

- setting and position phrases;
- paths, directions, origins, and destinations;
- movement expressions when they independently locate a trajectory or transition;
- containment and proximity;
- recurring-context relations;
- genuine figurative or mental location/path relations when the source treats them as such.

A LOCATOR may overlap a VERB or LABEL if it independently performs a locating/context job. Cross-class overlap is allowed.

Do not create LOCATOR from isolated prepositions, routine possession/recipient/topic marking, purely temporal wording, or characterization-only wording with no locating/context function.

## qualities_available

`qualities_available` is a boolean only.

Set it true when material qualifying/descriptive language is associated with the coordinate in the source. Otherwise false.

Do not enumerate qualities. `Q` is not a unit reference and never creates a row by itself.

## Ordering

The apparatus owns final IDs and numbering.

Within each class, order by the earliest material source anchor of the resolved semantic coordinate after alias/coreference resolution.

When later wording refers back to an already established coordinate, keep the coordinate at its earliest material anchor. Where broad and contained coordinates are introduced at the same anchor, broad precedes contained. Keep reported, intended, hypothetical, and prospective material in source-discourse order rather than reconstructing real-world chronology.

## Stateless verification

If the apparatus supplies an unscored provisional output, rebuild from the complete source and this contract.

Verification is **coverage first, pruning second**:

1. identify missing class-local coordinates from the source and add them;
2. then remove only same-class aliases, true duplicates, grammar debris, or invented rows;
3. preserve legitimate cross-class overlap;
4. repair source copying and semantic ordering;
5. do not preserve or delete a row merely because it appeared in the provisional output.

## Compounds

Build compounds only after units are final.

Create one compound for each materially distinct proposition/situation useful for reconnecting retained coordinates.

For each compound:

- identify the proposition first;
- include every retained unit that materially participates in that proposition;
- include the specific local PLACE/TIME/LOCATOR frame that governs the proposition even when that frame was established earlier in nearby discourse and is not repeated in the same sentence;
- local frame inheritance may continue across nearby propositions while the same episode/frame remains in force;
- stop inheriting a frame when the source transitions to a different episode, setting, relation span, reported frame, reflection, or prospective frame;
- do not propagate a broad/global setting or frame through the entire story merely because it remains generally true;
- keep one proposition together and split materially different propositions;
- do not make quality-only fragments, arbitrary subsets, or every possible recombination;
- preserve source/semantic order of references.

A compound cannot compensate for a missing unit and cannot invent semantics.

## Apparatus boundary

The worker must not depend on hidden canonical IDs, archetypes, evaluator criteria, expected counts, or scored outputs.
