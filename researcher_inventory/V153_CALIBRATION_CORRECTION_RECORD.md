## 4. Root cause

V152 defines the correct high-level direction — one represented coordinate/relation role rather than every class-valid surface occurrence — but ties that direction too closely to `canonical primitive` and `smallest complete source-native kernel` language.

That wording can be interpreted as a lexical-minimization objective. The Researcher Inventory needs a different grain test: the smallest contiguous span that preserves the **complete researcher-addressable role as the source stages it**.

A relation's correct unit may contain grammatically separable words when those words define the relation role a researcher needs to recover. Conversely, a larger clause may still be excessive when a smaller role-complete envelope exists.

The same issue appears structurally: PLACE/TIME identity must be derived from the source's staged scene/episode lattice, not primarily from explicit locative/temporal phrases or relation kernels.

## 5. V153 corrective principle

V153 replaces V152's kernel-centric identity formulation with:

`whole-source source graph → scene/episode lattice → researcher-addressable class role → role-complete source-native envelope → same-class identity reconciliation → structural and relation recall → frozen role graph → selective minimal compounds`

The controlling question becomes:

> What distinct coordinate or relation role can a researcher address in the represented source, and what smallest exact contiguous source span preserves that role completely?

This remains stricter than an occurrence census and broader than a bare lexical-head census.

## 7. Relation-grain correction

### LABEL

LABEL is functional rather than part-of-speech based. Source-applied categories, states, evaluations, comparisons, questions, rejections, corrections, and relation states remain eligible when they are independently addressable characterizations. Incidental texture remains excluded.

### VERB

VERB uses a `role-complete lexical envelope`, not a bare grammatical head and not a whole clause. Keep exact contiguous complements, particles, contextual function words, polarity, modality, direction, or role-bearing wording when removing them changes what source-staged relation the researcher can address. Suppress only wording that is genuinely external to that relation role or duplicates another width of the same occurrence.

Repeated identical wording may remain separate when it represents separate acts at different source anchors or with different participants/frames.

### LOCATOR

LOCATOR remains selective but recognizes any independently represented orienting/contextual role, including situational, recurrence, figurative, or relation-bound positioning. Ordinary recipient/topic/possessive/abstract-argument markers remain excluded unless the source independently stages them as orientation/context.

### Cross-class multiplicity

The same source occurrence may lawfully survive in more than one class when each class supplies a distinct researcher function. Same-span cross-class overlap is not itself duplication.
