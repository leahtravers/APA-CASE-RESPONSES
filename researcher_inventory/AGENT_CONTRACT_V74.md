## Mission

Produce only a literal lightweight Researcher Inventory candidate from the supplied source. Recover the source-present research-coordinate map at the natural represented-coordinate grain demonstrated by the approved archetypes, preserving lexical form, source order, local binding, and epistemic posture.

`Lightweight` is neither a narrative summary nor a lexical/phrase census. Do not prune a coordinate merely because it is minor, one-off, background, ambient, reported, hypothetical, prospective, or emotionally unimportant. At the same time, do not mint a coordinate from every noun phrase, adjective, predicate token, preposition, temporal cue, clause fragment, or decomposable subphrase. Preserve the source's represented nodes, frames, values, relations, and orientations at their natural source-native grain.

Do not interpret psychological meaning, score APA, infer protected threads, draw conclusions, promote records, mint APA IDs, or admit anything to sovereign/production fabric. The worker never receives approved archetypes, expected counts, evaluator findings, prior scored outputs, sealed holdout source during calibration, or holdout outputs.

## 1. Reconstruct the represented map before extracting classes

Read the complete source first. Silently reconstruct in source order:

1. **Episode / scene ledger** — distinct represented situations, waits, transitions, remembered/reported episodes, recurring periods, prospective episodes, and the present-telling frame.
2. **Participant / referent ledger** — people, groups, concrete things, abstract/reified things, scene objects, values, choices, and other source-present referents.
3. **Binding ledger** — actions, relations, characterizations, corrections/rejections, reports, comparisons, intentions, orientations, and local co-bindings.
4. **Location / time anchors** — named or supported unnamed occurrence positions and temporal frames that locate those episodes or bindings.

The ledgers are internal planning devices. Do not output a lexical parse.

## 2. Five-part primitive admission test

A primitive survives when all are true:

1. **Source grounding** — it has exact source wording/cue, except a genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME anchor supported by an exact source cue.
2. **Class job** — it performs the semantic research job of one of the seven classes.
3. **Represented-coordinate identity** — it is a distinct node, frame, value, relation, or orientation in the represented map, rather than only grammar or an arbitrary parse fragment.
4. **Natural grain** — its span is the smallest complete source-native expression of that represented coordinate, not an under-specified token and not an unnecessarily broad proposition wrapper.
5. **Non-duplication** — after true coreference and role reconciliation, it is not merely a second alias, repeated mention, support shell, or alternate decomposition of the same coordinate.

**Do not add a materiality or narrative-importance gate.** Minor/background coordinates can qualify.

**Do not add an explicitness-entitlement gate.** Being explicitly worded does not automatically make every fragment a primitive.

When uncertain, ask: `If a researcher pointed to this item alone, would it name one represented coordinate at the source's own grain, or only a grammatical/lexical piece of another coordinate?`

## 3. PLACE — occurrence positions, not spatial tokens

PLACE is a represented physical setting or occurrence-position that locates an episode, participant/object configuration, destination, wait, remembered/reported interaction, prospective episode, or present telling.

Preserve broad and nested places when each supplies a real occurrence-position. Preserve supported unnamed places when the source clearly locates a represented participant/object/episode but does not name the place; use `source_wording: null` plus an exact source cue.

A local participant/object position may deserve its own PLACE even if it is one-off. A named broad setting may coexist with a more local occurrence-position.

Do not create PLACE merely from a deictic word, preposition, surface, body part, direction token, or movement particle. The PLACE coordinate is the represented occurrence-position; the lexical orienting phrase belongs in LOCATOR when it has a separate orientation job.

A concrete thing whose primary job is thing identity is OBJECT, not automatically PLACE. Cross-class projection requires a genuinely separate place job.

## 4. TIME — represented frames, not every temporal cue

TIME is a represented episode, period, transition-stage, wait, recurrence, remembered/reported frame, intended/prospective frame, or present-telling frame that organizes a distinct source binding or stage.

Preserve multiple nested/local TIME coordinates when the source distinguishes genuinely different episodes or stages. A short micro-episode may qualify when it is itself a represented stage, even if brief.

A duration, adverb, sequence word, clock phrase, tense/aspect form, question about duration, infinitive, or local predicate is evidence, not automatically a TIME primitive. Admit it as TIME only when it names or anchors a represented temporal frame rather than merely modifying another binding.

A supported unnamed TIME may use `source_wording: null` with an exact source cue. Do not invent wording.

## 5. PERSON

Retain `B` plus every represented human/social actor or stable group after true coreference, including minor, indirect, possessive, relational, offscreen, remembered, reported, prospective, and comparison actors when the source actually represents that actor.

A person can qualify through a possessive or relational mention even if the actor never appears directly in the scene. Exclude only rhetorical/generic addressees that do not denote a represented actor.

For true aliases, preserve one person coordinate and use the most stable source-native identity wording available while keeping source order by first establishment.

## 6. OBJECT — broad referent coverage at natural grain

OBJECT is a represented concrete or abstract thing/referent. Preserve source-present scene objects and props, products, documents, surfaces/things, parts, values/amounts, internal/mental referents, plans, choices-as-things, stable relations/situations, categories/sets, reified concepts, and figurative/comparison referents when the source represents them as things.

Background or ambient status is not a reason to discard a distinct object. A one-off scene object can be archetypally valid.

But do not atomize every noun phrase into every head noun, modifier, possessed subpart, or syntactic argument. Use the natural represented referent. If several words/mentions corefer with the same thing, preserve one coordinate using the most stable source-native identity wording and record aliases only in notes as needed.

Do not turn a whole event/assertion into OBJECT merely because it can be nominalized. A source-reified choice, relation, situation, result, thought-content, or category may be OBJECT when the source itself treats it as a thing.

## 7. LABEL — source-staged characterization values

LABEL is a represented characterization, state, evaluation, identity/classification, comparison value, correction/rejection, status, experiential/epistemic description, or other source-staged value.

Preserve discrete candidate/rejection/correction values separately when the source presents them as separate values. Preserve uncertainty, question, negation, intensity, contrast, and idiom when they belong to the value.

Use the shortest complete source-native value. Do not inventory every adjective, adverb, noun modifier, or descriptive flourish merely because it can characterize something. The span must function as a represented characterization value in the binding map.

A noun phrase whose primary job is a referent/reified thing belongs in OBJECT even if descriptive. A participial or verb-shaped form may be LABEL when its job is characterization rather than an asserted relation.

`qualities_available` remains separate from LABEL inventory.

## 8. VERB — natural source-native relation units

VERB is a represented lexical action/relation/predicate at its natural source-native grain.

Preserve actions, movement, possession, experience, cognition, speech/reporting, perception, intention, comparison, waiting, gestures, meta-telling, and other predicates when they establish a distinct represented relation or event in the binding ledger. Minor or reported actions are not excluded merely for being incidental.

Do not split one relation into every parseable predicate token. Use the smallest **semantically complete** source-native relation expression. Include a particle, complement, reflexive, directional element, or state phrase when removing it would leave the relation under-specified or change its identity. Conversely, omit ordinary auxiliaries, pure support grammar, aspect shells, and infinitival scaffolding when they do not establish a separate represented relation.

A matrix/report/cognition/attempt predicate is separate only when it contributes its own represented relation; it is not automatically separate merely because it grammatically contains another predicate. A coordinated or embedded action becomes separate when the source actually represents a distinct binding/event for it.

Preserve exact question, negation, uncertainty, attribution, and modality in source evidence and compound posture even when the lexical relation atom itself is shorter.

## 9. LOCATOR — complete orientation relations

LOCATOR is a complete source-native orientation/context relation that positions, contains, directs, originates, terminates, accompanies, situates, or otherwise orients a retained coordinate or binding.

Preserve physical and figurative position, containment, path, proximity, direction, origin/destination, accompaniment, embodied/internal orientation, and other meaningful locating constructions when the orientation is itself a represented relation.

Use the smallest complete meaningful orienting construction, not every preposition, adverb, particle, or deictic token. A directional/action phrase may function as LOCATOR when the phrase itself supplies a where/which-way/from/to relation; this can coexist with VERB only if the lexical action and the orientation are genuinely distinct research jobs.

Do not duplicate PLACE/TIME merely by copying their wording into LOCATOR unless the source phrase independently expresses an orientation/context relation.

## 10. Class assignment and overlap

Choose the primary class by represented job:

- occurrence-setting / physical position -> PLACE;
- temporal episode / period / frame -> TIME;
- actor identity -> PERSON;
- thing / referent identity -> OBJECT;
- characterization / value / state -> LABEL;
- lexical action / relation -> VERB;
- orientation / path / relational position -> LOCATOR.

Cross-class overlap is permitted only when the same source material genuinely supplies two different represented jobs. Do not duplicate a span across classes merely because a grammatical analysis can support both.

Before overlap, ask whether each projection would still be a useful distinct coordinate if the other projection disappeared.

## 11. Literal preservation lock

Every non-null `source_wording`, `source_cue`, non-null `order_cue`, and source-derived short tag must preserve source language character-for-character where the schema requires source text.

Copy source spans directly. Never substitute synonyms, grammatical repairs, dialect normalization, spelling cleanup, number changes, expanded contractions, apostrophe/quotation normalization, inferred terminology, or semantically convenient replacements.

Preserve questions, negation, uncertainty, correction, comparison, attribution, intention, recurrence, reported posture, prospective posture, figurative wording, punctuation, and dialect.

Only genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME coordinates may use `source_wording: null`, with exact source evidence in `source_cue`.

## 12. Ordering, coreference, and qualities

Order each class by first source establishment after true coreference, with speaker first in PERSON.

Coreference collapses aliases into one coordinate; it must not discard a minor actor/object simply because the first mention is possessive, figurative, descriptive, or indirect. When later source wording provides a clearer stable identity for the same coordinate, the stable wording may name the coordinate while order remains tied to first establishment.

`qualities_available` is true only when source-present qualities/descriptions are available for that coordinate. It neither forces nor forbids a separate LABEL.

## 13. Primitive freeze: coverage pass, then anti-census pass

Before compounds, perform two separate passes.

### 13.1 Coverage pass
Check for omitted minor actors, scene objects/props, nested/broad places, supported unnamed occurrence positions, distinct episode frames, source-staged characterizations, minor/reporting/gesture actions, and meaningful orientation relations. Do not remove them merely as incidental.

### 13.2 Anti-census pass
Remove:
- temporal/deictic cue tokens that do not themselves define a frame;
- arbitrary noun subparts or duplicate alias projections;
- every-adjective characterization inflation;
- auxiliary/support/attempt/aspect predicate fragments that add no separate relation;
- preposition/particle fragments that do not form a complete orientation relation;
- clause/proposition wrappers duplicating more natural primitives;
- cross-class duplicates with no separate class job.

Freeze the resulting natural-grain primitive inventory. Compounds may not repair or expand it.

## 14. Canonical local compound reconstruction

After primitive freeze, re-read the source in order and rebuild the binding ledger. Compounds represent **source bindings**, not primitive co-occurrence and not every syntactic clause.

For each distinct represented binding, create one materially complete local compound containing:

- the relation-bearing VERB/LABEL/LOCATOR primitive(s) that define the binding;
- every frozen PERSON/OBJECT/value coordinate actually co-bound in it;
- the applicable PLACE and TIME episode anchors when they locate that binding;
- preserved question/negation/uncertainty/attribution posture through the schema.

Several relation-bearing primitives may share one compound when the source presents one inseparable binding. Split compounds only when the source presents independently selectable bindings.

Do not generate one compound per primitive. Do not create pairwise subsets, nested partial duplicates, alternate decompositions of the same binding, or giant scene-wide bundles spanning unrelated bindings. Do not omit a place/time anchor merely because it is implicit once the episode ledger has made it available.

A primitive may remain unbundled only when the source supplies no binding to another retained coordinate.

## 15. Final audits

**Coverage audit:** no narrative-importance pruning; check minor/background/one-off coordinates.

**Anti-census audit:** no word/phrase census; every primitive must be one represented coordinate at natural grain.

**Episode audit:** PLACE/TIME are occurrence/episode anchors, not copied spatial/temporal tokens.

**Coreference audit:** collapse aliases while preserving minor/indirect actors and referents; prefer stable source-native identity wording.

**Type audit:** classify by represented semantic job before considering overlap.

**VERB grain audit:** one semantically complete relation unit, not every predicate token and not an under-specified bare verb when a source-native complement defines the relation.

**LABEL grain audit:** preserve source-staged values/corrections; reject modifier census.

**LOCATOR grain audit:** preserve complete orienting relations; reject isolated grammatical fragments.

**Literal audit:** compare every source-derived string character-for-character against the source.

**Compound audit:** one canonical source binding -> one complete compound with all and only co-bound frozen coordinates plus applicable episode anchors; no unmatched refs, alternate subsets, or scene-wide inflation.

Then verify source order, qualities flags, candidate-only status, and holdout isolation.
