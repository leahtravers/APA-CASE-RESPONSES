## 2. Generalized V145 defect

V145 improved substantially over V144 by suppressing lexical/grammatical census behavior, but its controlling selection architecture remained too strict in one direction and too permissive in another.

V145 required primitives to clear an `independent research handle` gate before compound construction. Across both approved archetypes this produced the same structural pattern:

- source-presented but low-salience, unnamed, generic, deictic, relation-bound, present-telling, reported, or prospective slots were omitted when they lacked obvious standalone prominence;
- relation kernels and orientation/characterization wording could be suppressed merely because their identity was tied to one relation;
- simultaneously, abstract content/proposition phrases could be admitted as OBJECTs because they appeared semantically trackable even though the source had not reified them as separate referents;
- the primitive mismatch then cascaded into compound mismatch because compounds were forced to rebuild source relations from an independently selected primitive inventory.

The cross-case recurrence makes this defect generalizable. It is not a case-specific patch.

## 3. V146 corrective principle

V146 changes the order of resolution:

`represented frames → source-native relation ledger → functional primitive slots → identity reconciliation → frozen primitives → compounds`

A primitive is retained when it preserves a represented functional slot in a retained relation/frame, or when it is a stable source-established coordinate/referent reused across relations.

Standalone or cross-context usefulness is no longer a prerequisite.

The governing test becomes representational difference:

> Would removing this exact source-native slot erase a distinction the source actually presents about who/what, where, when, characterization, relation, or orientation?

This preserves relation-bound distinctions while still rejecting grammar-only material and analyst-created proposition shells.

## 4. OBJECT correction

V146 strengthens the source-reification boundary for OBJECT.

A proposition, relation, clause, or discourse content is not turned into an OBJECT merely because it can be summarized as a noun phrase. The source itself must give it referential standing through handling, possession, selection, pointing-back, comparison, decision-about, questioning-as-a-thing, or another source-grounded reification cue.

This correction is intended to reduce semantic content-shell inflation without suppressing legitimate abstract referents.

## 5. VERB / LABEL / LOCATOR correction

V146 removes the requirement that these classes be independently reusable outside the host relation.

They remain gated by represented function, not grammar:

- VERB preserves the smallest contiguous source-native relation kernel needed to express a research-bearing relation;
- LABEL preserves source-presented characterization distinctions;
- LOCATOR preserves source-presented orientation/context distinctions.

Brief, generic, copular, reporting, support, questioned, negative, colloquial, or one-use wording may survive when it is necessary to preserve the relation's represented identity. Grammar-only fragments remain excluded.

## 6. PLACE / TIME correction

V146 keeps V145's unnamed-coordinate capability but ties it directly to retained relations/frames.

An unnamed PLACE or TIME is required when the source distinctly stages a where/when coordinate necessary to reconstruct a represented relation or frame even though no noun/date names it. It is not created merely because every occurrence logically has a place and time.

## 7. Compound correction

V146 does not treat compounds as a downstream repair layer.

Compounds replay selected multi-primitive relations from the same relation ledger that licensed primitive slots. This creates one semantic backbone for both layers while preserving the rule that compounds cannot create or retype primitives.

Not every primitive-bearing relation becomes a compound; compound admission remains limited to distinct minimal multi-primitive relations useful for reconstruction.
