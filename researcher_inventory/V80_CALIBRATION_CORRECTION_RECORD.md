## Generalizable requirement admitted

V80 establishes **POSITIVE CLASS-NATIVE SATURATION WITH BOUNDED SEMANTIC DEDUPLICATION**.

The construction order changes prospectively:

1. read the whole source and map coarse scene/episode transitions and coreference;
2. perform a positive class-native coverage pass for each requested class across the complete source;
3. preserve every source-supported coordinate that performs that class's own researcher-reconnectable job, including low-salience, one-use, nested, remembered, reported, prospective, figurative, supported-unnamed, and cross-class material where applicable;
4. only after positive coverage, apply a bounded suppression/deduplication pass;
5. freeze all primitives;
6. construct relation-led compounds from the frozen primitive set.

### Positive saturation is not lexical census

Positive saturation means the worker must first ask whether a source span or supported unnamed frame genuinely instantiates the requested class. It does not mean every noun, verb token, adjective, preposition, temporal word, or clause becomes a coordinate.

Suppression is permitted only for a bounded set of defects:

- true alias/coreference duplicates;
- pure auxiliary/function grammar;
- bare prepositions with no independent orientation job;
- duplicate same-grain lexical relations;
- analyst-created abstractions or paraphrases;
- arbitrary subparts with no independent source-established identity;
- whole-clause/proposition wrappers not treated by the source as an identifiable content, choice, plan, decision, relationship, situation, or object of thought;
- duplicate projections that perform the same researcher job.

Low salience, one-time appearance, background placement, or participation as scene furniture is not by itself a suppression reason when the source independently treats the referent or coordinate as a thing, setting, frame, value, relation, or orientation.

## Class-native coverage requirements

### PLACE

Inventory source-distinguished physical/institutional settings and occurrence positions, including broad/contained settings, waiting positions, interaction/conversation locations, object positions when spatially staged, destinations, another participant's represented scene, remembered/reported scenes, prospective/comparison scenes, and present-telling location. Supported unnamed PLACE is required when a distinct represented scene/location job exists without an explicit place name.

### TIME

Inventory source-distinguished episodes, phases, attempts, waits, transitions, remembered/reported scenes, recurring spans, prospective/future frames, comparison/imagined frames, stable periods, and present telling. Supported unnamed TIME is required when a materially distinct episode exists without explicit temporal wording. Bare deictics, frequency words, durations, or action clauses do not qualify solely because they are temporal.

### PERSON

Inventory the speaker and every distinct represented human/social actor or stable group after coreference, including possessive, offscreen, remembered, reported, prospective, institutional, and relational actors when source-established.

### OBJECT

Inventory independently source-treated concrete and abstract referents, including low-salience scene items, products/documents/parts, amounts, choices, decisions, relationships, recurring situations, mental contents, comparison vehicles, and other reified content. Reject pronoun/deictic duplicates and whole-clause wrappers unless the source itself treats that content/course of action/situation as an identifiable thing.

### LABEL

Inventory source-staged qualities, states, classifications, evaluations, comparisons, corrections, rejections, polarity responses, identities, and statuses at the shortest complete source-native grain. Do not convert every modifier or ordinary action predicate into LABEL.

### VERB

Inventory every materially distinct content-bearing lexical relation/event at the shortest complete source-native grain, including movement, possession, experience, cognition, perception, speech/reporting, intention, waiting, stance, comparison, copular/locative relation, and other represented predicates. Preserve required particles/reflexives/complements/idiomatic material when removing them changes relation identity. Suppress only pure auxiliaries/function grammar and true same-relation duplicates.

### LOCATOR

Inventory materially distinct source-native orientation/context relations for position, containment, path, direction, origin/destination, proximity, accompaniment, recurrence context, internal/embodied orientation, and figurative/comparison orientation. Mere prepositional grammar is insufficient.

## Mandatory cross-class audit

Cross-class overlap is not presumptively suspect.

For each retained source span or supported unnamed frame, ask whether it independently answers more than one reconnectable question:

- what person/referent/content is involved;
- what value/state/classification is staged;
- what lexical relation/action occurs;
- what orientation/path/context is supplied;
- what physical scene/position is represented;
- what episode/period frame organizes it.

If the source gives genuinely different jobs, preserve the projections in each applicable class. If the projections merely reflect alternative grammar for the same job, retain one.
