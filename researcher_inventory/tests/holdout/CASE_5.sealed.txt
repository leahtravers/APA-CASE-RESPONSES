Ugh, I’ve been thinking about this way too much since it happened. Honestly, I don’t know if what Gary said in the meeting was actually “racist,” and I am not even sure if I was “offended” personally. I mean, I knew at the time that some people would think it was and some people would be, but it wasn’t really us in the moment. But it also sort of was?

I feel pretty confident he didn’t mean harm by it. Gary is just older and kind of clueless, but he is not a bad guy. And I expect if I brought it up, he would say that it was not intended that way and I shouldn’t take it that way. And then I would likely have to explain to him that he doesn’t get to tell me how to take it. And (rolls eyes and sighs) you see where I am going with this…

But if I don’t, then who will, right? I’ve turned that over in my mind, and I am not sure how I would do it, if it’s even my responsibility, or if it is worth it. Everybody likes Gary, mostly, and I don’t want to be the girl who called Gary “racist,” you know? Because it is hard enough culturally for me anyway without everyone walking on eggshells.

And reporting it to HR feels like I’d have to do all that work of explaining this, too. And as you can see, I am not very good at it! (Laughs) It’s a really nebulous society thing, and yet it does happen so often! I’m even sort of put out by having to make a decision for myself about what to do here.

It’s like if I say nothing, Gary will just go on being Gary, and HR will go on being HR. And then I have to sit with it and ask myself, “Should I have said something?” And if I do say something, Gary will be Gary, and HR will do some kind of “we’re an inclusive and diverse workplace” (gestures and changes tone while saying this) company stuff that will have absolutely nothing to do with what it was like for me to have to make this decision in the first place.

Sometimes it just feels like no one really gets it.
