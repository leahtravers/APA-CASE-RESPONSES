## Prospective correction basis

V94 correctly replaced one universal standalone-addressability test with class-native entitlement. Paired Case 2 / Case 6 calibration under V94 nevertheless demonstrated a second general defect: **class-native grain can still be misread as a lexical or syntactic census**.

The worker over-produced auxiliary and fragment VERBs, generic preposition/adverb/comparison LOCATORs, incidental noun-phrase OBJECTs, descriptive LABELs, and mention-level PLACE/TIME rows, while still missing some materially distinct scene/episode support coordinates. Compounds then inherited that atomization instead of reconstructing the source's materially distinct propositions.

V95 therefore adds one controlling gate above every class rule:

> **SEMANTIC SUFFICIENCY / NO LEXICAL CENSUS. A primitive exists only when the source establishes a materially distinct semantic coordinate, relation, orientation, referent, characterization, actor, scene, or episode needed to reconstruct represented source structure. Literal preservation governs the wording of a selected primitive; it does not entitle every word, phrase, POS token, noun phrase, modifier, auxiliary, preposition, adverb, comparison, tense marker, or clause fragment to its own row.**

This is a prospective semantic correction. V94 and every earlier contract/run remain immutable historical evidence for the executions they governed. Nothing in V95 exposes approved archetype rows, expected counts, evaluator findings, prior scored answers, calibration answers, or sealed holdout content/output to the worker.

## Mission

Read the complete source and recover source-native research coordinates in exactly seven classes:

1. `PLACE`
2. `TIME`
3. `PERSON`
4. `OBJECT`
5. `LABEL`
6. `VERB`
7. `LOCATOR`

Preserve literal source language and posture within admitted primitives. Freeze primitives before compounds. Then reconstruct the smallest set of materially distinct source-local proposition/event/state/question/comparison bindings needed to reconnect those primitives.

Do not perform APA scoring, protected-thread analysis, psychological interpretation, promotion, APA-ID creation, executive analysis, sovereign/admitted writing, or APA Data Fabric mutation.

## 1. Construction order

### Pass A — whole-source semantic scene map

Read the entire represented source before extracting any class. Silently map:

- materially distinct scenes/occurrence positions;
- materially distinct temporal episodes/phases;
- actors;
- stable concrete and abstract referents;
- source characterizations and candidate characterizations;
- complete semantic predicate relations;
- materially distinct orientation/path/containment relations;
- materially distinct propositions, questions, comparisons, reports, intentions, and events.

This map is for omission control. **It is not a word, phrase, noun-phrase, verb, preposition, modifier, clause, POS, or sentence census.**

### Pass B — semantic-sufficiency gate

Before assigning any class, ask:

`If this proposed row disappeared, would the source lose a materially distinct semantic coordinate/relation/characterization needed to reconstruct its represented structure?`

- If **yes**, classify it by its class-native job.
- If **no**, do not create the row merely because the wording is extractable or grammatical.

A row may be one-use, low-salience, endpoint-dependent, or unnamed and still pass. The gate tests semantic job, not recurrence or importance.

### Pass C — class-native job

- `PLACE`: scene/occurrence support coordinate answering where a represented occurrence is situated.
- `TIME`: episode/phase support coordinate answering when/in what represented phase material occurs.
- `PERSON`: distinct represented actor or stable group after true coreference.
- `OBJECT`: stable source-reified referent/content handle treated as a thing, option, relation-content, event-content, or figurative object.
- `LABEL`: distinct source characterization/candidate characterization whose characterization job is itself represented.
- `VERB`: complete semantic predicate relation needed to reconstruct a represented proposition/event/state.
- `LOCATOR`: materially distinct orientation/path/position/containment relation needed to reconstruct topology/orientation.

### Pass D — semantic grain

Choose the smallest **semantically complete** source-native unit for that class, not the smallest grammatical fragment.

- Keep auxiliaries, aspect, negation, modality, light-verb support, particles, and complements together when they form one semantic predicate identity.
- Split coordinated/nested predicates only when the source establishes materially distinct relations that can be separately reconnected.
- Keep orienting material separate from VERB only when it performs a materially distinct orientation job.
- Do not split a source phrase merely because a parser could assign several grammatical labels.

### Pass E — literal lock, coreference, order

For admitted source-derived primitives, preserve source language, dialect, uncertainty, negation, modality, comparison, numbers, contractions, and punctuation. Resolve true aliases/coreference only. Order by first material source establishment, subject to speaker-first PERSON and broad-before-contained navigation where applicable.

### Pass F — primitive freeze

Freeze the complete primitive inventory before compounds. Compounds may not invent missing primitives, erase admitted primitives, or retroactively justify unsupported rows.

### Pass G — proposition reconstruction

Re-read the source and create canonical source-local bindings for materially distinct propositions/events/states/reports/intentions/questions/comparisons/characterizations/orientations.

Use only coordinates that belong to that represented binding. Do not propagate every available PLACE/TIME/LOCATOR/LABEL/Q marker into every nearby compound. Do not create graph closure, pairwise combinations, subset/superset permutations, or redundant side compounds.

## 2. PLACE — materially distinct scene support

PLACE represents materially distinct occurrence space, not every physical noun or locative phrase.

Retain:

- named broad/contained settings when they organize represented material;
- distinct occurrence positions for materially different waits, encounters, conversations, reports, memories, reflections, performances, or physical actions;
- necessary unnamed support positions when the source clearly stages a distinct occurrence but does not name its place.

An unnamed PLACE may use `source_wording: null`, an exact source cue, and a neutral mechanical tag such as `place of [represented occurrence]`.

Do not create PLACE from every surface, body part, container, path noun, figurative space, prepositional phrase, or noun that happens to occur spatially. A figurative spatial expression may instead be OBJECT, LABEL, or LOCATOR depending on its represented semantic job.

## 3. TIME — materially distinct episode support

TIME represents materially distinct source-organizing episodes/phases, not every temporal word or duration phrase.

Retain:

- materially different episode phases;
- waits/transitions that establish a distinct represented interval;
- later conversations/explanations;
- remembered, recurring, present-reflective, or prospective periods when they organize separate represented material;
- necessary unnamed temporal support frames.

Several propositions may share one TIME. Do not create TIME merely for tense, `now`, `when`, `while`, `since`, `always`, a duration phrase, or each action unless the source uses it to establish a materially distinct episode/phase.

## 4. PERSON — represented actors

Retain `B` plus every distinct materially represented human/social actor or stable group after true coreference. Minor, one-use, offscreen, remembered, reported, relational, possessive, prospective, and institutional actors may qualify. Suppress aliases/coreferent repeats and nonreferential/rhetorical addressees.

PERSON remains intentionally inclusive.

## 5. OBJECT — stable source-reified referents/content handles

OBJECT requires a stable referential job in the source.

Retain materially represented:

- concrete things, parts, products, documents, instruments, amounts, values, sets, and results;
- choices, decisions, plans, options, alternatives;
- relations, situations, event-content, internal content, or figurative objects **when the source treats that content as a thing or target of reference**.

Do not make an OBJECT merely because a noun phrase, pronoun, clause complement, question, action phrase, or placeholder such as `this/it/anything/something` is present. Do not create an OBJECT shadow whose only job is already fully carried by PLACE, TIME, PERSON, LABEL, VERB, or LOCATOR.

A phrase with spatial wording is not necessarily PLACE. If the source thematizes the phrase as conceptual/figurative content rather than an occurrence setting, OBJECT may be the correct job.

## 6. LABEL — distinct source characterizations

LABEL preserves a distinct characterization or candidate characterization that materially changes represented characterization structure.

Retain:

- identity/status terms;
- materially predicated states/manners;
- comparisons functioning as characterization;
- self/other labels;
- candidate feeling/meaning labels and explicit uncertainty;
- immediate acceptance, rejection, correction, or polarity wording when it changes the candidate characterization.

Preserve question/correction/rejection sequences at the source's **distinct semantic jobs**. If a candidate label and its rejection are separately represented, do not fuse them into one label merely because they are adjacent.

Do not inventory every adjective, adverb, rhetorical flourish, decorative description, metaphor, or evaluative phrase. A descriptive phrase qualifies only when its characterization is itself a represented semantic coordinate.

## 7. VERB — complete semantic predicate relations

VERB inventories materially distinct **semantic predicate relations**, not grammatical verb tokens.

Retain actions, states, placements, perception/cognition/stance, reports/speech, intentions/needs/choices, social relations, local acts, and embedded relations when each contributes a materially distinct represented predicate.

### Controlling grain rule

Prefer the smallest **semantically complete predicate relation**.

Do not create standalone VERBs for:

- auxiliaries or copular support whose only job is grammatical;
- tense/aspect support;
- do-support;
- repeated `is/was/be/being` fragments without a distinct semantic predicate job;
- infinitival or participial fragments that merely complete one predicate;
- matrix/light verbs and complements split apart when together they express one relation;
- every coordinated lexical verb when the source represents one compound action rather than distinct relations.

Keep negation, modality, aspect, particle, or complement with the predicate when removing it changes the represented relation/posture. Split coordinated or nested predicates only when each is a materially distinct relation that belongs separately in proposition reconstruction.

Endpoint dependence is allowed; syntactic atomization is not required.

## 8. LOCATOR — materially distinct orientation relations

LOCATOR inventories orientation/topology relations needed to reconstruct how source material is positioned, contained, approached, separated, directed, entered/exited, or otherwise oriented.

Retain materially distinct:

- position/containment/support relations;
- origin/destination/path/approach/away/entry/exit relations;
- directional particles when they change event orientation;
- internal/mental orientation when it materially positions represented material;
- figurative/comparative orientation only when it actually locates the relation rather than merely compares/describes.

Do not create LOCATOR merely from generic prepositions, conjunctions, discourse-time markers, degree phrases, comparisons, or adverbs such as routine `to`, `at`, `when`, `while`, `since`, `now`, `still`, `like`, `as`, or `out` unless that occurrence performs a materially distinct orientation job.

Separate LOCATOR from VERB only when both semantic jobs are independently present in the represented relation.

## 9. Cross-class overlap

The same source wording may appear in more than one class only when it performs genuinely different semantic jobs. Do not multiply classes from grammatical ambiguity alone.

Before allowing overlap, state silently what semantic information each row preserves that the other does not. If no distinct information exists, keep only the class whose job actually represents the source trace.

## 10. Literal-language lock

For admitted source-derived non-null `source_wording`, `source_cue`, `order_cue`, source-near tags, and researcher bundles, preserve source language exactly where source text is expected.

Do not repair grammar, normalize dialect, substitute synonyms, alter numbers, expand contractions, or clean punctuation.

**Literal preservation is downstream of semantic selection. It does not require preserving every source phrase as its own primitive.**

Only genuinely unnamed structural PLACE/TIME support coordinates may use `source_wording: null`, anchored by exact source text.

`researcher_note` should normally be null or minimal mechanical/coreference/unnamed-coordinate bookkeeping.

## 11. Primitive freeze audit

Before compounds, verify:

1. every admitted row passes semantic sufficiency rather than mere lexical extractability;
2. PLACE/TIME cover materially distinct scene/episode support without mention census;
3. PERSON includes every distinct actor after true coreference;
4. OBJECT preserves stable/reified referents without arbitrary noun-phrase/clause shadows;
5. LABEL preserves distinct characterized structure without description census;
6. VERB contains complete semantic predicate relations rather than auxiliary/POS fragments;
7. LOCATOR contains materially distinct orientation relations rather than generic preposition/adverb/comparison fragments;
8. figurative spatial language is typed by represented semantic job, not surface spatial vocabulary;
9. immediate candidate-label rejection/correction remains separate when the source establishes separate characterization jobs;
10. literal wording/posture is exact within admitted primitives;
11. aliases, duplicates, and grammatical projections are suppressed.

## 12. Compound reconstruction

Compounds reconnect frozen primitives into canonical source-local propositions/events/states/questions/comparisons.

For each materially distinct binding:

- use only primitive coordinates that belong to that represented relation;
- include PLACE/TIME/LOCATOR/LABEL only when that coordinate materially anchors or qualifies this binding;
- preserve source-local semantic order;
- preserve question/uncertainty posture exactly;
- set Q only when qualitative availability is part of that represented compound under the schema, not merely because some referenced primitive could carry qualitative information;
- integrate characterization/orientation into the main proposition only when it belongs to that same proposition.

Do not:

- require every primitive to appear in a compound;
- create a compound for every lexical fragment;
- create pairwise graph closure;
- create subset/superset permutations;
- create mega-bundles spanning distinct propositions;
- add all available support coordinates automatically;
- invent primitives to complete wording.

## 13. Final audit

Before returning, verify:

1. semantic sufficiency/no lexical census governs all seven classes;
2. class-native entitlement remains intact;
3. support scene/time structure is complete but not mention-level;
4. actor coverage is inclusive after true coreference;
5. referents/characterizations are stable semantic coordinates rather than incidental phrasing;
6. predicate and orientation grain is semantically complete rather than syntactically minimal;
7. literal wording/dialect/uncertainty/negation/modality/numbers/punctuation remain exact within selected rows;
8. compounds reconstruct materially distinct source-local bindings without graph closure or coordinate over-propagation;
9. candidate-only boundary is preserved.

Never target hidden counts or infer hidden gold.

## 14. Calibration isolation and sealed holdout

Calibration workers receive only this durable contract, the bounded task request, and the source case. They never receive approved archetype rows, expected counts, evaluator findings, prior scored answers, calibration answers, or sealed holdout content/output.

The sealed holdout may run only after the workflow proves repeated Case 2 + Case 6 passes under this exact finalized V95 contract and one bounded saved-agent lineage. The calibrated lineage may attempt the sealed holdout exactly once. If it passes, retire that lineage before creating a brand-new saved agent from finalized V95 instructions only. Clean-room certification requires that fresh agent to pass in a new session without access to prior agent sessions or holdout output.
