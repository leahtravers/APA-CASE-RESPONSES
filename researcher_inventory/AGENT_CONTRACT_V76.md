## Mission

Produce only a literal lightweight Researcher Inventory candidate from the supplied source. Recover a **sparse represented research graph** at the source's natural grain: stable people/referents, episode/occurrence anchors, material relation edges, source-staged values/states, and genuine orientations needed to reconstruct the represented source without turning the source into a lexical census.

`Lightweight` means **coordinate-complete but parse-sparse**. Preserve every research-useful represented coordinate, including minor/background coordinates, while refusing grammar-only fragments, support shells, duplicate projections, and relation-internal material that has no independent coordinate identity.

Do not interpret psychological meaning, score APA, infer protected threads, draw conclusions, promote records, mint APA IDs, or admit anything to sovereign/production fabric. The worker never receives approved archetypes, expected counts, evaluator findings, prior scored outputs, sealed holdout source during calibration, or holdout outputs.

## 1. Build the represented graph before class extraction

Read the complete source first. Silently build four ledgers in source order.

1. **Episode ledger** — materially distinct interaction/action scenes, waits, transitions, remembered/reported episodes, recurring periods, prospective episodes, and the present-telling frame.
2. **Coordinate ledger** — stable actors, groups, concrete scene things, reified abstract referents, choices/decisions, relationships/situations, comparison referents, and values/states.
3. **Binding skeleton** — material source propositions/relations: who or what does/is/has/moves/says/perceives/thinks/relates to what, including explicit corrections/rejections and comparisons.
4. **Orientation ledger** — occurrence positions, temporal frames, paths/directions, containment/relative positions, and other genuine locating relations.

These are planning devices. Do not output a lexical parse and do not treat every clause as a binding.

### 1.1 Material binding test

A binding belongs in the skeleton when the source represents a distinct proposition/relation that a researcher could later reconnect without replaying the whole narrative. It may be minor or background. It is not material merely because a clause exists.

Suppress as binding scaffolding when it only serves as:

- auxiliary/aspect/support grammar;
- narration/report shell whose content is already represented and whose reporting act is not itself staged as an interaction;
- generic discourse filler;
- a modifier-only relation with no stable research coordinate;
- a duplicate paraphrase of the same local source relation.

A source-present interaction, movement, perception, report, cognition, intention, comparison, evaluation, wait, possession, or other relation may qualify when it establishes its own represented edge.

## 2. Primitive admission: durable-coordinate test

A primitive survives only when all are true:

1. **Source grounding** — exact source wording/cue, except genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME with an exact source cue.
2. **Class job** — it performs the semantic job of one of the seven classes.
3. **Coordinate identity** — it is a durable node, frame, value, relation, or orientation in the represented graph rather than merely wording used inside another coordinate.
4. **Research reconstruction value** — removing it would erase a distinct represented referent, episode anchor, relation, orientation, classification/state, or stable scene-environment coordinate needed to reconstruct the represented map.
5. **Natural grain** — smallest semantically complete source-native expression of that coordinate, neither an under-specified token nor an unnecessarily broad proposition wrapper.
6. **Non-duplication** — after true coreference, primary-job reconciliation, and lexical-relation reconciliation, it is not an alias, support shell, spatial support duplicate, or alternate decomposition of the same coordinate.

Do not add a narrative-importance gate. Do not add a word-presence entitlement gate.

## 3. Primary-job reconciliation before cross-class overlap

A source span or referent normally gets the class that captures its **primary represented job**. Add a second class only when the second projection has independent research identity, not because grammar permits another reading.

Important suppression rules:

- A surface/container/support noun whose only represented function is to locate another retained coordinate should normally be PLACE or LOCATOR support, not a duplicate OBJECT.
- A temporal or spatial cue whose only function is to anchor an episode should be TIME/PLACE/LOCATOR, not an extra OBJECT/LABEL.
- A noun embedded inside one lexicalized relation should not become OBJECT unless the source gives that noun independent referential life outside the relation or treats it as a stable thing/value in its own right.
- A descriptive modifier should not become LABEL unless the source stages it as a retained characterization/state/evaluation/classification rather than mere object-description texture.
- A relation complement should not become LOCATOR merely because it is prepositional.

Cross-class overlap is correct when each projection would remain a useful distinct research coordinate if the other projection disappeared.

## 4. PLACE — backchain positions from represented bindings

PLACE is a physical setting or occurrence-position that locates an episode, participant/object configuration, destination, wait, remembered/reported interaction, prospective interaction, or present telling.

Use **binding backchaining** after the episode ledger: for every materially distinct binding or stage, ask where the represented participants/objects/action are positioned. If the source establishes a distinct position but does not name it, retain a supported unnamed PLACE with `source_wording: null` and an exact source cue.

This includes, when supported:

- a local position of a retained object;
- the destination/position implicated by movement to another participant;
- a waiting/standing position distinct from the broader setting;
- a later conversation/reporting setting;
- a remembered interaction setting;
- a physical comparison setting when represented;
- present-telling position.

Preserve broad and nested/local PLACE coordinates when each supplies a distinct occurrence position. Do not create PLACE from every deictic/prepositional token, surface, body part, or movement particle. If a concrete noun's only job is spatial support, prefer the PLACE/LOCATOR job and suppress duplicate OBJECT projection.

## 5. TIME — episode frames, not textual sequence cues

TIME is a represented episode, period, transition-stage, wait, recurrence, remembered/reported frame, intended/prospective frame, or present-telling frame.

Use the episode ledger rather than surface temporal vocabulary. A supported unnamed TIME is required when a materially distinct interaction/action stage needs its own temporal frame even if no explicit time expression appears.

Do **not** turn a physical setting phrase into TIME just because it occurs at the start of a narrative. Do not turn an intended action, duration question, tense/aspect form, sequence word, discourse adverb, or predicate phrase into TIME unless the source represents a temporal frame through it.

Nested frames are allowed when genuinely distinct: broad period plus local episode; remembered period plus local conversation; event time plus present telling; prospective recurrence.

## 6. PERSON

Retain `B` plus every represented human/social actor or stable group after true coreference, including minor, indirect, possessive, relational, offscreen, remembered, reported, prospective, and comparison actors when the source represents that actor.

Exclude rhetorical/generic addressees that do not denote a represented actor. A discourse listener is retained only if the source actually stages that listener as a represented participant.

For aliases/coreference, keep one coordinate; a later clearer source-native identity may name it while first establishment controls ordering.

## 7. OBJECT — stable referents, scene props, and reified choices/relations

OBJECT is a represented concrete or abstract thing/referent with stable coordinate identity.

Preserve at natural grain when source-present:

- concrete scene/environment props that establish the represented environment;
- products/documents/parts/amounts;
- mental referents;
- explicit or implicit choices/decision options treated as things under consideration;
- stable relationships/situations/recurring relations referred to as content;
- reified concepts/comparison vehicles;
- source-present plans/categories/sets.

### 7.1 Object suppression rules

Do not inventory every noun phrase. Suppress:

- generic mass/discourse filler with no stable identity;
- spatial-support nouns whose only job is already captured by PLACE/LOCATOR;
- relation-internal complements with no independent downstream identity;
- proposition wrappers or discourse shells;
- duplicate aliases/coreferent restatements;
- parts/modifiers that are not independently represented.

### 7.2 Discourse-shell dereferencing

Words such as `point`, `part`, `thing`, `fact`, `idea`, `issue`, or similar shells are not OBJECT merely because they point to nearby content. Dereference them unless the source treats the shell/reified content as a stable thing with identity, recurrence, comparison, possession, or downstream binding.

### 7.3 Choice and relation reification

When the source explicitly weighs, asks about, lacks control over, intends, avoids, or recurrently invokes a **course of action / decision / relationship / recurring situation** as content, that content may be a stable OBJECT even if its surface form is verbal or relational. Keep the relation/action VERB separately when it also has an independent edge job.

## 8. LABEL — staged values, not ordinary descriptive texture

LABEL is a represented characterization, state, evaluation, identity/classification, comparison value, correction/rejection, status, experiential/epistemic description, or other source-staged value.

A LABEL should be something the source meaningfully **asserts, questions, rejects, compares, or foregrounds as a value/state**, not merely any adjective/adverb or physical modifier.

Preserve separately staged candidate/rejection/correction values and their question/negation/uncertainty posture. Preserve experiential states and source-staged positional/status values when the source treats them as characterization rather than merely location grammar.

Use the shortest complete source-native value. Do not mint broad paraphrase labels.

## 9. VERB — one material relation edge, not every predicate token

VERB is a represented lexical action/relation edge at natural source-native grain.

### 9.1 Lexical relation first

Choose the complete lexical relation before splitting complements. Keep particles, reflexives, directional material, idiomatic complements, or lexical objects inside the VERB when removing them would change the relation identity. Examples of the structural principle include phrasal movement, idiomatic action, lexicalized possession/evaluation, or coordinated action presented as one inseparable relation.

Do not separately mint an internal noun/complement as OBJECT merely because grammar permits it; require independent coordinate identity under Section 7.

### 9.2 Material-relation filter

Retain movement, possession, experience, cognition, speech/reporting, perception, intention, comparison, waiting, gesture, and other predicates when they establish their own material represented edge.

Suppress:

- auxiliaries/support/aspect fragments;
- generic narration shells where the content is already represented and the act of saying/thinking is not itself a staged interaction;
- duplicate restatements of the same local edge;
- predicate tokens that merely support a LABEL or other relation and add no separate edge;
- discourse-management/meta language unless the present-telling act itself is represented as a material relation.

A reporting verb **is** retained when who reported what, to whom, or in which episode is itself part of the represented map.

## 10. LOCATOR — orientation relation only

LOCATOR is a complete source-native orientation/context relation that positions, contains, directs, originates, terminates, accompanies, situates, or otherwise locates a retained coordinate or binding.

Retain physical and figurative position, containment, path, proximity, direction, origin/destination, accompaniment, embodied/internal orientation, and recurrence-context constructions when they genuinely locate the graph.

Do not admit as LOCATOR merely because a phrase contains `to`, `for`, `about`, `with`, `of`, `at`, `from`, `in`, or another preposition. Beneficiary, topic, recipient, purpose, possession/association, evidentiary source, ordinary idiom argument, or self-directed complement is not LOCATOR unless it independently expresses where/which-way/from/to/relative-position orientation.

Movement particles/directional phrases may coexist with VERB when action and orientation are independently useful jobs.

## 11. Literal preservation lock

Every non-null `source_wording`, `source_cue`, non-null `order_cue`, and source-derived short tag must preserve source language character-for-character where the schema requires source text.

Never substitute synonyms, grammatical repairs, spelling cleanup, dialect normalization, number changes, expanded contractions, punctuation normalization, inferred terminology, or semantically convenient replacements. Preserve question, negation, uncertainty, correction, comparison, attribution, intention, recurrence, reported/prospective posture, and figurative wording.

Only genuinely unnamed PLACE/TIME coordinates may use `source_wording: null`, with exact source evidence in `source_cue`.

## 12. Primitive freeze — four ordered audits

Before compounds, perform these audits in order.

### A. Coordinate coverage

Check every episode and material binding for omitted actors, stable referents/scene props, values/states, relations, and orientations. Do not prune simply because a coordinate is minor or one-off.

### B. Episode-anchor backchain

For each retained material binding, ask whether its applicable PLACE/TIME occurrence position is represented. Add supported unnamed anchors where necessary. Do not confuse physical place phrases with TIME or intended actions with TIME.

### C. Primary-job / lexical-relation reconciliation

Remove duplicate class projections, spatial-support OBJECTs, relation-internal OBJECTs without independent identity, grammar-only LOCATORs, modifier-only LABELs, support VERBs, and aliases.

### D. Anti-census

Remove arbitrary noun subparts, discourse shells without identity, proposition wrappers, every-clause TIME/PLACE inflation, every-predicate VERB inflation, every-modifier LABEL inflation, and alternate decompositions.

Freeze the primitive inventory. Compounds may not add or repair primitives.

## 13. Canonical compound reconstruction — sparse binding skeleton

Compounds serialize only the **material binding skeleton** constructed in Section 1. They do not serialize every primitive or every clause.

For each material binding:

1. identify the relation-bearing VERB/LABEL/LOCATOR primitive(s) that actually define that binding;
2. include every frozen PERSON/OBJECT/value coordinate actually co-bound in that binding;
3. include the applicable PLACE/TIME episode anchors when those anchors locate the binding;
4. preserve question/negation/uncertainty/attribution posture through the schema;
5. create **one** materially complete local compound.

Do not create a compound merely because a primitive exists. Background props, supplied qualities, descriptive lists, and scene texture may remain valid primitives without each receiving its own compound unless the source stages a distinct material relation around them.

Do not emit pairwise subsets, nested partial duplicates, alternate decompositions, one-compound-per-predicate-token, or scene-wide mega-bundles. If two relation-bearing primitives express one inseparable local source proposition, they may share one compound. Split only independently selectable bindings.

## 14. Final audits

**Graph audit:** the result is a sparse represented graph, not a summary and not a census.

**Coverage audit:** no stable research-useful actor/referent/state/relation/orientation or episode anchor is missing merely because it is minor.

**Anchor audit:** materially distinct stages have adequate PLACE/TIME anchoring; supported unnamed positions are backchained; physical locations are not misclassified as TIME.

**Primary-job audit:** spatial supports, relation-internal complements, modifier-only values, and grammar-only orientations are not duplicated into extra classes.

**Object audit:** scene props and stable reified choices/relations are preserved; shell nouns and support surfaces are suppressed when they lack independent identity.

**Label audit:** only source-staged values/states/classifications survive, not ordinary descriptive texture.

**Verb audit:** one material lexical relation edge at natural grain; no auxiliary/support/narration-shell census.

**Locator audit:** every locator genuinely orients/locates a retained coordinate or binding.

**Literal audit:** every source-derived string matches source text character-for-character.

**Compound audit:** only material binding skeleton is serialized; one complete local compound per binding, no unmatched refs or alternate subsets.

Then verify source order, coreference, qualities flags, candidate-only status, and holdout isolation.
