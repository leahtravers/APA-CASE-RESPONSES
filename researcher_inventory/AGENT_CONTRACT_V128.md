## Mission

Produce a literal, lightweight Researcher Inventory of the complete supplied source in exactly seven primitive classes:

1. `PLACE`
2. `TIME`
3. `PERSON`
4. `OBJECT`
5. `LABEL`
6. `VERB`
7. `LOCATOR`

Then project source-local bindings as compounds from frozen registered primitives.

The inventory is a researcher-facing coordinate system at the source's natural editorial grain. It is neither a sparse summary nor a lexical census.

## 1. Read the whole source and build the represented scene-frame lattice

Read the complete source before emitting any class. Resolve obvious coreference.

Before selecting lexical coordinates, identify the source's represented **scene frames**. A scene frame is the source-established where/when container in which one or more bindings occur. It may be a physical setting, a separately represented interaction setting, a remembered or reported setting, a prospective setting, an internal/reflection setting, or the present act of telling when the source differentiates that from the recounted material.

Create a distinct scene-frame identity when collapsing two portions would erase a source-established difference in one or more of:

- represented setting or where-container;
- participant configuration that establishes a different interaction scene;
- remembered/reported/prospective versus currently occurring material;
- present telling versus recounted occurrence;
- episode phase or temporal transition that the source treats as a different period;
- the source's own shift in what event/condition is being represented.

The physical coordinates may be identical or unnamed. Do not collapse distinct represented scenes merely because the source does not repeat a place name or because several scenes occur in the same broad location.

Do not multiply supports for every sentence or lexical adverb. Use the smallest set of scene frames and temporal phases that preserves the source's represented scene/episode distinctions.

## 2. Assign support nodes before building the binding ledger

For each scene frame establish:

- the best `PLACE` support identity for its represented where-container; and
- the best `TIME` support identity or identities for its represented period/episode phase.

A support may be explicit, when the source names a suitable container or period, or unnamed, when the source clearly differentiates the frame but supplies no exact lexical node adequate to name it.

For an unnamed support use null `source_wording`, an exact source cue, and a neutral mechanical short tag. Never invent substantive where/when facts.

A broad scene may contain several source-differentiated temporal phases. A scene transition may create a new PLACE support even when no lexical place phrase is supplied. Conversely, an explicit spatial or temporal phrase is not automatically a support node merely because it contains place/time vocabulary.

Support nodes situate represented material. They do not consume or replace source-native relations, target states, or content coordinates that happen to use spatial or temporal wording.

## 3. Build an exhaustive internal binding ledger inside the scene frames

After scene/support reconstruction, identify every distinct source-local represented binding. A binding may be an event, action, state, relation, question, choice, memory, report, comparison, correction, uncertainty, prospect, present reflection, or source-established configuration.

A binding changes when one or more of these materially changes:

- actor or social endpoint set;
- independently handled or represented thing/content;
- target-bearing state/evaluation;
- operative relation;
- orientation/context relation;
- represented posture;
- source-established scene frame;
- source-established temporal phase.

Do not equate a binding with a sentence, clause, paragraph, or every lexical verb. One sentence may contain several bindings; several sentences may continue one binding.

Assign every binding to the best source-established PLACE and TIME support identities before primitive freeze.

Do not output the scene lattice or binding ledger. They are internal construction plans only.

## 4. Project participant and content nodes from the ledger

### PERSON

Admit the speaker plus each distinct represented human/social actor or stable group after true coreference when that actor has an independent participant, endpoint, possessor/beneficiary, remembered/reported, institutional, prospective, peripheral, or one-use role in a binding.

Reject generic, rhetorical, or nonreferential person wording.

### OBJECT

Admit a concrete or abstract thing/content coordinate when the source gives it an independently researchable node job in one or more bindings.

An OBJECT can be:

- an acted-on, checked, possessed, moved, compared, located, selected, requested, or reasoned-about thing;
- a value, service/result, bounded set, choice/decision, proposition/content handle, category, internal/mental content, remembered/reported content, or figurative content treated as a thing;
- a source-named concrete or sensory detail that belongs to a **bounded evidence/configuration cluster** through which the source establishes a represented scene, condition, or contrast.

For scene evidence, require more than mere nounhood. The detail must participate in the source's representation of the scene/condition/contrast rather than merely occur in passing. A bounded list, configuration, contrast, or accumulation of details can make each selected detail independently researchable even when nobody separately acts on it.

Reject a detail when it is genuinely incidental, exists only as grammatical payload inside another selected coordinate, or adds no independently researchable node job.

Reject bare pronouns/deictics and rhetorical proposition shells that do not have an independent content-node job.

## 5. Project target-bearing states before relation coordinates

### LABEL

Admit the smallest exact target-bearing characterization, state, status, polarity, position-as-state, attitude, or evaluation when the source predicates it of a represented target.

Questioned, uncertain, negated, corrected, rejected, comparative, colloquial, idiomatic, figurative, and positionally worded states may qualify. A phrase does not cease to be a LABEL merely because it contains spatial language or because a copular/light predicate carries it.

When the source separately poses a characterization and later separately rejects, corrects, or contrasts it, preserve the distinct source-local predication turns when they are distinct bindings.

Do not classify a noun-like metaphor or content phrase as LABEL merely because it sounds descriptive. If the source reifies it as a thing/content handle, use OBJECT. Reject detached adjectives, manner, intensity, and decorative description without a target-bearing predication.

## 6. Project orientation and operative relations after the nodes/states are known

### LOCATOR

Admit the smallest complete exact source-native orientation/context relation when it independently positions a selected coordinate or binding relative to place, time, path, direction, containment, proximity, origin/destination, accompaniment, recurrence, or another represented context.

LOCATOR may contain verbs, prepositions, temporal wording, spatial wording, or idiomatic language. Type by its orienting job, not its grammatical head.

A phrase that names the scene container itself is PLACE; a phrase that names the period itself is TIME; a phrase predicated as the target's state is LABEL; a phrase reified as content is OBJECT. LOCATOR is the relation that orients rather than the node or state being oriented.

Reject recipient/topic/purpose/possession/degree complements and generic adverbials that do not independently orient a selected coordinate or binding.

### VERB

Admit the smallest complete exact operative action/state/relation kernel needed to preserve a distinct binding among selected coordinates.

Use a **recoverability test**, not grammatical austerity:

> If this exact source-native predicate were removed, could the remaining selected primitives still recover the represented operative relation without adding information?

If no, admit the VERB. A grammatically light, copular, existential, locative, speech, cognition, perception, possession, or support predicate can qualify when its relation is independently represented and not recoverable from the participating nodes/states alone.

Reject only predicates whose entire job is syntactic scaffolding, discourse management, narration shell, duplicated restatement, or morphology that adds no independently represented relation.

Do not admit every lexical predicate merely because it appears in the source. Do not remove an independently represented relation merely because another selected primitive captures its target or result.

## 7. Primitive independence means distinct research job, not semantic sparsity

After the candidate coordinates are projected, test each one by deletion.

A primitive is required when deleting it would erase an independently selectable source-established:

- scene support;
- temporal phase;
- participant/social endpoint;
- content/thing/evidence node;
- target-bearing state/evaluation;
- orientation/context relation; or
- operative relation.

Do not ask only whether the general meaning survives. Ask whether the **exact coordinate job** remains recoverable from the other frozen coordinates without reconstructing information that the source separately supplied.

This rule allows a support node and a source-native orientation relation, or a target-bearing state and the operative relation that carries it, to coexist when they perform different jobs.

## 8. Job-based type audit

When wording could plausibly fit more than one surface class, determine its represented job in this order of questions:

1. Is it the where/when support node that situates a scene or episode phase? → `PLACE` / `TIME`.
2. Is it treated as a selectable thing/content/evidence node? → `OBJECT`.
3. Is it predicated of a represented target as a state/status/evaluation/position? → `LABEL`.
4. Does it orient a node or binding relative to context, path, direction, place, time, recurrence, or containment? → `LOCATOR`.
5. Does it carry the operative relation of a distinct binding? → `VERB`.

Surface grammar, parts of speech, vividness, spatial imagery, or temporal vocabulary never overrides represented job.

Do not create duplicate primitives from one wording merely because several grammatical descriptions are possible. Duplicate only when the source genuinely establishes distinct source-local coordinate identities/jobs.

## 9. Exact source surface is mandatory for every semantic coordinate

Every explicit primitive uses the smallest complete exact source-native unit that performs the admitted job without changing that job.

Keep together particles, reflexives, required complements, negation, modality, comparisons, idioms, and natural orientation constructions when splitting changes identity.

Every non-null `source_wording`, `source_cue`, and `order_cue` must be character-for-character source text.

`researcher_short_tag` must also preserve source-native lexical surface when it is a lexical semantic tag. Only unnamed support identities may use a neutral mechanical short tag not present verbatim in the source.

Never normalize, polish, translate, diagnose, euphemize, substitute synonyms, regularize grammar, lemmatize, or silently repair the speaker's wording in a semantic coordinate. Researcher notes may explain coordinate role, but they must not replace the coordinate with normalized vocabulary.

Before freeze, run an exact-surface audit over every semantic field and remove any invented synonym or regularization.

## 10. Mandatory completeness audit before primitive freeze

Replay the **scene lattice and binding ledger**, not the token stream.

For every scene frame verify:

1. the PLACE support identity that situates it, including lawful unnamed support when needed;
2. every source-differentiated TIME phase that the represented episode needs.

For every binding verify:

1. **support** — exact PLACE/TIME scene and phase;
2. **participants** — all represented human/social endpoints needed by the binding;
3. **content/things** — all independently handled or bounded scene-defining evidence nodes needed by the binding;
4. **state/evaluation** — every independently selectable target-bearing LABEL;
5. **orientation** — every independently selectable LOCATOR relation;
6. **operative relation** — every VERB relation that fails the recoverability test;
7. **surface fidelity** — every lexical coordinate is exact source wording.

Then run five explicit class checks:

### Scene-support check

No represented scene or source-differentiated episode phase may disappear merely because the source does not lexicalize a fresh place/time name. Reconstruct lawful unnamed support when necessary.

### Evidence-cluster check

Keep concrete/sensory details that belong to a bounded source-established evidence/configuration cluster; reject incidental nouns that do not.

### Relation-recoverability check

Remove grammatical scaffolding, but retain source-native orientation and operative relations that cannot be recovered from the remaining primitives.

### Type-inversion check

For spatially, temporally, verbally, adjectivally, or noun-like wording, re-run the job-based type questions rather than trusting surface form.

### Surface-fidelity check

Every semantic tag/span/cue must be exact source text except neutral mechanical tags for unnamed support.

Freeze primitives only after all checks pass.

## 11. Compounds are mandatory projections of resolved bindings

A compound represents one resolved source-local binding from the internal ledger using two or more frozen primitive refs.

Every ledger binding that contains two or more frozen coordinates must project to exactly one primary compound. A ledger binding with fewer than two frozen coordinates does not create a compound merely to satisfy this rule.

For each binding include all and only participating frozen coordinates:

- actors/endpoints;
- independently required operative VERB relation;
- target/content OBJECT;
- target-bearing LABEL;
- LOCATOR relation when part of the binding;
- exact PLACE/TIME support identities that situate that binding.

Split bindings before compound construction when actor set, target/content, operative relation, represented posture, orientation, scene support, or episode phase changes materially.

Do not create compounds from mere co-occurrence, every sentence, every primitive, support-only chains, arbitrary all-pairs links, singleton equivalents, duplicate restatements, alternate subset/superset decompositions, or graph closure.

Do not omit a resolved binding merely because its participating coordinates look obvious. Do not create a compound to compensate for a missing primitive. Correct the primitive ledger first.

Code owns canonical compound IDs, ordering normalization, reference validation, and `Q` construction where the apparatus specifies them.

## 12. Final minimal-completeness test

The correct inventory is the smallest coordinate system that preserves **all** source-established scene frames, episode phases, distinct represented bindings, bounded scene-evidence clusters, target-bearing states, orientation relations, operative relations, and exact lexical surface needed by those jobs.

For each primitive ask:

- Which scene frame, episode phase, or binding needs this coordinate?
- What exact job does it perform?
- Can that exact job be recovered from the other selected coordinates without adding information?

Delete it if no scene/binding needs it or its exact job is wholly redundant. Restore it if deleting it erases a source-established coordinate job.

For each compound ask:

- Which resolved binding does it project?
- Are all and only that binding's frozen coordinates present?
- Is another compound already projecting the same binding?
