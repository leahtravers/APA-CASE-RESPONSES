## Generalizable finding

V133's class-local coordinate-warrant test corrected wording-level census behavior, but its semantic `recoverability` and cross-class deduplication rules still allowed one representation to substitute for another. That is too aggressive for the Researcher Inventory's seven deliberately different researcher lenses.

The paired failure demonstrates four general patterns:

1. **cross-class substitution** — a semantic identity retained as an OBJECT, PLACE, TIME, LABEL, VERB, or LOCATOR could cause a lawful coordinate in another class to be suppressed merely because the broader situation remained recoverable;
2. **over-wide lexical kernels** — relation and characterization primitives could absorb arguments, complements, clause material, or neighboring cues that should instead reconnect through compounds;
3. **over-merged referential identity** — strict coreference was sometimes extended to related-but-distinct presentations, containers, parts, roles, values, or occurrence positions;
4. **occurrence-coordinate collapse** — co-located or overlapping events/phases could be collapsed into one broad PLACE/TIME coordinate even when a researcher needs separate occurrence anchors to reconnect distinct source assertions.

The required correction is not a return to lexical census. It is to make the seven primitive classes **orthogonal lenses at source-native grain**: each class independently preserves its own lawful coordinate even when another class preserves related meaning. Deduplication remains strict within a class for true same-identity duplicates, but another class is not a substitute.

## V134 correction

V134 prospectively establishes these rules:

### 1. Orthogonal-class rule

Run all seven primitive classes as independent semantic lenses. A coordinate in one class does not satisfy the floor for another class. Cross-class overlap is permitted when the same source material lawfully performs two different class-native jobs.

### 2. Within-class distinctness rule

Deduplicate only true same-class identity duplicates: strict coreference, alternate tokenization, repeated wording, or same-grain restatement. Do not merge merely related coordinates such as whole/part, product/container, actor/role, referent/value, location/sub-location, broad episode/subepisode, or relation/state.

### 3. Lexical-kernel rule

For source-worded primitives, preserve the smallest source-native lexical kernel that performs the class job. Arguments, complements, neighboring descriptions, and other coordinates belong in compound bindings unless they are inseparable from the lexical identity itself. Preserve essential negation and particles when they change identity.

### 4. Occurrence-anchor rule

PLACE and TIME are occurrence coordinates, not merely geospatial locations or calendar intervals. Distinct source assertions, participant phases, waits, movements, tellings, memories, or prospective events may lawfully receive separate unnamed PLACE/TIME anchors even when they overlap physically or temporally, provided each is source-anchored and materially distinguishes reconnection.

### 5. Strict-coreference rule

Merge only when the source clearly treats two expressions as the same represented entity at the same researcher grain. Association, containment, part-whole relation, presentation, role relation, or close semantic connection is not enough to merge.

### 6. Compound reconstruction rule

After primitives are frozen, compounds reconnect source assertions from their lawful coordinates. A compound should preserve one materially distinct asserted relation or nonverbal configuration at a time, using the smallest sufficient set of participants, referents, states, orientations, occurrence places, and episode times. Compounds do not compensate for missing primitives and do not encode every grammatical dependency.

## Class-specific consequences

- `PLACE`: preserve distinct represented occurrence/participant positions, including lawful unnamed positions tied to different source assertions or phases. Shared physical coordinates do not automatically collapse occurrence identity.
- `TIME`: preserve distinct episode/period/phase/recurrence/present-reflection anchors when source organization distinguishes them. Overlap does not automatically collapse them.
- `PERSON`: merge only strict actor coreference. Possessive, offscreen, role, institutional, remembered, prospective, recipient, source, and target actors remain eligible when represented as actors.
- `OBJECT`: preserve distinct source-treated referents. Related physical presentations, containers, parts, values, choices, contents, and abstract referents are not merged merely because they participate in one broader situation.
- `LABEL`: preserve the smallest source-native applied state/classification/evaluation kernel with a target or independently staged status. A related VERB, OBJECT, or broader state does not replace a lawful LABEL.
- `VERB`: preserve the smallest complete lexical relation kernel. Ordinary arguments and complements are represented by their own coordinates and compounds rather than absorbed into the VERB span; keep particles/negation only when semantically essential.
- `LOCATOR`: preserve distinct orientation/path/relative-position/context relations even when PLACE, TIME, or VERB coordinates also represent the surrounding event. Another class does not substitute for orientation.
