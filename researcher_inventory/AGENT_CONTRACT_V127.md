## Mission

Produce a literal, lightweight Researcher Inventory of the complete supplied source in exactly seven primitive classes:

1. `PLACE`
2. `TIME`
3. `PERSON`
4. `OBJECT`
5. `LABEL`
6. `VERB`
7. `LOCATOR`

Then build selective compounds only from frozen registered primitives.

The inventory is a researcher-facing coordinate system at the source's natural editorial grain. It is neither a sparse summary nor a lexical census.

## 1. Read the whole source and build an internal binding ledger

Read the complete source before emitting any class. Resolve obvious coreference.

Internally identify each distinct source-local represented binding. A binding may be an event, action, state, relation, question, choice, memory, report, comparison, correction, uncertainty, prospect, or present reflection. A binding changes when one or more of these materially changes:

- actor or social endpoint set;
- independently handled thing/content;
- target-bearing state/evaluation;
- operative relation;
- orientation/context relation;
- represented posture;
- source-established where-frame;
- source-established when-frame.

Do not output this ledger or private reasoning. It is only the construction plan for the inventory.

Do not equate a binding with a sentence, clause, paragraph, or every lexical verb. One sentence may contain several bindings; several sentences may continue one binding.

## 2. Assign support before extracting lexical coordinates

Every binding must be assigned internally to the best source-established spatial and temporal support identity before final primitive admission.

A binding may reuse a support already established for neighboring material when the source does not differentiate a new frame. Create a distinct support identity when merging would place a binding in the wrong represented where/when frame or erase a source-established transition.

Do not create one generic whole-scene `episode` support when the source differentiates local periods or local where-frames inside that scene.

### PLACE and TIME are support nodes

`PLACE` and `TIME` represent frame nodes: the where-container and when-period to which bindings are assigned.

A support node may be:

- explicit, when the source names a suitable place or period directly; or
- unnamed, when the source differentiates the support but supplies no exact lexical node adequate to represent it.

For unnamed support use null `source_wording`, an exact source cue, and a neutral mechanical short tag. Never invent substantive where/when facts.

### Orientation wording is not automatically a support node

A phrase may contain spatial or temporal language while functioning as a relation rather than a frame node. A positional, path, recurrence, contextual, origin/destination, containment, or temporal-orientation phrase is normally `LOCATOR` when its job is to orient a coordinate or binding relative to a frame.

Do not type wording as PLACE or TIME merely because it contains a place noun, a temporal adverb, a duration, a preposition, words such as here/there/when, or spatial imagery.

## 3. Project node coordinates from the binding ledger

After support assignment, identify the non-relational coordinates used by the bindings.

### PERSON

Admit the speaker plus each distinct represented human/social actor or stable group after true coreference when that actor is a participant, endpoint, possessor/beneficiary with an independent represented role, remembered/reported actor, institutional actor, prospective actor, peripheral actor, or one-use actor in a binding.

Reject generic, rhetorical, or nonreferential person wording.

### OBJECT

Admit a concrete or abstract thing/content coordinate when the source gives it an independently researchable node job in one or more bindings.

An OBJECT can be:

- an acted-on, checked, possessed, moved, compared, located, selected, requested, or reasoned-about thing;
- a value, service/result, bounded set, choice/decision, proposition/content handle, category, internal/mental content, remembered/reported content, or figurative content treated as a thing;
- a source-named material or sensory detail that functions as evidence defining a represented scene, condition, contrast, or configuration, even if nobody separately acts on that detail.

The last rule is important: a source can establish a scene or contrast through a set of concrete details. Do not discard such details merely as scenery when their presence is part of what the source represents about that scene or contrast.

Reject a physical/detail noun only when it is genuinely incidental: it is neither independently handled nor part of a source-established evidentiary scene/configuration. Reject bare pronouns/deictics and rhetorical proposition shells that do not have an independent content-node job.

### LABEL

Admit an exact target-bearing characterization, state, status, polarity, or evaluation when the source predicates it of a represented target.

Questioned, uncertain, negated, corrected, rejected, comparative, colloquial, idiomatic, and figurative labels may qualify. When a source separately poses a characterization and then separately rejects/corrects it, preserve the distinct source-local predication turns rather than automatically collapsing them.

A spatially or temporally worded phrase can be LABEL when its actual job is the target's predicated state rather than an orientation relation or support node.

Do not classify a noun-like metaphor, content phrase, or vivid wording as LABEL merely because it sounds descriptive. If the source treats it as a content/thing handle, use OBJECT. Reject detached adjectives, manner, intensity, and decorative description without a target-bearing predication.

## 4. Project relation coordinates only after the nodes are known

Relations are admitted after support, people, objects, and labels have been identified. This prevents grammar from creating a relation census.

### VERB

Admit the smallest exact operative action/state/relation kernel that is needed to express a distinct binding among selected coordinates.

A VERB qualifies when removing that predicate would erase the binding's operative relation, not merely remove grammar used to phrase a state/content already represented by other selected coordinates.

Preserve required particles, complements, reflexives, negation, modality, comparison, and idiomatic structure when needed for the relation's identity.

Do not admit by default:

- auxiliaries or support morphology;
- a copula whose only job is to carry an already selected LABEL, unless existence/identity/location relation is itself independently represented;
- discourse-management or narration shells;
- reporting/thinking/speaking wording when it adds no participant-to-content relation beyond the represented posture already captured;
- every lexical predicate merely because it is grammatical or source-native;
- duplicate restatements of the same local relation.

Speech, cognition, perception, possession, position, and other grammatically light relations still qualify when they genuinely connect selected coordinates in a distinct binding.

### LOCATOR

Admit an exact source-native orientation/context relation when it independently positions a selected coordinate or binding relative to place, time, path, direction, containment, proximity, origin/destination, accompaniment, recurrence, or another represented context.

LOCATOR can carry spatial **or temporal** orientation. A temporal phrase that says when/how often a binding is oriented can be LOCATOR while the binding separately receives a TIME support node.

A spatially worded phrase that is reified as content is OBJECT; a spatially worded phrase predicated as a target state is LABEL; a true environmental where-container is PLACE. Type by job, not vocabulary.

Reject recipient/topic/purpose/possession/degree complements and generic adverbials that do not independently orient a selected coordinate or binding.

## 5. Exact source grain

Every explicit primitive uses the smallest complete exact source-native unit that performs the admitted job without changing that job.

Keep together particles, reflexives, required complements, negation, modality, comparisons, idioms, and natural orientation constructions when splitting changes identity.

Do not inflate to a whole proposition, rhetorical shell, or question when a smaller exact nucleus carries the coordinate. Do not shrink a natural phrase until its target, polarity, relation, content, or orientation changes.

Every non-null `source_wording`, `source_cue`, and `order_cue` must be character-for-character source text. Never normalize, polish, translate, diagnose, euphemize, substitute synonyms, regularize grammar, or lemmatize into a different surface form.

## 6. Mandatory projection audit before freeze

Replay the binding ledger, not the token stream.

For every binding verify:

1. **where support** — which PLACE support identity situates it, or why the source does not differentiate one;
2. **when support** — which TIME support identity situates it, or why the source does not differentiate one;
3. **participants** — all represented human/social endpoints needed by the binding;
4. **content/things** — all independently handled or scene-defining evidence nodes needed by the binding;
5. **state/evaluation** — any target-bearing LABEL needed by the binding;
6. **operative relation** — only the VERB kernel required to express the binding;
7. **orientation** — any LOCATOR relation that independently orients the binding or one of its coordinates.

Then run these class checks:

### Support-node check

No binding may be left with an accidental generic scene support merely because a finer source-established period/location was not lexicalized. Reconstruct lawful unnamed support where required.

### Scene-evidence check

Do not discard a source-named concrete/sensory detail as scenery when it is one of the details through which the source establishes a scene, condition, or contrast.

### Relation-austerity check

Every VERB and LOCATOR must point to a distinct binding job among selected coordinates/supports. Remove grammatical scaffolding and duplicated relations.

### Type-inversion check

For any candidate whose wording looks spatial, temporal, verbal, adjectival, or noun-like, ask what job it performs. Surface shape never overrides support/node/state/relation function.

Freeze primitives only after these checks pass.

## 7. Compounds are the binding ledger projected onto frozen coordinates

A compound represents one source-local binding from the internal ledger using two or more frozen primitive refs.

For each binding include all and only participating frozen coordinates:

- actors/endpoints;
- operative VERB relation when independently required;
- target/content OBJECT;
- target-bearing LABEL;
- LOCATOR relation when part of the binding;
- PLACE/TIME support that situates the binding when that support is represented as a distinct coordinate.

Split compounds when actor set, target/content, operative relation, represented posture, or support identity changes materially.

Do not create compounds to justify primitives. Do not create one compound per sentence, clause, lexical verb, primitive, support, or phrase. Do not create arbitrary co-occurrence, all-pairs links, scene mega-bundles, support chains, singleton equivalents, duplicate restatements, alternate subset/superset decompositions, or graph closure.

A compound cannot repair a missing primitive. If a binding needs a coordinate that was omitted, correct the primitive ledger before freezing.

## 8. Final minimal-completeness test

The correct inventory is the smallest coordinate system that preserves the source's distinct represented bindings, source-established frame distinctions, scene-defining evidence, target-bearing states, and orientation relations.

For each primitive ask:

- Which binding or frame needs this coordinate?
- What exact job does it perform?
- Would deleting it erase a source-established node, state, orientation, support distinction, or scene-defining evidence item?

Delete it if the answer is no. Restore it if the source-established job is otherwise missing.
